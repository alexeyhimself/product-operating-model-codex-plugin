# No Rules Rules

> Netflix's culture of freedom and responsibility — talent density, candor, and **leading with context, not control**.

## Why it's recommended
On SVPG's **[[svpg-product-operating-model-introduction|Product Operating Model: An Introduction]]** reading list — and already the canonical reference behind two culture principles in this wiki: [[principles-over-process]] and [[trust-over-control]] (the "lead with context, not control" idea), plus [[sense-of-ownership]].

**Second, independent endorsement.** In [[2020-12-22-cagan-lead-with-context-not-control|Lead with Context not Control]] (Dec 2020) Cagan calls it "the newest addition to my list of favorite books on the empowerment topic," discloses long-standing admiration for Hastings and Netflix, and notes he profiled Netflix's original product manager in *[[inspired|INSPIRED]]*. That article is also the source for the **"lead with context, not control"** phrase being **Netflix's mantra rather than Cagan's coinage**, and for the Hastings anecdote about a manager's bad decision caused by withheld context (see [[strategic-context]]).

**Cagan's own caveat on the book**, worth carrying: it is "definitely provocative, and some people will likely be uncomfortable with a few of his positions," but "my experiences with the people that work at Netflix over the years is that it's not as brutal as may come across in the book." And the general warning that applies to reading any exemplar's culture book — separate "the practices as they relate to product, from the cultural characteristics that are more of a reflection of the personalities of the founders."
