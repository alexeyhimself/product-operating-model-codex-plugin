# Thomas Fredell

> Chief Technology and Product Officer who led the technology + product side of the 50-year-old Merrill Corporation's transformation into Datasite. The practitioner voice on wiping out and rebuilding a management layer, replacing an Oracle monolith with a Spotify-inspired squad organization + microservices platform, and moving from quarterly releases to 20 deploys/day. Brought [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] into Datasite from their Interlinks days.

## Contributions & positions
- **The "wipe out the management layer" call** as a courageous transformation-leadership move. Fredell arrived to find product had been carved out of IT with a quarterly-release cadence, no product culture, and no people who had done product before. Rather than coach up the existing management layer (which he lacked the time to do), he wiped it out and started from scratch — bringing in [[christian-idiodi|Idiodi]] and Jeremiah Ivan from Interlinks. See [[datasite-transformation]].
- **Modular org + modular platform** as the parallel-tracks of transformation — Spotify-inspired squads on the org side; microservices architecture on Pivotal Cloud Foundry (with MongoDB replacing Oracle) on the tech side. *"If the platform and the people can both be modular in a way that mimics each other, plus a focus on the customer, you tend to win."* See [[team-topology]].
- **The tech-company-valuation pitch** as the board-level case for transformation investment. Fredell's clearest single articulation: *"a technology-based business has a much higher valuation than an equivalent revenue service business — so if we look at the portfolio of businesses Merrill had, there is a software-service play here that can create tremendous value."*
- **The M&A life-cycle vision** — the vision that emerged from collaborative work at Datasite: not just due-diligence software, but the *whole* M&A life cycle (pre-diligence prep → diligence → integration → operations → next deal). See [[datasite-transformation]] and [[product-vision]] discussions.
- **The Workiva catalyst** — Fredell surfaced (on Ep 24) that CEO Rusty Wiley had watched [[marty-cagan|Cagan]]-coached Workiva disrupt Merrill's financial-print business with collaborative-across-time-and-space technology; that was the demonstration that convinced Wiley the model would work.
- **Communication that resonates with the listener's frame, not yours** — Fredell's contractor analogy for stakeholder communication (good contractor = clear on what will happen, does what they said, circles back on issues; bad contractor = surprises everyone). Applies at all levels but especially product/tech ↔ finance and sales.
