# Elias Lieberich

Product coach at **[[product-matters|Product Matters]]**, based in Europe. Long-time YouTube-ads / Google product background. External coach — not an SVPG partner. Works with fast-scaling and global-enterprise product organizations.

Lieberich is treated in this wiki as a **wiki-primary voice on organizational focus** — how to build the focus muscle inside a team when leadership hasn't given clarity, and how to coach a leader through the alignment friction that follows. His teachings complement [[marty-cagan|Cagan]]'s Ep-29 statement that *"you don't have a prioritization problem, you have a strategy problem"* — Lieberich is where the *how* of building focus lives.

## Core teachings surfaced in the wiki

- **Focus as a human problem** (not just a strategy problem). Everyone comes from the right place — CEO FOMO, sales urgency, team ideas — and we simply can't say no.
- **The litmus test for focus:** the team can navigate day-to-day trade-offs. If a new escalation arrives and they can weigh it against what they're doing, focus is real.
- **The written narrative as fact-finding exercise** (before the artifact). Answer *what are we building? for whom? what's the value? what's our current solution? what data? what unknowns? what risks?* — the goal is diagnostic, not production. The parts that were hard to write tell you where the gaps are.
- **The scale multiplier** — uncertainty in your head becomes ambiguity times N in the org. *"If you don't have a clear answer in your mind, you have no business turning around to a big organization and telling them."*
- **Focus → alignment → immediate feedback loop** as the coaching sequence.
- **The Product FAQ** as connective tissue — for elephant-in-the-room questions with no clean answer, park them in a Product FAQ with *"as of Q1 2026, this is the best answer we have"* language. Acknowledges the concern without pretending to resolve it.
- **Concentric-circles method** for building alignment — start with one person answering the fundamental questions, then their trusted counterpart, then leadership, then the team.
- **Magic coaching questions:**
  - To a leader in the noise: **"Who is this for?"**
  - To a CEO: **"When was the last time you had a hit that was intentional?"**
- **Stop-doing prescription:** *back-to-back meetings all day* — *"when did you have time to think?"*
