# Shreyas Doshi

Product leader, coach, and writer. Led teams at **Stripe, Twitter, Google, and Yahoo**. Advises founders and product executives across the industry through his advisory practice and teaches through his **Product Sense** and **Product Strategy for Founders & Executives** courses on Maven. Known for writing that separates *what actually works in practice* from *what sounds good in theory*.

Doshi is treated in this wiki as a **wiki-primary voice on product leadership** — the archetypes framework, the *product problems are leadership problems* thesis, and the *packaged intuition* framing of frameworks are all attributed to him.

## Core teachings surfaced in the wiki

- **All problems are people problems; people problems are leadership problems.** Leadership has three buckets: **context, culture, coaching.** → grounded on [[product-leadership]] via [[2025-08-21-idiodi-doshi-coaching-product-leadership-part-1|Product Therapy Ep 26]].
- **Signing up for outcomes vs feeling responsible for outcomes.** *Signing up* is situational; *feeling responsible* is permissionless. Most leaders conflate the two.
- **The three archetypes / three hats:** [[product-leadership#the-three-archetypes|Operator, Craftsperson, Visionary]] — each with a preferred hat (superpower + where they like to spend time). Superpowers: Operator → communication/influence; Craftsperson → product insight; Visionary → seeing what others cannot.
- **Product-stage-fit for leaders.** A leader who's excellent at *mature-stage* products often stalls in a *growth-stage* startup, because their instinct is to set up all the "right" systems (OKRs, review meetings, cross-functional staffing) while quarters pass with no progress. In a startup a few quarters is like five years in a larger company.
- **The MSN list** for hiring. 3 bullets each under Must / Should / Nice to have. Every bullet concretely evaluatable; no table stakes; assign specific interviewers to specific bullets. → grounded on [[hiring-and-onboarding]] via Ep 26.
- **The 11-out-of-10 question.** *"9–12 months in, what is the one thing this person should have absolutely done a spectacular job on?"* Communicate that one thing to candidates at the right stage of the interview.
- **The founder-still-owns-what question.** Before hiring, get clear on what aspects of the product the founder will continue to own, consult on, or expect to be consulted on. This is the friction point that ends most leadership tenures early.
- **Listening as art, not skill.** *"People can tell if you're not truly listening — even if they can't put it in words."* First weeks: listen with your full body.
- **Judgment + courage — often it's not lack of courage, but lack of the right words.**
- **The 2 min / 20 min / 2 hr / 2 days / 2 weeks rule** for accurately diagnosing your own leadership/communication problems. *"To save 2 days you're wasting months. To save 2 weeks, you're wasting years."*
- **Good frameworks are packaged intuition of world-class practitioners.** Their inverse — [[product-management-theater#framework-theater|framework theater]] — is *"an octagonal framework with eight colors with a backronym launched on LinkedIn — 5,000 likes; the problem is it doesn't work."*
- **AI is surprisingly good at empathy and politics.** Current LLMs show more empathy than the median VP/director at a tier-one Valley company.
- **Taste is being able to explain *why* something is good** (citing Steve Jobs). *"We've all worked with a leader with great taste who says 'I don't like it' with no explanation — we're just left confused."*
