# Datasite

> Formerly **Merrill Corporation** (founded 1968 in Minneapolis by Roger Merrill and his wife). **Rebranded to Datasite in 2020** after divesting the legacy financial-printing business to focus on the SaaS platform for the M&A community. Included in the wiki because Merrill → Datasite is *TRANSFORMED*'s clearest transformation of an *old, sales-driven, non-tech legacy company* — the exact profile most people believe cannot transform. Full narrative in [[datasite-transformation]].

## Profile
- Old-line financial services (financial printing, document management, virtual data rooms) → modern SaaS platform for the M&A community.
- Transformation began under CEO **Rusty Wiley** with [[thomas-fredell|Thomas Fredell]] as Chief Technology and Product Officer, [[doug-cullen|Doug Cullen]] as Head of Sales, [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] as head of product (brought in by Fredell from Interlinks, before joining [[svpg|SVPG]]), and **Jeremiah Ivan** as senior engineering lead (also from Interlinks).
- Originally 11 operating divisions (Merrill Corporation). CEO Rusty Wiley divested translations, legal-tech services, court reporting, and corporate chachki businesses to focus on the "crown jewel" — the Datasite M&A due-diligence platform. Rebranded Merrill → Datasite in 2020.
- Technology transformation: Oracle monolith → **microservices on Pivotal Cloud Foundry**; MongoDB replacing Oracle; quarterly-max release cadence → **20 deploys per day** by the time Fredell left, all seamless with no customer impact.
- Vision that emerged from collaborative work: **the M&A life cycle solution** — not just due-diligence software, but the whole M&A life cycle (pre-diligence prep → diligence → integration → operations → next deal).
- Marketing catalyst: [[workiva]]-style disruption. Rusty Wiley had watched Cagan-coached Workiva disrupt Merrill's financial-print business with collaborative-across-time-and-space technology; that was the demonstration that convinced him the model would work.
- **4,000+ employees, 23 countries** at intervention — grown by acquisition without unifying strategy.
- 2019 revenue +30%+; 10,000+ M&A deals annually today.

## Why it's here
Exemplar. Cagan notes: *"another very common misconception in the product community is that innovation only happens in consumer products, but that it's a different game when building products for large companies. It is unfortunately true that many enterprise software companies have been slow to move to the product model, and it's also true that sales-driven organizations can be difficult to change."* Datasite is the counter — including the emblematic **sales-owns-everything** diagnosis and the patented redaction innovation ([[transformed|TRANSFORMED]] Ch 35).
