# Workiva

> **Exemplar — the counter to "you'd have to be Google or Apple."** A cloud company serving finance and accounting staff at large public companies, used by Cagan in [[2017-09-25-cagan-customer-inspired-technology-enabled|*Customer Inspired; Technology Enabled*]] precisely *because* nobody has heard of it: the argument being that technology-enabled innovation is not the preserve of famous consumer brands. Its product is **WDesk**.

## Why Cagan uses it
He introduces the other four examples in that article — Amazon Alexa, Disney MagicBand, Google Translate, the iPhone — and then pre-empts the obvious objection: *"you might be thinking you need to be a Google, Amazon, Apple or Disney to innovate like this, but that's not at all the case. I just picked those product teams because you know them."*

**The customer.** Finance and accounting people at nearly every large public company — who *"consistently work some of the longest hours in the company and it's largely a thankless and unrecognized effort because the rest of the company views this as overhead."* The founders met them, saw visible pain, and judged they could dramatically improve those lives.

**The differentiator — not listening harder.** There were already several enterprise software providers in the market, and Cagan is blunt about what they were doing: *"most enterprise software companies are terrible examples of best practices, and they were literally building what their customers were asking for."* Workiva instead **reimagined the solution**, making early bets on two enabling technologies: **cloud infrastructure**, and **a rich, modular front-end system for very interactive, office-like apps entirely on the web**.

**The second-order argument — the part worth keeping.** The technology bet was *also* a talent strategy, and the two compound: the team *"believed that leveraging state of the art technologies would help them attract and retain top engineers, which would then allow them to continue to provide disruptive and truly innovative solutions."* That is a causal loop, not two separate benefits — useful with CTOs who treat platform modernity and hiring as unrelated budget lines. See [[empowered-engineers]], [[missionaries-vs-mercenaries]].

**The outcome.** NPS *"higher even than Apple – which is unheard of in the enterprise software world"*, plus growth from early startup to public company in a few years.

## Also appears as
- The **Cagan-coached precedent inside [[datasite|Datasite]]'s transformation story**: Workiva's collaborative-across-time-and-space technology disrupted Merrill Corporation's financial-print business, and that demonstration is what convinced Datasite CEO Rusty Wiley the product model would work. Note the two roles are consistent — the same technology bet that produced the NPS result is what made Workiva the disruptor in Merrill's market.

## Coaching use
The go-to counter-example when a leader in unglamorous B2B or internal-tooling software says the SVPG material "only applies to consumer companies." Workiva is enterprise, serves a back-office user the rest of the business calls overhead, and competed against incumbents who were doing exactly what their customers asked — and the differentiator was an engineering-side technology bet, not better requirements-gathering.
