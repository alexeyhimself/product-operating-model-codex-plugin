# Product Myths

> Nine common misconceptions the wiki keeps hearing from product organizations, named crisply by [[chris-jones|Chris Jones]] across [[2025-04-03-idiodi-jones-coaching-product-myths-part-1|Product Therapy Ep 17]] and [[2025-06-26-idiodi-jones-coaching-product-myths-part-2|Ep 23]], with the SVPG-canon correction for each. A coaching artifact for confronting the misconceptions directly rather than fragmenting the corrections across ten concept pages.

## The argument
Most product-transformation failures are not the *absence* of the product model but the *misinterpretation* of it. Companies adopt the vocabulary (product manager, product discovery, empowered team, roadmap alternatives) without adopting the underlying mechanics — and the misinterpretations are consistent enough that Jones can enumerate them from years of coaching. The nine myths below are the wiki's canonical enumeration; each has a distinct correction that agents should apply verbatim when they see the myth in a coaching conversation.

**Voice discipline:** each *myth* is Jones-restated from the two podcasts; each *correction* is SVPG-canon that the wiki grounds elsewhere in its own concept/framework pages. This synthesis is a coaching index, not the primary teaching.

## The nine myths

### Myth 1 — "Product decides the *what*; engineering decides the *how*."
**Correction:** the best ideas come from engineers who *know what's possible right now* — the "what" is a shared team output. RACI/DACI matrices actively harm this; they force everyone to retreat to their corners. Jones's own admission: *"nearly all the best ideas came from engineers on the team — not from me, not from my bosses, not from sales, not from customers."* See [[empowered-engineers]], [[feature-teams-vs-product-teams]], [[cross-functional-partnering]].

### Myth 2 — "Design is downstream / just makes things pretty."
**Correction:** design is a **discovery** role. A good designer produces **~two dozen prototypes per week**, most thrown away; the ~10% of design work that is delivery-asset production is spiky. Cagan's line: *"giving a designer wireframes is like giving an engineer pseudocode."* Idiodi's: *"in the best companies I often cannot even differentiate between a product manager and a designer."* Steve Jobs's ur-quote: *"design is not what it looks like — design is how it works."* See [[product-designer]], [[prototypes]].

### Myth 3 — "Product managers should be hired for domain expertise."
**Correction:** **domain expertise = domain knowledge − domain dogma** (Shreyas Doshi, cited by Jones). Adding domain to a strong PM (~3 months) is easier than stripping dogma from a domain expert (~near-impossible). Not naivety — the PM must aggressively acquire domain knowledge on the job — but the starting point matters. Idiodi's rule: *"a good PM takes ~3 years to develop; domain knowledge takes ~3 months if you focus — pick the deeper craft."* See [[product-manager]], [[hiring-and-onboarding]].

### Myth 4 — "Our job is to give the customer what they ask for."
**Correction:** customers don't know what they want because they don't know what's *possible* with technology. Bezos in both directions: *"the biggest mistake you can make is listening to your customers … the second biggest mistake is not listening to your customers."* The Walkman focus-group anecdote (kids picked bright colors verbally, all took the black one home) illustrates the say/do gap. Managing feature requests / **"specials"**: high escalation bar, quota'd, done knowingly not by default, or the product becomes a Frankenstein. See [[product-manager]], [[insights]].

### Myth 5 — "Product discovery will give us the answer."
**Correction:** discovery is **not a formula, recipe, or process — it's a set of tools that inform your judgment.** People love this misconception because it lets them *replace* judgment with process ("I followed discovery, product didn't work, must be the process"). Right-size discovery based on actual risks and confidence needed. Idiodi's illustration: PM asks *"how do I do discovery?"* — actual issue = the site is down. Direct manager's job = spot when a PM is **grinding metal** (weeks of tweaking a prototype) and call it. See [[product-discovery]], [[product-discovery-techniques]].

### Myth 6 — "We need to iterate on a solution until it succeeds."
**Correction:** some grit is valuable; runaway sunk-cost pushing a rock uphill is not. Product teams don't recognize they're running against a **clock** — the moment the business gives you a problem, there's a race for the value of the team. Idiodi's diagnostic: *"when a company says 'we have an innovation team,' that literally means my product team is not innovating."* Same for acquisitions: *"I've not met a single CEO who tells me 'if we thought we could build this better and quicker ourselves, we wouldn't have bought this company.'"* See [[the-need-for-speed]], [[time-to-money]].

### Myth 7 — "Product managers need product owners."
**Correction:** the PO/BA role is a **historical artifact of the waterfall→agile transition** — companies changed the title but not how they worked. **Two people doing one job = handoffs = command-and-control by another name.** Jones's reframe: **inside game vs outside game.** Inside game = engineers, stories, technical decomposition. Outside game = customers, markets, discovery, stakeholders. Both must live *in a single skull.* **The PO role has no career path in a serious product company.** Idiodi's cost argument: 10-person team = ~$1.5–2M/year in salaries = ~$55–65k/sprint — that is not a decision to split across two roles. See [[product-manager]], [[delivery-manager]], [[feature-teams-vs-product-teams]].

### Myth 8 — "If you build it, they will come."
**Correction:** product teams are responsible for the *whole* solution, not just the product's functionality. **Go-to-market is part of the solution.** How do customers learn the product exists? How do they get it? What entices them to buy? These may involve sales, marketing, or the product itself (product-led growth). A PLG product and a direct-sales product are *dramatically different products*. Applies to internal products too. Jones's admission: *"I was an idiot on this when I started."* See [[product-manager]], [[product-marketing]], [[leading-product-marketing]].

### Myth 9 — "The product manager is the CEO of their product."
**Correction:** the *idea* is right; the *power* interpretation is wrong. Idiodi's flip: *"the PM is the opposite of Spider-Man — with great responsibility comes no power."* Right reading: holistic understanding of the company, deep sales/marketing/stakeholder relationships, outcome-oriented mindset. Wrong reading: license to bark orders, control the team, be the boss. PM's role is elevated because of value responsibility, not authority. See [[product-manager]], [[empowered-product-teams]].

## Counterpoints
Two edges worth naming:

**On Myth 3 (domain expertise) — the domain-first case still holds in some very narrow environments.** In deeply regulated pharma, medical devices, aerospace safety, or defense, the *cost* of not-knowing domain from day one is unusually high — a PM ramp of 3 months may not be safe. In those cases, hire a domain expert *and* invest heavily in coaching the domain-dogma out of them, or pair them with a strong non-domain PM early. Jones's own crediting: he *is* saying the PM must aggressively acquire the domain — the myth is about *hiring* on it as the primary filter.

**On Myth 5 (discovery is not a formula) — some formulaic constraints are still useful.** Definition-of-done, right-sizing checklists, and prototype-testing conventions are all discovery scaffolding that stops teams from making it up every time. The myth Jones is naming is not *"structure is bad"* — it's *"structure as substitute for judgment is bad."*

## Where this stands
The nine myths are the wiki's canonical enumeration as of Ep 23. Future Product Therapy episodes revisiting these topics belong in the same enumerator's tree — either enrich the correction under the same myth number, or add Myth 10+ if Jones (or another partner) names a genuinely new misconception with a distinct correction.

The **coaching move** when using this synthesis in a live conversation:
1. Name which myth you're hearing (the myth as *literally* stated by the coachee).
2. Point to the specific correction on the concept/framework page linked above — do not summarize it inline.
3. If the coachee is skeptical, cite the Jones podcast episode directly — this is not opinion; it's an SVPG partner's crisp statement of the misconception + correction from years of coaching.
