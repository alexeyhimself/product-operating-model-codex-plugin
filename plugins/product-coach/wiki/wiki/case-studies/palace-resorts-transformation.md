# Palace Resorts Transformation

The **Palace Resorts (Palace Company) transformation** — a two-year (2023–2025) shift from a project-model software factory to the product operating model at a Mexico-based family-run hospitality group with **15,000 employees, 6,000+ rooms across 18+ properties in 7 countries.**

Primary source: [[2025-09-18-idiodi-chapur-bufrem-coaching-transformation-palace|Product Therapy Ep 28]] with [[anuar-chapur|Anuar Chapur]] (CPTO, Palace Company) and [[gabrielle-bufrem|Gabrielle Bufrem]] (product coach).

The wiki's first hospitality / non-US / family-run transformation exemplar. Complements [[datasite-transformation]] (SaaS, US, PE-backed) and [[trainline-transformation]] (established European B2C tech).

## Starting point

- Business was **doing well post-COVID** — record years. But the technology work was not the reason.
- ~150-person product/tech org that was effectively a **software factory**: intake → prioritize → project-fund → design prototype → convert to story points → build → deploy → team disperses → no maintenance.
- All the agile ceremonies wrapped around fundamentally waterfall work — [[transformation-anti-patterns#product-management-theater-installation-not-adoption|installation-not-adoption]] theater.
- No real product managers — *"product owners"* who were translators from business to IT, prioritizing features in a list.
- Discovery was absent as a practice.

## Trigger

Idiodi's framing on this case is important — Palace is a *"doing-well business"* transformation, not a *"burning platform"* transformation. Chapur, a family member with no tech background, took over technology, attended [[transformed|TRANSFORMED]] workshop in NYC, and realized the technology work was not producing outcomes for the business it should be able to serve. *"Everything is broken"* was not the trigger — earning the space to make the change was.

Cagan introduced Chapur to [[gabrielle-bufrem|Gabrielle Bufrem]] at the workshop as a Spanish-speaking coach who could work with his team.

## The commitment test

Bufrem's first question to Chapur: *"What's plan B here?"* Chapur, straight in the eye: *"There's no plan B. This is it."* This became her canonical commitment-signal test with every transformation sponsor.

## Trust before assessment

Chapur to Bufrem: *"If this is going to work, you have to come here. People are going to have to see you. You are not a screen on Zoom. You are a person and they need to trust you."* → Two days in Mérida answering every question from Chapur's brothers and the team, before assessing anything.

## Bufrem's assessment framework — three areas

- **How you build.**
- **How you solve problems.**
- **How you decide which problems to solve.**

Verdict for Palace: build wasn't the best but not the worst — not the highest-ROI investment. *"We don't have trust yet to be telling people we're going to change which problems we solve — we have not earned that right."* → **Start with how you solve problems.**

## The pilot team

Bufrem's canonical [[pilot-teams|pilot-team]] treatment traces here. Best PM (or would-be-PM), best designer, best engineers. Customer-facing area big enough to be visible, not so business-critical that a stumble breaks the business. *"When we started, our clock started ticking. We need to deliver results like yesterday."* Weekly 1.5-hour coaching by Bufrem.

**First meeting: what it is to be a team, what our roles are, who we are as people.** Before: design was agency-model, PM was list-taker, engineers were barely there.

## The App Team saga — the multi-million-dollar mistake avoided

Backstory: a survey years earlier said 40% of members would like to book via an app. The team was literally called *"the app team."* After real discovery — going to the hotels, talking to guests, understanding how they actually book — **the team concluded the app was not the solution.** They pivoted to the existing web page (which had been shipped as done-in-Jira months earlier and stopped being maintained). Result: **booking conversion 1% → 5%+ over ~18 months.** A multi-million-dollar app-build avoided.

## From features to problems

Bufrem's coaching frame: *"Customers are not going to tell you what to build, and your teams are not going to tell you what to build, because they don't know. This is your job — customers know their problems, the business knows its problems. It's on us to almost be a detective and figure out the clues."*

## Data as trust builder

Palace hired a head of data. Whenever a company myth arose (*"no one wants X" / "we need an app"*), the data team could produce a fact that trumped the myth. *"Data was not only crucial for the product teams — it was a very big lever for us to gain trust with the company."* Moved from opinion-fueled to data-driven decisions. Under the old model, *only sales projects got funded* because they were the only ones you could put a plausible ROI number on; hotel-operations, guest-experience, and conversion problems were invisible to funding.

## Team topology moves

Verticals: sales operations, vacation club, HR, hotel operations, etc. — divided so each team owns a real business area rather than a project. Recruiting shifted to bring in people who *wanted* to work this way. Structure: **one PM + one tech lead + one designer per team** ideally (or one designer per two teams). Leadership overhaul was needed — *"the majority of the team was able to adapt to the new way, but the leadership team we had was not."*

## The hardest part — convincing the brothers

*"My brothers were the last people I got to convince."* After a year of transformation, the brothers wanted to add stakeholder sign-off (stakeholder receives / approves) on top of the outcome measurement — which would have collapsed the model. Chapur pulled all four brothers into a full day: what has been happening, why, what has to change on both sides. *"After so many broken promises from the past, the fact that they trusted me on this speaks a lot of the dynamics between us."* Family-company principle: **"protect the company from ourselves if we're ever in disagreement."**

## Results (~2 years in)

- **Booking-engine conversion: 1% → 5%+.**
- **The cart-abandonment call-center handoff feature: $600K in bookings in 2 months** (March–April) while also generating continuous-discovery insight into why guests weren't booking (e.g. concerns about payment-link security).
- **Cross-department OKRs** replaced isolated tech goals — marketing, sales center, and product all rallied around one objective per period.
- Restaurant-availability at all-inclusive hotels (a real guest problem historically solved by *"physically expanding the restaurant"*) was reframed as a discoverability / booking / gastronomy-vision-marketing problem — the kind of problem the old model couldn't see, let alone solve.
- Culture: *"more data-driven, more customer-centric, more collaborative, more aligned, and more discovery-centric."*

## Chapur's advice to leaders starting the journey

*"Stop having thoughts of your own and start trusting Cagan's thoughts on everything that has to be done. Then get a coach like I did with Gabby — one small derailing can set you in a whole different direction. They're the guiding light like a boat going to an island. You may get distracted, a wave comes in, but you know how to correct course."*

Bufrem's add: Chapur's *unwavering commitment* and *ability to ask for help* were huge — he emailed her back on articles by 7am after she sent them at night.

## Chapur's own summary

**"I hired a team that allows me to be bold."**

## What this case teaches

- The product model **applies outside tech** and **outside the US**.
- A **doing-well business** transforming pre-emptively works — you don't need a burning platform.
- **Trust must be earned before assessment**, especially in relationship-heavy cultures.
- The **three assessment areas** (how you build / solve / decide) give a coach a real starting-point diagnosis.
- **How you solve problems** is often the right first area to change, before how you decide (which requires trust already earned).
- The **App Team pivot** is a textbook example of how real discovery kills the wrong solution — even after it's got a team named after it.
- **Data as a political lever** — building the reliable-source-of-truth capability is often the fastest path to organizational trust.
- **Family-owned business politics** — Chapur's "protect the company from ourselves" is a durable coaching frame.
