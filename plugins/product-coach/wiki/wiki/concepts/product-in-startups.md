# Product in Startups

> How product work is different — and often harder — inside a startup that is still hunting for [[market-fit|product-market fit]], compared with product work at a growth-stage or enterprise company.

## Why it matters
The mechanics of the Product Operating Model as taught in *TRANSFORMED* / *EMPOWERED* / *INSPIRED* assume you have found the market — you're empowering teams to solve real customer problems against real revenue signals. In a pre-PMF startup, none of that is stable yet. The market is still forming; the product may pivot; the team is 5–10 people wearing all the hats. Founders and early hires need a different playbook — one that shares the model's grounding (outcomes over output, customer discovery, feature-parity-is-not-strategy) but reweights heavily toward *market-side pressure testing* and *founder-trait assessment*. [[martina-lauchengco|Lauchengco]] anchors this teaching from her Costanoa Ventures operating-partner lens on [[2025-03-06-idiodi-lauchengco-coaching-startups|Product Therapy Ep 16]].

## How it works
**Ideas are dime-a-dozen; the readiness test is grit for market-side pressure testing.** *"If you're not prepared to talk to 100 people to pressure-test your idea, do not take your idea to VCs — that's the first thing you're going to be asked"* (Lauchengco). Founders who won't share the idea for fear of copying disqualify themselves before they start.

**A great idea ≠ a great business.** VCs don't fund the idea — they fund *"the kernel of an exceptional business."* Lauchengco: *"we see the kernel of a great business, not just a good business — a great one."*

**Market-product fit, not product-market fit** — Lauchengco and Jim Wilson's multi-part series argues the *market* side is what most product-background founders under-invest in. **Coaching pattern:** if the founder is product-background, coach them on market; if marketing-background, coach them on product; if engineering-background, coach on both.

**The 9–10x adoption bar** (Marc Andreessen / Ben Horowitz, cited by Lauchengco): you are displacing an existing product or behavior, and the switch drags every adjacent stakeholder along with it. *"They only use Excel"* is a **warning, not a permission** — Excel is the most-customized, most-mastered tool on the planet. Whiz example: *"turn this one thing on, turn these six other things off"* — the fastest-approved security purchase in history because the switching cost calculus was inverted.

**Great-founder traits** (Lauchengco):
- **Growth mindset** — remain curious as markets, technology, and product strategy shift.
- **Humility** to say *"the market's changed; we need to revisit our product strategy."*
- **Perseverance and grit** — but not stubborn cling-to-a-belief perseverance.
- The single crispest founder-trait indicator: *"do they run into breaches when they see a gap, or do they stay on their side and complain about the gap?"*

**Co-founders — 100% of the time.** Every solo founder Lauchengco has worked with regrets it in retrospect. Look for **intellectual-equal + complementary skills** (not a clone). If you're technical, find a market-side complement; if you're market-side, find a technical.

**The single startup focus is [[market-fit|product-market fit]].** Kingdom-building (marketing dept, sales dept, org chart) before PMF is an absolute distraction. **Idiodi's crisp coaching move:** *"the number one reason you run out of money is you're not trying many things fast enough — 6-to-9-month MVPs against the ~50-attempt-to-PMF benchmark = you'll run out of money."*

**First hire is contextual + opportunistic.** Contextual = whoever your product/space most needs (security space → security engineers or a field CISO). Opportunistic = the Davey-Crockett-first-sales-rep type ("this is the best salesperson I've ever worked with; they're in transition; it's too early but I'm hiring them because they'll create the sales function themselves").

**Joining a startup — the four assessments:**
1. **The team** — strong decision-makers, proven track records, or the essential-elements-of-a-strong-founder traits.
2. **Quality of revenue** — not top-line. Churn, retention, quality of logos, pipeline health. Companies can reach $10M ARR with junk revenue.
3. **Backer quality** — a proxy for due diligence. Some funds are just money; others actually pressure-tested the founders and the market before writing the check.
4. **Your own passion for the space** — do it only if you're passionate. Early-stage work is too hard for a "rational reasons" hire.

**Startup vs enterprise culture** (Lauchengco):
- Enterprises have unavoidable political undercurrents. It's not that they're bad; it's the natural consequence of scale.
- Startups don't yet — which is a *feature* for some (energizing, hard puzzles, constant learning) and stress for others (unclear, ambiguous, "class-five rapids you have to navigate adeptly").

**Leading indicators of success/failure:**
- **Pre-PMF:** how fast the team's problem list turns over. Winners move through problems fast; losers cling to the belief that this specific problem should be solvable *this way*.
- **Post-PMF:** adoption speed + customer quality — quality of revenue, retention, pipeline.

**Lauchengco's investment stance:** *"we invest in the people, not the idea — the idea/product/market/space all change; the people have to have shown they can operate that environment."*

## Anti-patterns & misunderstandings
- **Precious about the idea; won't share it.** Disqualifying.
- **Building the org chart before PMF.** Distraction; kingdom-building.
- **Slow MVP → out of money.** Idiodi's crispest single diagnosis.
- **Product-background founder chasing more product; marketing-background founder chasing more marketing.** Both are optimizing where they already excel; both should coach in the opposite direction.
- **"They only use Excel."** Warning, not permission.
- **Solo founder.** *Every* solo founder Lauchengco worked with regrets it.
- **Hiring on "great sales talent, we need to strengthen product later."** OK as a Davey-Crockett opportunistic hire; not OK as a pattern that leaves product weakness intact.
- **Junk revenue mistaken for PMF signal.** $10M with 60% churn is not PMF.

## In your context
When coaching a product person considering a startup jump, walk through Lauchengco's four assessments before anything else. When coaching a founder, first identify their background bias and coach in the opposite direction. When coaching a founder whose MVP has been in build for 6+ months, invoke Idiodi's *"you'll run out of money"* frame directly. When helping a founder decide whether to accept "big-brand money" vs "hands-on money" from a VC, name the tradeoff Lauchengco named: money-only vs co-thinking-partner, and match to what the founder actually needs at their stage.
