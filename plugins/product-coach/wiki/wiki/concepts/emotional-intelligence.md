# Emotional Intelligence

> The skill of **being smart about emotions** — recognising, understanding and managing your own, and recognising, understanding and influencing other people's. [[kate-leto|Kate Leto]] prefers the broader label **human skills** over "soft skills", and the wiki follows her.

## Why it matters

Product work is [[collaboration|collaborative problem solving]] carried out largely **without line authority**. A product manager persuades a stakeholder, absorbs a difficult customer interview, disagrees with an engineer's estimate, tells a leader that a launch missed, and coaches a peer — all in a week, none of it on the strength of a title. Every one of those is an emotional-intelligence task before it is a craft task.

[[christian-idiodi|Idiodi]]'s claim, made in [[2025-03-20-idiodi-leto-coaching-emotional-intelligence|the *Product Therapy* episode this page is rooted in]] and unchallenged by Leto: **IQ is entry-level for product work; EQ is the higher-order competency.** And it is *foundational to the craft rather than adjacent to it* — better EQ makes you better at customer interviews, at usability testing, at running a meeting, at an objective-setting session, at a roadmap conversation. It is not a bolt-on to the [[the-product-team-trio|trio]]'s technical skills; it is what lets those skills leave your own head.

Two structural facts make it worth a page of its own:

1. **It is developable.** Unlike IQ, EQ can be grown — with commitment, over time, and (Leto is blunt about this) very hard to do alone. That makes it a legitimate subject for [[product-coaching|coaching]] rather than a hiring filter you either pass or fail.
2. **The AI argument.** As models absorb more of the analytical load, the differentiating human contribution shifts toward the things a model cannot do: read a room, hold a hard conversation, decide what is right *for these people, now*. Leto: AI does not help you become the person who is there when things get difficult. Idiodi's compression: *"AI is IQ now."* See [[ai-and-product-teams]] and [[product-sense]].

## How it works — the four competencies

Leto works from **Daniel Goleman**'s four-competency model, split into what she calls the **inner game** and the **outer game**:

| | Competency | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Inner game** | **Self-awareness** | Recognising your emotions, where they come from, and how they land on others. Leto calls it *the* foundational skill — "the meta-skill of our time" — and warns that people commonly mistake it for the internal half only. The external half (how you affect the people around you) is where the work is. |
| **Inner game** | **Self-management** (emotional self-control) | Managing disruptive emotions and impulses. Not never having them — noticing which hot buttons trigger you, and getting back online quickly when one is hit. |
| **Outer game** | **Social awareness** | Empathy: understanding another person's emotions and perspective. The prerequisite for discovery work — customer interviews, reading a room, cultural competence. |
| **Outer game** | **Relationship management** | Influence, productive conflict, teaching and mentoring, inspirational leadership. Leto: *"it's the juicy one."* For product people it is the whole stakeholder surface — up, down, sideways and diagonally. |

**Flipping your lid.** Leto uses Dan Siegel's hand model of the brain: fist closed, thumb inside as the limbic system and amygdala, fingers over the top as the cortex. When an emotional hot button is hit, the amygdala hijacks the prefrontal cortex — the lid flips, and either an outburst or a shutdown follows. Her point is not that this is avoidable; it is that **emotions are contagious**, so what you model becomes what the culture permits, and *the response matters more than the flip*. Idiodi's version: *"there are no good or bad emotions, just good or bad responses to emotions."*

## How to build it — Leto's practice stack

The reason this page is more than a definition: EQ has a concrete, cheap, repeatable practice. Leto calls it the **self-awareness trilogy** — *know thyself · accept thyself · improve thyself*.

**Know thyself — daily reflection.** Ten minutes, morning or evening. *What am I looking to achieve today? What am I worried about? What went well? What do I want to do differently?* **Frame every question with "what", never "why"** — a *why* question sends you into a negative spiral and you probably could not answer it honestly anyway. Patterns emerge from the habit, not from any single entry.

**Accept thyself — get outside your own account of yourself.** A full 360 if you can get one. If not, two exercises that need no budget:

- **Trusted critic.** Pick one or two people who will not sugarcoat. They write down pros and cons of you (working with you, living with you). You write your own. Compare. *The width of the gap is the size of the work.*
- **Observational feedback.** Over a day or a week, simply write down what people actually say to you — *"you're late again", "stop interrupting", "great job on that"* — and read the list at the end. The patterns are usually already being said out loud; they are just not being absorbed.

**Improve thyself — run yourself as an experiment.** Product people are already good at this: take one pattern, define one change, run it for a week, observe the response. *I will join calls five minutes early. I will leave 15 minutes between meetings.*

**Growth partners.** Leto deliberately renames "accountability partner" to **growth partner** — a coach, a peer, or a manager you have told what you are working on, who keeps you honest and asks good questions. Her stated reason for insisting on one: it is very hard to do this alone, and the first two steps of the trilogy will surface things you do not enjoy discovering.

## Anti-patterns & misunderstandings

- **"Soft skills."** The framing itself does the damage — it implies optional and secondary. Leto's *human skills* is the wiki's preferred term. (The hard/soft split, she notes, comes from military training vocabulary.)
- **Treating EQ as niceness or emotional suppression.** It is not "don't have feelings" and it is not conflict avoidance. Productive conflict is *inside* relationship management.
- **Reading only the outer game.** The visible tells — awkwardness, poor room-reading — get attention while the inner game is ignored. Leto: self-awareness comes first, and everything else is built on it.
- **The tells people don't file under EQ, but should.** Blaming others / low accountability; needing to be right and litigating it for days; being unable to take criticism. All three are self-awareness and relationship-management failures, and all three visibly damage a team's ability to solve problems together.
- **Outsourcing it to a class.** Idiodi's version: sending someone on a communication course is not coaching. *Go with them* — learn what they are learning, then practise it together and give feedback. A class does not make you better at communicating; communicating better does. See [[product-coaching]] and [[coaching-vs-contracting]].
- **Confusing it with [[psychological-safety]].** Related and mutually reinforcing, but distinct: EQ is an *individual* capability; psychological safety is a *team property*. High-EQ leaders tend to create it; it is not the same thing.

## In your context

_Agent prompt — ask about the org's own situation, do not assume it. Where do this org's promotion criteria sit on human skills: are they written down at all, or is technical competence the whole ladder? When someone "flips their lid" in a meeting here, what happens next? And does anyone in this organisation have a growth partner — a named person who knows what they are working on and asks about it?_
