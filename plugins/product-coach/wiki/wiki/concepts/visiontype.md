# Visiontype

> A **visiontype** is a special type of prototype used to *communicate* a product vision — a rendering, storyboard, or working demo that dramatizes what the future could look like, before the product, or the ability to build it, actually exists.

## Why it matters
[[product-vision|Vision]] is "mainly a persuasive piece" (INSPIRED Ch 24) — not a spec — and the visiontype is the concrete artifact that does the persuading: it's what a head of product shows a CEO, a board, employees, and prospective recruits to make an abstract multi-year direction feel real and worth pursuing. Without one, a vision tends to stay a slogan (see [[vision-quality]]'s "slogan vision" failure mode). With one, it becomes something people can see, react to, and rally behind.

## How it works
- **Produced after vision is crafted, not instead of it.** In Cagan's process ([[product-vision|see "Crafting it"]]), the 1–2 day offsite with the right people produces *direction*; the visiontype is built **afterward** by the head of product working with a strong product designer.
- **A prototype, not a product.** Like any [[prototypes|prototype]], it is deliberately incomplete — it skips the real engineering work entirely. Its job is to show a *future* customer experience, not to be built out and shipped as-is.
- **It inspires; it doesn't discover or deliver.** The overwhelming majority of the work required to turn a visiontype into an actual product — [[2020-09-04-cagan-discovery-problem-vs-solution|mostly *solution* discovery]], plus [[product-delivery|delivery]] — comes *after* the visiontype exists. A compelling visiontype is the starting gun, not evidence the hard problems are solved.
- **A visiontype can inspire someone else's vision, not just your own.** The canonical illustration ([[2024-01-02-cagan-power-of-visiontypes|Cagan, 2024]]): Xerox PARC's research team built an impressive visiontype of a desktop computer with a GUI — a demo, not a product. It inspired Steve Jobs's *own* product vision for what a desktop computer could be. Xerox had the visiontype and never had the product or company leadership to productize it; Apple discovered and delivered the actual product. **Whoever discovers and delivers wins — not whoever demos.**

### The canonical worked example: the Marissa visiontype (Adobe)
The wiki's fullest example of a visiontype used exactly as intended is Lea Hickman's **Marissa visiontype** at Adobe — the aspirational day-in-the-life narrative for a designer's life inside Creative Cloud, which aligned executives, the board, employees, and customers behind the Creative Suite → Creative Cloud transformation. See [[adobe-creative-cloud-transformation]] for the full case study; Hickman named it the "carrot" half of a stick-and-carrot narrative that helped secure a bet-the-company decision.

## Anti-patterns & misunderstandings
- **Falling in love with the enabling technology, not the problem.** Cagan's own cautionary tale: his HP Labs team's visiontype, "the domesticated computer," was bet on early AI technology that turned out not to be ready for productization — years of work on a visiontype whose underlying tech choice, not its customer insight, was the flaw.
- **Treating the visiontype as proof the vision is validated.** It isn't — Cagan is explicit that "any product vision is a leap of faith" ([[product-vision|see the 10 principles]]); the visiontype makes the leap of faith *legible*, it doesn't remove it.
- **Sharing a visiontype publicly without the ability to execute.** There are real recruiting and partnership benefits to sharing your vision widely, but "if you don't have the ability to discover and deliver the actual products, then don't be surprised if someone else ends up being the one that delivers on your product vision" — a long-running pattern in the industry, PARC/Apple being the canonical case.
- **Confusing the visiontype (the artifact) with the vision (the direction).** The vision is the multi-year destination and rationale; the visiontype is one form — usually the most persuasive one — of communicating it. A storyboard or narrative white paper can serve the same role.
