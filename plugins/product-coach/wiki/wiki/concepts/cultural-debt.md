# Cultural Debt

**Cultural debt** is the hidden cost of neglecting the system that governs how people work together — trust, relationships, communication, decision-making. Analog to technical debt: some is inevitable, but the interest compounds silently. Primary source: [[2026-08-20-idiodi-fisher-understanding-cultural-debt|Product Therapy Ep 43]] with [[mike-fisher|Mike Fisher]].

## Definition

[[mike-fisher|Fisher]]: *"Culture is the system that governs how people work together. It covers the trust we have with each other, the relationships, how we communicate, how we make decisions. Cultural debt is the hidden cost of neglecting those systems. Every time we build products we'll have technical debt. When we make decisions we'll embrace some cultural debt. That's OK. The problem is when we ignore the interest on that debt."*

## What you reward always beats what you say

The core mechanism by which debt accrues: **the gap between stated values and actual incentives.** Fisher: *"The higher you go, you don't realize how much people watch what you do. They watch what you say, who you meet with — they notice tiny little things. If that's different from the values you claim, people notice, and that becomes cultural debt."*

**The Wells Fargo case (early 2000s):** externally proclaimed *customer-first*; internally rewarded *sales quotas*. The gap eventually pushed employees to sign customers up for fake accounts to hit their numbers — millions in fines, lost customer trust. *"You say one thing and reward something else. That is a cultural debt."*

## Wartime rationalizations that become the culture

Fisher: *"It's OK to say 'we're in wartime, we're in a crisis, we've got to do something for a short-term gain.' If you keep doing that, it becomes the culture. Public companies under quarterly-earnings pressure often get stuck — talking about long-term thinking and big bets while only worrying about optimization. Eventually you run out of things to optimize."* Coaching test: *is this quarter's exception setting the pattern for next year's default?*

## Signals cultural debt has gone unhealthy

- **Silence in rooms where there used to be debate.** People stop pushing back, they just go *"yeah."* *"That's a signal because people aren't comfortable — they don't have the trust or relationships."*
- **Increased translation work.** *"When middle management hears you say something and goes 'well, this is what he really meant when he was saying this.'"*
- **Losing great people.**
- **Impacting customers.** Wherever product touches lives — marketplaces where sellers make a living, payments, health — cultural debt has real downstream customer impact.

## The Boeing case — how long cultural debt can last

*"For decades Boeing was synonymous with engineering excellence. Priorities shifted to cost efficiencies. Cultural debt built up. Safety concerns were minimized. Engineers were discouraged from raising issues. Internal dissent seemed like inconvenience. Tragically, two 737 Max crashes. And what's interesting: years later, Alaska Airlines' 737 Max came off the factory line missing critical bolts in one of its doors. That shows how long cultural debt can last."*

## Attribution — blameless postmortem for cultural failures

Fisher: *"If we start with the premise that people come to work to do good work — 99.99% of us come to work saying 'I'm going to do the best I can' — a postmortem is usually about 'something surprised me.' Culture debt's the same. Engineers, designers, mechanics were trying to work within a system. Things got ignored. Ultimately it's a leadership problem — 100%. But it's not the people. It's all the things that led up there for years."*

## Culture is a C-suite responsibility — not an HR problem

Fisher: *"You can't go to your HR leader and say 'fix our culture.' This should be a C-suite issue that people at the highest level own — by the way what they say, the walk they do, what they measure, what they reward, who they talk to, who they praise, the projects that get pushed forward. All of those are indications, and that is how we change our culture."*

## The MyFitnessPal example — paying debt down deliberately

Fully-remote company. Last fall the leadership decided to skip the all-company gathering to focus on shipping features before the January signup surge. *"That's cultural debt — we didn't name it. But for a remote team, getting together renews relationships, renews friendships, builds trust. By not doing that, we incur debt."*

Fisher's paying-down move: at every monthly all-hands, he now speaks for 10 minutes on a topic — the **"School of Fish"** segment. *"Little things like that are how we pay it down."*

## Kindness vs avoidance — the *No More Mr. Nice Guy* fork

Fisher's frame (matching Idiodi's *coaching culture versus conflict avoidance* teaching): *"The problem is not kindness, it's avoidance disguised as kindness."* The candor of [[psychological-safety]] is not conflict — it's *candor without fear.*

## The Idiodi scaling frame

*"I think about scaling from a people, process, and technology perspective. Every company is scaling. If we scale technology too late, everything falls over. Too early — wasted money. Organization is the same — three layers of management for five people is bureaucracy; one layer for 5,000 is confusion. Culture debt is the same. You've got to keep paying it down because you keep incurring it."*

## In your context

An org's cultural-debt baseline lives in that org's own [[strategic-context|strategic-context]] store. When coaching, look for: which values-behavior gaps have been in place longest; where has the debate gone silent; where is *"what he really meant"* translation work appearing; which C-suite behaviors are actively contradicting stated values; is culture being owned at C-suite or delegated to HR.
