# Product Management Theater

> Holding the **title** of product manager (or product leader) while doing a fundamentally different job — usually project management and backlog administration — so the real product work (value + viability, [[product-discovery|discovery]]) goes undone. The *performance* of product management without the substance. Coined and defined in writing by Cagan in [[2024-01-30-cagan-product-management-theater|Product Management Theater]] (Jan 2024); sharpened across his 2024–2026 talks and extended to the leadership layer two weeks later in [[2024-02-13-cagan-product-leadership-theater|Product Leadership Theater]].

## Why it matters
This is Cagan's central diagnosis of the current product-management crisis. The [[2024-01-30-cagan-product-management-theater|root article]] opens with the warning that a long-predicted reckoning for delivery-team product owners and feature-team PMs has begun, and names generative AI as a likely accelerant. The tell, verbatim: a popular PM-training program summarizes the role as *"40% communication and coordination, 20% design, 20% engineering, and 20% business"* — a *project* manager's job description, not a product manager's, and one that a strong tech lead, designer, engineering manager, or program manager could cover just as well. Cagan's name for a person holding the title without the skills: **"big hat, no cattle."** It's theater.

In a [[feature-teams-vs-product-teams|feature team]], the product manager is really a project manager — "dramatically overpaid for the value they provide" ([[2024-03-10-cagan-product-management-theater-lennys|Lenny's]], the fullest spoken elaboration, delivered six weeks after the article) — because the actual product decisions were already made by stakeholders and handed down as a roadmap. Cagan is blunt that in that model "you don't really need a product manager." The theater is the gap between the cool title (which the world knows, thanks to the empowered-team model) and the absent skills and responsibilities.

**The way out, in the order the article prescribes it:** an honest [[2019-04-08-cagan-coaching-tools-the-assessment|self- or coach-led skills assessment]] (blind spots are real), then a [[2019-04-22-cagan-coaching-tools-the-plan|plan]] to build the *right* skills — explicitly **not** design or engineering (that's what the designer and engineers are for), but business skills, data skills, and customer knowledge.

The stakes rose sharply with generative AI, which **simultaneously exposes and accelerates** the theater ([[2026-06-04-cagan-pm-theater-age-of-ai-modern-cto|Modern CTO]]): it makes clear the bottleneck was never the engineers ("how long to build") but product ("*what* to build"), while also making it easier than ever to speed up the project model — "more garbage being shipped than I ever remember." Cagan's "reckoning": delivery-team product owners and feature-team PMs are the most vulnerable roles as companies cut, and GenAI compounds it.

## How it works
**Three models, and the theater lives in the first two** ([[2026-06-04-cagan-pm-theater-age-of-ai-modern-cto|Modern CTO]]): (1) the *product owner* model (agile PO + developers — a "software factory," which AI is making irrelevant); (2) the *feature team* / project model (a roadmap of features handed down; the PM gathers requirements and herds cats); (3) the *empowered product team* (problems to solve; the PM owns value + viability). Only the third needs — and produces — a real product manager.

**The tells:**
- **Output over outcome.** You're measured by shipping, not by solving the problem. Cagan reframes it for executives as *[[time-to-money|time to money]]*, not time to market.
- **The "three ways" a PM works** ([[2018-06-07-cagan-behind-every-great-product-mtpcon-london-2016|Behind Every Great Product]]): (a) escalate every decision to your manager; (b) call a meeting for every decision (design-by-committee); (c) *actually do the job*. Only (c) is product management.
- **The mealy-mouthed job definition** — "I facilitate, I communicate, I herd the cats, I say 'why'." Cagan: "the *why* comes from [[product-strategy|product strategy]] anyway." A real PM is a **creator, not a facilitator**.
- **The written definition of the alternative** ([[2025-05-28-cagan-the-era-of-the-product-creator|The Era of the Product Creator]]): *"anyone actively shaping the product and tackling the product risks... is a product creator. Their title doesn't really matter."* Non-creator PMs — the theater performers above — are named as "increasingly and especially vulnerable" as GenAI removes the need for pure facilitation and administration.
- **Agile theater** — the sibling ([[2024-04-14-cagan-escape-the-project-trap-agile-theatre|Scandinavian Product]], [[2023-01-16-cagan-master-class-context-not-control|Master Class]]): all the rituals (scrum master, CSPO/PSPO product owner, stand-ups, retros) yet **releasing monthly or quarterly** and not empowered. "Waterfall with a marketing name of agile." Agile only ever addresses *one* of the three transformation dimensions (how you build) — the easiest one.
- **Product leadership theater** — the leader version, named and defined in [[2024-02-13-cagan-product-leadership-theater|Product Leadership Theater]] (the direct sequel, two weeks later): a "product leader" who staffs an agency-like org, owns no vision or strategy, and does little coaching.

**Why it proliferated** ([[2024-03-10-cagan-product-management-theater-lennys|Lenny's]]): pandemic over-hiring and a lowered bar; higher cost of funds; predicted GenAI impact; role bloat (agile coaches, product owners, product ops, business analysts, scrum masters, APMs); SAFe "depressingly popular" outside Silicon Valley; and ~90% of online PM advice coming from the feature-team world. Cagan's *Epic Waste* article named the cost a decade earlier.

## Product leadership theater
[[2024-02-13-cagan-product-leadership-theater|Written two weeks after the PM-theater article]], once Cagan noticed readers deflecting blame upward to their managers. His point was never to assign blame — skill-building helps regardless of who's "at fault" — but he names a real pattern: **where PM theater is found, product leadership theater usually travels with it**, because strong PMs are typically coached and developed by strong product leaders.

- **The deflection pattern.** Challenged leaders tend to point up — a non-product CEO, a top-down/sales-driven/date-driven culture — rather than at their own practice. Senior leaders do have real changes to make, but **product leaders have at least as much work to do**, and many wrongly believe that once executives change, everything else falls into place.
- **The five complaints, named.** Leaders complain about weak product managers, about not being empowered, about disengaged/mercenary engineers, about stakeholders who don't trust them, about CEOs who demand detailed roadmaps — without seeing that each is a consequence of their *own* actions or inactions. A leader unwilling to take responsibility for raising her people's skill level will staff teams that lack the competence empowerment requires; the rest of the list follows from that, not from someone else's failure.
- **Two lines worth keeping close:** *"you have as much ownership as you have credibility"*, and *"part of your job as a product leader is to change hearts and minds."*
- **The closing balance.** Success in a transformation starts with the product organization — leaders and teams — raising its own game first, not waiting on the C-suite to move.
- **Also grounds [[coaching-agency]]'s** "leaders blaming down at their teams, up at their executives, and sideways at their peers" framing — previously cited there only via a podcast paraphrase of Shreyas Doshi; this article is the more precisely on-topic root.

## Anti-patterns & misunderstandings
- **Not identical to [[feature-teams-vs-product-teams]].** That page is the *structural* axis (problems vs features; durable vs project). Theater is the *role-substance* symptom that rides on it: a person performing product-management activities without the skills or responsibilities. They travel together but name different things.
- **"My team is empowered."** Often said, rarely true — Cagan treats the claim itself as a clue to look harder.
- **Certifications as proof.** Cagan calls the big PM certifications "bogus"; a CSPO/PSPO teaches backlog administration in Jira, "very analogous to learning how to operate Google Docs" — a tool, not the job. The root treatment is [[2021-06-10-cagan-the-cspo-pathology|The CSPO Pathology (2021)]]: the certification is fine as PO-mechanics training, disastrous when it stands in as PM training.
- **Using AI to end the theater by speeding the project model.** Generating PRDs, aggregating feedback into a roadmap — this deepens the theater. The escape is to *learn the product model* (see [[model-as-product-coach]], [[coaching-vs-contracting]]).
- **Fatalism.** Cagan's counter: even an individual contributor has agency — self-assess, raise your skills from product-owner/feature-team PM toward real PM; companies notice and promote.
