# Coaching Agency

> **Agency** is the belief that you can meaningfully change your circumstances — versus feeling like a victim of factors beyond your control. In an empowered team, agency is a **prerequisite**: without it, the mechanics of ownership, discovery, and outcome-orientation don't take hold, no matter how the org is structured. From [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] on the inaugural episode of [SVPG's *Product Therapy* podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9DAPrL5mdY): *"Agency in the work context means — do people feel they have the ability to change their circumstances in meaningful ways, versus feeling the victim of factors beyond their control?"*

## Why it matters
[[empowered-product-teams|Empowered teams]] work only when the people on them believe they can act. Cagan's most common inbound: *"I'm trapped in a feature team"* — the language itself is the tell. A person with no sense of agency inside an empowered structure will drift back into feature-team behavior — waiting for tasks, escalating decisions, blaming the environment — because outcomes feel like someone else's job. Idiodi's diagnosis: *"selflessness is the opposite of what we're trying to build; the opposite of helplessness is self-efficacy."* The [[missionaries-vs-mercenaries|missionaries-vs-mercenaries]] distinction is the visible surface of the same trait — a mercenary is a person whose agency has been squeezed out of the role.

## How it shows up — three root causes
Ep 1 of *Product Therapy* names three distinct psychological roots for low agency; each needs a different coaching move.

1. **Do they even want the responsibility?** *"There are people in the world that do not want the pressure of being asked to take responsibility for an outcome."* This is not judgment — some people are genuinely happier being told what to do. It is diagnostic: an empowered team is not a fit for that person, and both sides are better off knowing.
2. **Do they feel *worthy*?** *"I'm just a new product manager — why should I get to decide these things?"* — the self-worth story where outcome ownership feels like an overreach. The coaching answer: *"that is your job; the company is counting on you to do that."*
3. **Are they *scared*?** Low-agency environments are lower-stress — *"if things go wrong, it's not your fault."* Empowerment moves accountability toward the person, and the fear of visible failure is often what actually blocks the shift, especially where past risk-taking was punished.

**Product Leadership Theater** ([[product-management-theater|Cagan]]) is the leadership version of this: *"many leaders go through their entire career blaming down at their teams, blaming up at their executives, and blaming sideways at their peers — and don't realize how much agency they truly have"* (Shreyas Doshi, quoted in this episode).

## How it works — the coaching move (Ep 1's tip of the week)
Idiodi's script for turning a handed-down feature into a problem-to-solve (repeatable, disarming, works across seniority):

1. *"That's a great idea."* — you have to be the safe place for people to bring ideas; the disarming phrasing prevents the fight.
2. *"I want to make sure we succeed when we do it."* — say *when*, not *if*; the framing signals partnership, not resistance, without committing to build the specific thing.
3. *"What problem are we trying to solve, and how will we know we've succeeded?"* — the two questions that convert an output request into an outcome and expose whether the requester actually has clarity.

Practiced across every incoming request, this script quietly rewires the team from output-taking to problem-solving. It also exposes upstream leadership gaps — the leader dictating tasks often can't answer the two questions either, which starts a different conversation.

## Agency in leaders — the "care" prerequisite
Idiodi's leadership version of the same claim: *"it's very hard for people to care if you don't care as a leader."* The demonstration of care is what unlocks agency downstream — a leader who provides deep context, gives clarity about why work matters, and knows individuals' situations well enough to coach one-on-one. Agency is not coached in a group setting; it is coached one-on-one, one person at a time. Skip-level leaders who never do one-on-ones therefore also cannot build agency in their orgs.

## Anti-patterns & misunderstandings
- **"Empowered" = told to be empowered.** Structural empowerment without agency development just moves the frustration inward — the person now feels responsible for outcomes they don't believe they can influence. Empowerment must be earned in both directions.
- **Treating low agency as attitude.** Frame it as attitude and you get defensiveness; frame it as one of the three root causes (want / worth / fear) and you get somewhere.
- **Coaching agency in a group setting.** Teams are groups of individuals — agency is coached one person at a time.
- **Ignoring the environment.** Sometimes low agency is well-earned: leaders punish risk, or make decisions from an ivory tower. The coaching move is still individual, but the org-level fix is on the leadership side.
