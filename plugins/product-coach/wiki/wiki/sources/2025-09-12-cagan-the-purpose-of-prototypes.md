# Cagan — The Purpose of Prototypes

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The Purpose of Prototypes" · svpg.com, 2025-09-12 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-purpose-of-prototypes/). First in Cagan's "product creator series." Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** Root source for the wiki's **fidelity framework** (how realistic a prototype needs to be) and for the argument that gen-AI prototyping tools (Lovable, Bolt, Figma Make) changed the *cost calculus* of prototyping — specifically making **live-data prototypes** dramatically cheaper — without changing the *purpose* of prototyping itself. Does **not** cover the four prototype types in detail (that taxonomy's own root is [[2014-02-23-cagan-flavors-of-prototypes|Flavors of Prototypes]], ingested 2026-09-06 — this article only links to it) — cite [[prototypes]]'s own Sources list for the types breakdown, not this card.

## Notes for the coach
- **The core claim.** Gen-AI prototyping tools didn't change *why* we prototype — the highest-order purpose is still to discover a solution worth building, tested against the four risks — they changed the *cost*, specifically of live-data prototypes, which used to require developer time and are now within reach of anyone.
- **The fidelity framework (new to the wiki).** Three dimensions: **visual** (how realistic it looks), **behavioral** (how realistic the interactions are), **data** (real/"live" data vs. realistic-but-fake vs. made-up). "Just enough fidelity" is Cagan's caveat against — it depends on *which risk* you're testing and *who* you're testing it with: a CISO often needs low visual/behavioral fidelity to assess security; a marketing exec or CEO may need high visual fidelity but not behavioral; a lawyer often needs high fidelity across all three; a feasibility prototype may need no UI at all.
- **Prototype as spec.** Tom Kelley (IDEO): *"if a picture is worth a thousand words, then a prototype is worth a thousand meetings."* Cagan's caution: this is a *secondary* benefit — teams that use prototyping tools only for communication (never testing the prototype) end up building and shipping something that fails in market.
- **Interview signal.** Cagan notes top product-model companies are now evaluating candidates' use of these tools during interviews — corroborates the "new litmus test" already on [[product-manager]] from the 2026-04-14 talk.
