# Cagan & Fredell — M&A and the Product Model

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan & Thomas Fredell · "M&A and the Product Model" · svpg.com, 2025-01-21 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/ma-and-the-product-model/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon), co-authored** — Thomas Fredell, a transformation coach Cagan profiles in *TRANSFORMED* via the Datasite case study, co-authors. Applies the product model as a **due-diligence and integration lens** for mergers and acquisitions — a different use case from the rest of the transformation canon (not "how does an org transform" but "how do you assess a target org's ability to keep creating product, before and after you buy it").

## What it backs
- synthesis: [[where-the-model-applies]] (a main pillar — M&A as a special-case application: using the product model as a due-diligence lens)
- diagnostics: [[transformation-readiness]], [[model-maturity]] (light cross-reference — the article's "product organization assessment" is the same category of instrument these two diagnostics already formalize, applied to an acquisition target rather than one's own org)

## Notes for the coach
- **The headline statistic:** Harvard Business Review estimates 70%+ of M&A deals don't generate expected results (Roger L. Martin, HBR, June 2016).
- **The core diagnostic move:** typical M&A due diligence assesses the *products being bought* (cost, revenue, customer satisfaction); what most companies fail to assess is *the organization's ability to keep creating new product going forward* — a much deeper, product-and-technology-organization-focused diligence.
- **"Deal fever" named as the central failure mode** — leaders get excited, rush diligence, fall prey to confirmation bias once a target looks promising. Named countermeasures: red teams, cross-functional expert engagement, defined decision criteria, scenario planning/stress testing, walk-away disciplines, external advisors, a culture of healthy skepticism.
- **The product-organization-assessment questions, four categories** (deliberately overlapping with existing wiki diagnostics — do not duplicate, cross-reference): building/deploying cadence and tech-debt state; team type (delivery/feature/empowered) and ownership level; how the org solves problems (empowerment, customer access, outcome focus); how the org decides what to invest in (vision, strategy, outcome tracking).
- **M&A as a transformation strategy, and its named failure mode:** acquiring an innovative smaller company hoping its culture spreads to the parent — but the more common outcome is the reverse: the acquired company buckles under the parent's weight (innovation slows, best people leave, customers notice). Requires real top-level commitment to protect the acquired culture.
- **Cross-reference:** [[datasite-transformation]] is the wiki's own worked example of a company (Merrill Corporation → Datasite) that went through the kind of organizational transformation this article is assessing readiness *for* — worth pointing a coach to both together.
- **When to cite:** any coaching conversation framed around evaluating an acquisition target, or a company that was itself recently acquired and is assessing what changes.
