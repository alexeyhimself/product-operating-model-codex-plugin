# Product Discovery at Apple

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Product Discovery at Apple* · SVPG, Sep 28, 2018 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/product-discovery-at-apple/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — and the SVPG endorsement that grounds the [[creative-selection]] book entity page.** The article is Cagan's review of *Creative Selection: Inside Apple's Design Process During the Golden Age of Steve Jobs* by **Ken Kocienda**, and it doubles as the only detailed account in the corpus of how discovery worked inside [[apple|Apple]].

- **The endorsement.** *"I wanted to encourage product people everywhere to read it"* and *"I really do hope you'll all read this book."* Under CLAUDE.md §2 this is an explicit, checkable endorsement of a specific work — and *Creative Selection* also appears on the [SVPG recommended-reading list](https://www.svpg.com/recommended-reading/), so it clears the bar twice over.
- **The vantage point.** Told *"from the perspective of an engineer"* — Kocienda's ~15-year arc from inexperienced developer to principal engineer, mostly as tech lead, *"most significantly, the original iPhone keyboard."* Strong material for [[empowered-engineers]]: the innovation is engineer-led throughout.
- **What Cagan points out is *absent*, and says is no accident.** *"You won't see a single reference to product roadmaps, or to business cases, or to techniques for prioritizing features, or even the concept of product requirements."*
- **The risk framing of the iPhone keyboard.** *"Obvious and major technical feasibility risk, but equally significant usability risk, and the most significant risk of all, the value of the overall device was clearly a function of whether this keyboard solution would be perceived as good enough."* A clean three-of-four-risks worked example ([[the-four-big-risks]]).
- **The Newton as the stakes.** Apple's earlier device *"was doomed because they couldn't come up with an effective solution for entering text."*
- **"Creative selection" — Kocienda's term**, which Cagan relays: an idea evolving through *"literally hundreds"* of prototype iterations, *"continuously guided in the right direction by feedback from different perspectives."* Prototypes tested on colleagues and on demanding product leaders *"including but not limited to Steve Jobs."*
- **Corrects a common myth about Apple.** *"Most people think Apple does all their discovery work qualitatively, and while you'll see that is certainly a big part of it, they also intelligently use data to inform tough decisions"* — his favourite example being how the team determined the optimal icon size for the original iPhone.
- **Cagan ties it back to his own thesis.** The book's three themes are *"precisely"* the three from [Beyond Lean and Agile](https://www.svpg.com/beyond-lean-and-agile/) *(uncarded)*: tackle risks up front, solve problems collaboratively, focus on results not output. He also calls it *"another great example of the type of engineering-powered innovations"* from [[2017-09-25-cagan-customer-inspired-technology-enabled|Customer Inspired; Technology Enabled]].

## Notes for the coach
The strongest available counter to *"that only works at companies like Apple"* — because the account is Apple's, is told by an engineer, and contains none of the artifacts the coachee's company treats as essential. The icon-size example is a useful corrective in the other direction too, for teams that romanticize Apple as purely taste-driven and therefore conclude evidence does not matter.
