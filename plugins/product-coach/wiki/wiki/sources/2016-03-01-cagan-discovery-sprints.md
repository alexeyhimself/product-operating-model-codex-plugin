# Discovery Sprints

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Discovery Sprints* · SVPG, Mar 1, 2016 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/discovery-sprints/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the definition of the discovery sprint, and Cagan's explicit renaming of the "design sprint."**

- **The definition.** *"A discovery sprint is a one week time-box of product discovery work, designed to tackle at least one substantial problem or risk in your product's definition."* See [[2012-08-21-cagan-time-boxing-product-discovery]].
- **The nomenclature argument.** *"Some people use the term design sprint for this, but as the purpose of the work, when done well, goes significantly beyond design, I prefer the more general term."* Worth citing whenever a team says "design sprint" — Cagan's objection is about scope, not about Google Ventures' method.
- **Positioning against the rest of the toolkit.** He frames it as *"a good example of a product team working in what's known as Dual-Track Agile, or Continuous Discovery and Delivery"* ([[continuous-discovery]]), and as *"a very good way to start getting the value from"* MVP if a company has been struggling with that concept.
- **Where it came from.** The [Google Ventures](http://www.gv.com/team/) team, whose model was to *"spend a week with the startup, rolling their sleeves up, and showing them how to do product discovery by doing it with them side-by-side."* Cagan notes others do the same: *"several other proven product people that are known as discovery coaches"* — an early use of that role name, relevant to [[product-coach]].
- **The shape of the week.** Map the problem space → pick the problem and target customer → pursue several approaches → narrow and flesh out → build a high-fidelity prototype → put it in front of real target users and observe.
- **When to use one.** Three named situations: the team is *"struggling to learn how to do product discovery"*; there is something *"big and critically important and/or difficult to tackle"*; or *"things are just moving too slow."* Also: *"we don't do discovery sprints that often as they're a special tool"* (per [[2016-11-29-cagan-planning-product-discovery]]).

**⚠ Endorsement-test candidate — flagged for a curator decision, not acted on.** Cagan reviewed and explicitly recommends *[Sprint: How To Solve Big Problems and Test New Ideas In Just Five Days](https://www.amazon.com/Sprint-Solve-Problems-Test-Ideas/dp/150112174X)* by **Jake Knapp, John Zeratsky and Braden Kowitz**, calling it *"one of the best new books on product I've seen in a long time"* and *"a proven, step-by-step recipe."* Under CLAUDE.md §2 this is an **explicit, checkable endorsement of a specific work** — the same shape as the Torres and Kerievsky exceptions — but *Sprint* does **not** appear on the current [SVPG recommended-reading list](https://www.svpg.com/recommended-reading/). The wiki therefore holds `sprint-knapp` as an **unbuilt deliberate non-target pending a ruling**; do not create an entity page for it without one.

## Notes for the coach
Two live uses. First, when a team proposes a design sprint as *the* discovery process — Cagan's own framing is that it is a **special tool for intense, high-stakes moments**, not a cadence. Second, when a team has never done discovery at all: he explicitly recommends it as a structured on-ramp, which is a rare case of Cagan endorsing a recipe. Flag the *Sprint* book question to the curator rather than resolving it in a session.
