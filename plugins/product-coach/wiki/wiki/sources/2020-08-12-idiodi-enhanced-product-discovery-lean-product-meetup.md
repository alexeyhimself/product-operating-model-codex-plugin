# Idiodi — Knowing What You Can't Know: Enhanced Product Discovery (Lean Product Meetup)

> **Citation:** Christian Idiodi (SVPG), *Knowing What You Can't Know: Enhanced Product Discovery*, Lean Product Meetup (Dan Olsen), talk delivered 2020-07-23, published 2020-08-12 · YouTube — [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eQKaFEqhiqc). Slim provenance card; the transcript is **not reproduced**. The Q&A/fireside chat that followed the same evening is a separate card: [[2020-08-19-idiodi-olsen-enhanced-product-discovery-fireside-lean-product-meetup]].

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** The **earliest and most complete Idiodi statement** of what would later become the wiki's [[reference-customer-program|reference-customer-program]] entry in Idiodi's own voice — three-plus years before the [[2023-12-21-idiodi-essence-of-product-management-lennys|Lenny's Podcast]] restatement and four years before *TRANSFORMED* Ch 23. The framing here is different from the later ones: Idiodi treats the reference-customer program as **the** *enhanced* discovery technique — the one you reach for when time is short and you need answers with evidence fast. Same substance as [[inspired|INSPIRED]] Ch 15's Charter User Program, but pitched as a **pressure cooker** rather than as one of many go-to-market instruments.

## What it backs
- frameworks: [[reference-customer-program]] (the earliest Idiodi long-form treatment: **6–8 B2B / 25 B2C** early adopters; recruiting is a demand signal; all references must want the same thing; the [[market-fit|product-market-fit]] definition — *"the smallest product that meets the needs of these six or twenty-five is what I build in production"*)
- concepts: [[the-four-big-risks]] (the clean four-question restatement in Idiodi's own words: *"Will they buy it? Can they use it? Can our stakeholders support it? Can we build it?"* — value · usability · viability · feasibility), [[product-discovery]] (**the seven-item failure taxonomy** for why discovery struggles in most orgs — see below), [[empowered-product-teams]] (named as the second of three preconditions for enhanced discovery to work at all), [[technology-adoption-life-cycle]] (early adopters vs. technologists, per Geoffrey Moore's *Crossing the Chasm* — the sushi-restaurant thought experiment)
- competencies: [[engineers]] (engineers must be involved in *defining* the problem and *validating* the solution, not handed a spec — *"no handoffs"*; the Steve Jobs *"hire smart people so they can tell us what to do"* line)
- concepts: [[strategic-context]] (the first of three preconditions: teams must know they're solving the right problems), [[team-objectives]] (OKRs as how problems are assigned to teams — *"discovery is the technique of us discovering the best solutions to those problems"*)

## Notes for the coach
- **The seven-item failure taxonomy for discovery**, delivered as the setup for *why* an enhanced technique is needed. The wiki should carry this as Idiodi's own diagnostic checklist because it names failure modes the SVPG book canon addresses only in passing:
  1. **Confirmation-based discovery** — using discovery to prove you were right, not to find the answer.
  2. **Over-processed discovery** — so ceremonial it no longer feels natural.
  3. **Partial discovery** — PM and design in the room, no engineering.
  4. **One-dimensional discovery** — a single technique used for every question.
  5. **Big-bang discovery** — one long phase, then throw over the wall.
  6. **Waterfall discovery + agile delivery** — discovering a specification, then sprinting it out. *"I get teams today that do waterfall discovery and agile delivery."*
  7. **Outsourced discovery** — handed to another group or agency. *"I'm not a fan of outsourced delivery, so I'm definitely not a fan of outsourced discovery."*
- **The three preconditions** for enhanced discovery to work, stated at the end of the talk: **strategic context**, an **empowered team**, and **who is in the room** — design and engineering involved from problem-framing through solution, *no handoffs*. This is the wiki's clearest short list of what has to be true *before* the technique is even worth trying.
- **The Snagajob / Starbucks / McDonald's / LAX story** — his career worked example of the technique. The bakery-acquisition trigger at Starbucks (~800 potentially undocumented workers to replace fast, quietly); the McDonald's construction site he stumbled onto that revealed the same "hire a lot of people fast" problem; the Macy's holiday-hiring generalization; the LAX airport counter-example that killed the demographic-matching special (they had to raise pay to $14/hr and still couldn't recruit) — the technique also tells you **who your customer is not**. Batch 9's log already noted (Judgment call 2, 2026-09-05) that this story does not earn its own case-study page — cited from inside this card, not built out separately.
- **The reference-customer definition**, in Idiodi's exact words: someone who *"has the problem, uses your solution, loves it enough to tell other people about it."* Behavioural, not attitudinal — the same test the [[2023-12-21-idiodi-essence-of-product-management-lennys|Lenny's]] card already carries, first attested here.
- **Recruiting is a demand signal.** If you can't find six people (or twenty-five) that have the problem strongly enough to give you their time, *"maybe there really is not a problem. Maybe you actually haven't defined the problem."* Predates and matches [[inspired|INSPIRED]]'s formulation.
- **All references must want the same thing.** *"If one of the 25 says 'I want the app to be red' — great idea. But if all 25 don't say they want it red, you don't do it."* This is how the *minimum* in minimum-viable is arrived at — the intersection of what every reference wants, not the union.
- **Two philosophy quotes Idiodi returns to** — worth preserving because they're his framing devices, not the wiki's:
  - **Marc Andreessen** (attributed): *"The most important thing is to know what you can't know."* Idiodi's version to PMs: *"Your job is to know what you can't know and to admit when you don't know and to bridge the gap between not knowing and knowing."*
  - **Ed Catmull**, *Creativity Inc.*: discovery, by definition, means *you do not know the answer when you start.* Pixar as the standing example of a team that treats every film as a discovery effort.
- **The Steve Blank frame** — *"No facts exist inside the building, only opinions. Teams must get out of the building."* Idiodi attributes the reference-customer/customer-development technique to Blank's *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* and cites Eric Ries as Blank's student. **Blank himself is *not* a Cagan-endorsed source** by the wiki's non-SVPG inclusion test (see `CLAUDE.md` §2) — cite him in passing from *inside this card* only, do not build him a source card or entity page.
- **The talk predates the Feb 2025 pessimism on zero-to-one** on [[2025-02-25-cagan-a-vision-for-product-teams]]: Idiodi in 2020 is unambiguous that this technique *is* the zero-to-one discovery playbook and works B2B, B2C, and platform. No contradiction — he's talking about *how* to do zero-to-one, not whether SVPG-typical teams are the right ones to attempt it.
