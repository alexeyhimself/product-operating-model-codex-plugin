## Notes for the coach
- **The prerequisite, before any of the three steps.** Senior leaders — "normally starting with the CEO" — must understand "the necessary role of technology as the key *enabler* of the business, and not just a necessary cost of doing business." Cagan: "Without this understanding, your chances of success are low." Same root cause as problem #3 in [[2021-06-15-cagan-the-mba-pathology]]; both are good sources for the unbuilt `technology-as-cost-center` page.
- **The three steps, and the order is the point** — "as a general rule, they need to happen in this order":
  1. **Strong product leaders in place.** Without them you can't recruit and coach the staff, can't produce a real product strategy, and "you won't earn the trust of the leaders and the stakeholders." Cagan notes this had been the main focus of his writing for the prior year.
  2. **Let those leaders recruit and develop the staff** for empowered teams — "this almost always means raising the bar on the product managers." Important nuance for coaching: **you don't need to up-level all teams at once** — only ensure a given team is staffed to the task *before* you empower it. This is the strongest SVPG grounding for team-by-team rollout and connects directly to [[pilot-teams]].
  3. **Redefine the relationship with the business** — only for teams ready to operate in the model.
- **The subservient→partner shift, stated cleanly.** In the feature-team model "the stakeholders were largely in control and the feature teams were set up to be *subservient* to the business." In the product model the intent is "to be *true partners* with the business." And the reframe of the stakeholder's role: not "the client" who tells you what to build and must "be managed," but "a strategic partner that we need to help understand the constraints so that together we can discover a solution that works" — specifically, stakeholders "help us with business *viability*." His example: sitting with a company lawyer to discuss legal constraints, because "no matter how much the customer might absolutely love the solution, if it's not legal it's a non-starter."
- **The honest note on incentives.** Cagan acknowledges leaders are being asked to "take a pretty big leap of faith," and that what's in it for them is mainly that "the old way of working has never been very effective" — conditional on trust in the product leaders. Useful realism when coaching someone building a transformation case.
- **Empathy for the current state.** "For most feature teams, the most dreaded aspect of the product manager role is dealing with the stakeholders … There simply is not enough time, or enough people, and sometimes the requests from the stakeholders don't even make sense." He is careful that empowered teams don't get to *ignore* stakeholders — the relationship changes, it doesn't lapse.
- **Provenance note.** The closing paragraph announces that [[lea-hickman|Lea Hickman]] "has been specializing in these topics and large company transformations, and she will be publishing her learnings over the coming months" — which dates the start of her transformation series.
