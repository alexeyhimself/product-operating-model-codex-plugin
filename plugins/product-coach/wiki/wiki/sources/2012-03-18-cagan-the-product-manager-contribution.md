# Cagan — The Product Manager Contribution

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The Product Manager Contribution" · svpg.com, 2012-03-18 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-manager-contribution/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** Cagan's canonical answer to "if we have a strong designer and a strong tech lead, do we still need a product manager?" — the article that names the **four deep-knowledge areas** the PM must bring to the empowered team:

1. **Deep knowledge of the users and customers** (qualitative + quantitative)
2. **Deep knowledge of the data** (product analytics, sales analytics, warehousing)
3. **Deep knowledge of the business** (GTM, stakeholders, business model, ecosystem, economics, privacy/security, ethics)
4. **Deep knowledge of the industry** (competitors, technology and behaviour trends)

Frames the PM as accountable for **value and viability** (not usability or feasibility), operating on an **empowered product team** (not a feature team), and as an **individual contributor** (the word "manager" in the title is misleading — the PM is not the boss). Notes that in early-stage startups the role belongs to the CEO/founders, and that in deep-domain contexts (tax software, medical devices) the PM may be supplemented by subject-matter experts.

## Notes for the coach
- **The 2012 date is the point.** The four deep-knowledge areas + PM-owns-value-and-viability framing pre-date *TRANSFORMED* (2024) and even the 2nd-edition *INSPIRED* (2017) by five and twelve years respectively — the modern PM accountability is not a recent invention. When someone treats "value + viability = PM" as a *TRANSFORMED* innovation, this article is the counter-cite.
- **CEO-of-the-product, correctly.** The article contains the crisp version of Cagan's "not the boss" framing: "an empowered product team is a flat structure, and this means the product manager is not the boss of anyone." Pair with [[product-manager]]'s Cagan-quote treatment.
- **Domain-expert caveat.** For deep-domain products the PM can be supplemented by SMEs — but "these cases are fairly rare. The normal case is that the product manager needs to have the necessary domain expertise." Useful when a user asks whether they can staff a PM without domain knowledge and lean on SMEs.
- **The design/research boundary.** Consistent with [[2020-06-12-cagan-product-management-start-here|the primer]]: user research and design are the designer's job. If the PM is doing them, either the team lacks a designer or the PM is misallocating time.
