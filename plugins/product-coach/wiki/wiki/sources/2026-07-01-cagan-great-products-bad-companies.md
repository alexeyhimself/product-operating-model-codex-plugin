## Notes for the coach
- **The reversal Cagan is making.** He's long argued great products power great companies — still true, but he now stresses **necessary, not sufficient**: *"I have seen too many examples where great products can actually lead companies to very bad outcomes."* A company that demonstrably ships great products becomes an attractive target for people who want a quick financial win, not a durable mission — "the kind that infiltrate boards and leadership teams, replace founders and other missionary leaders, and destroy cultures and companies."
- **Personal, not abstract.** Cagan says he has personally "begged board members not to replace leaders" over normal execution bumps, without success — flagged as his own limited track record persuading non-product investors and board members, not a solved problem.
- **The book that changed his mind.** [*Incorruptible*](https://www.incorruptible.co/) by Eric Ries (2026) — cited by plain hyperlink, not ingested as its own entity page (the wiki's non-SVPG-source policy is still an open decision, Batch 8 Part 4). Where Ries's earlier *The Lean Startup* was about building strong *products*, *Incorruptible* is about building strong *companies* — defined here as companies structurally protected from the predators that successful products attract. Cagan flags this is outside his own expertise (corporate governance, normally deferred to lawyers) and that he can't predict how well the ideas will land, but he's persuaded enough to want more "mission-locked" companies to exist.
- **The practical takeaway Cagan offers product people.** Understand your company's governance structure — it materially affects whether the culture and mission you joined for can survive. As more product people become aware of governance options, Cagan hopes they'll "vote with their feet."
