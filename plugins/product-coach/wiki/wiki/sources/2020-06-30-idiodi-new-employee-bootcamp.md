# New Employee Bootcamp

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] · *New Employee Bootcamp* · SVPG, Jun 30, 2020 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/new-employee-bootcamp/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — Idiodi's own five-day onboarding program**, and the article-form root for the onboarding half of [[hiring-and-onboarding]].

**⚠️ Authorship note.** The page's byline and most clippers read this as a Cagan article. It is not. Cagan wrote only the one-paragraph italic introduction (*"I asked Christian to describe his program here"*); the body carries an explicit **"By Christian Idiodi"** and is written in his first person (*"I created a New Employee Bootcamp"*). Attribute the program and every claim below to **Idiodi**. This makes it his **seventh** SVPG-authored article, not six.

Key material:
- **The problem it solves.** *"Product people don't start at a new company with everything they need to succeed, no matter how successful they've been in the past."* Standard new-hire orientation covers welcome and logistics but not the things that actually determine contribution — making hard decisions, and earning peer trust.
- **The four questions he designed around**, drawn from watching capable hires fail: *How are decisions made, and how have they been made in the past? What is important to the company now? How can I get people to trust me? What's the most important thing to do right now?*
- **Structure — five intensive days, in the first week.** Each day runs the same three-part shape:
  1. **Personal growth first** — communication exercises, personality tests, a self-authored career growth path. Rationale: *"put on your own oxygen mask before assisting other passengers."*
  2. **A [[strategic-context|Strategic Context]] topic** — he uses that exact term. Day one is understanding the customer, told through the company's own history, vision, financial models and customer discovery: who our customers have been, and who we want them to be. The rest of the week covers validation, building and prioritizing, learning and measuring, and going to market.
  3. **A serving product person tells their own story on that topic**, then an afternoon **Product Workshop** applying it with the actual teammates they'll work with.
- **The outcome test.** *"When the product person leaves the Bootcamp she's not asking, 'What do I do today?' She already knows the next, right thing to do."*
- **Why it's an empowerment mechanism, not an HR one.** *"We're not hiring smart product people to tell them what to do — we're hiring them to solve hard problems in ways our customers love, yet work for our business."*

## Notes for the coach
The most directly *actionable* source in the onboarding cluster: it is a week-by-week agenda a leader can copy, not a set of principles. Its coaching leverage is the diagnostic behind it — when a capable senior hire underperforms in their first quarter, Idiodi's claim is that the failure is almost never capability but **un-delivered strategic context plus un-built trust**, both of which are the hiring manager's to fix in week one.
