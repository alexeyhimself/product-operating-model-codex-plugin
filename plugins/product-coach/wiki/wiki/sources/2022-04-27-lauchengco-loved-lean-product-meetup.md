## Notes for the coach
- **The Microsoft Word story is the spine of the talk** and appears in two of the three Lauchengco sources in this batch. Development time halved, feature count halved, in an era when the *number of features* on the box was the perceived value. A keystroke study of 200 users showed ~75% of all actions fell into four basic categories (formatting, file management, printing) — so the release was positioned as *improving how people actually use a word processor*. Best-reviewed and most commercially successful version of Word to that point, with a feature deficit. It is the wiki's clearest example that **positioning can carry a release the product alone would not**.
- **The delivery mattered as much as the story:** they deliberately ditched PowerPoint — at Microsoft — and drew the frequency-vs-utilisation graph live on a whiteboard, so analysts leaned in instead of waiting out a deck.
- **Looker vs. RJ Metrics** is her messaging A/B: the short, benefit-shaped line that "could be Microsoft Excel," versus the specific, technical, *why-you-should-believe-it* line. Billions-dollar outcome vs. mediocre one; the RJ Metrics CEO's own "better horse-drawn carriage / Model T" line. Backs [[messaging]] — **the best messaging also positions**.
- **The word-count story** (Word for Mac: fix the one metric the press personally uses every day, and performance perception moves) is the canonical [[pmm-evangelist]] example.
- **Named critique of the industry's own frameworks:** Pragmatic Marketing's 37 boxes and the Product Marketing Alliance's five rings, as the reason the *tasks* got conflated with the *role*. Direct parallel to [[principles-over-process]] on the product side — the same failure mode, one function over.
