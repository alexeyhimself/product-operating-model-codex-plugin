# Cagan — Build vs Buy in the Age of AI

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Build vs Buy in the Age of AI" · svpg.com, 2025-08-19 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/article-build-vs-buy-in-the-age-of-ai/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — the root source for [[build-vs-buy-and-vibe-coding]]**, a new concept the wiki had no home for. Argues that "vibe coding" (natural-language user-programming tools like Lovable and Bolt) will not make SaaS obsolete, because enterprise business software encodes thousands of business rules that non-technical builders — and most technical ones — don't have access to. The Model Context Protocol (MCP) is the enabler that lets the future be "yes to both": companies keep buying complex component services, but those services become usable by both humans and AI agents.

## Notes for the coach
- **The history.** User programming isn't new: VisiCalc (1979, Apple II) is the first form; Visual Basic (1991) the first mainstream low-code tool; millions of Excel formulas run today as a direct descendant. What's new with GenAI vibe-coding tools (Lovable, Bolt) is that the "programming language" is now plain English, opening the capability to "nearly anyone with a problem to solve."
- **Why SaaS survives — the business-rules argument, verbatim.** *"The reason your business software... is not likely to be replaced with a user programmed solution is due to business rules and the associated business logic."* Thousands of rules and millions of lines of logic encode policy, compliance, security, legal, financial, and pricing constraints — the same knowledge PMs (and business analysts before them) have always needed to [define viable solutions](https://www.svpg.com/product-vs-policy/). This is also, per Cagan, why tech debt is hard to retire: teasing out which business rules still apply.
- **The forecast: "yes to both."** Companies keep buying complex component services, but those services get built to be used by both humans *and* AI agents — some agents built by the vendor, some by systems integrators, some by end customers. The enabler is [Anthropic's Model Context Protocol](https://www.anthropic.com/news/model-context-protocol) (proposed ~2024), which finally gives the industry a widely-accepted way for software to describe its own services to other software, not just to people — a problem Cagan says the industry had needed solved "starting with the advent of the internet."
- **Distinguishing vibe-coding audiences.** Footnote distinguishes engineer-productivity tools (Cursor, Replit) from tools aimed at non-technical end users (Lovable, Bolt) — acknowledges the line is blurring as the space develops.
- **The closing reframe, verbatim.** *"The most important lesson of all is that the hard part is rarely building and delivering the solution; the hard part is discovering the right solution to build."* — the same [[product-discovery|product-discovery]] discipline, now addressed to a newly-empowered non-technical builder audience.
