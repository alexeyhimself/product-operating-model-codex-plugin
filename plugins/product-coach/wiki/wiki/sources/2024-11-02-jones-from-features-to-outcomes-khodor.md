# Jones — From Features to Outcomes (Mahmoud Khodor)

> **Citation:** Chris Jones (SVPG) interviewed by Mahmoud Khodor, *From Features to Outcomes: Transforming Product Teams* · YouTube, published 2024-11-02 — [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7KA-FguOI2s). ~75 minutes; the longest and most competency-focused of the four Jones interviews in this batch. Slim provenance card; transcript **not reproduced**.

## What it backs
- competencies: [[product-manager]] (value + viability as the PM's two risks — and the observation that on a feature team *neither* is the team's problem, which is why the role degrades), [[product-designer]] (strategic not downstream; *"a good product designer is doing maybe more than a dozen prototypes in a week"*, most thrown away), [[engineers]] (**the tech lead's ~20–25% discovery time** — an explicit number, rare in the canon; "your day job is delivery, you have a side hustle on discovery"), [[product-leadership]] (the two halves: providing [[strategic-context]] and **coaching**), [[the-product-team-trio]]
- concepts: [[team-topology]] (**five ways to cut teams** — technology layer · actor in the ecosystem · journey stage · vertical industry · go-to-market motion; Jones's stated dislike of organising a team around a single KPI, and growth teams specifically, because they cut across others; the tell-tales of a wrong topology are **drowning in dependencies** and charters so thin nobody owns anything), [[outcome-based-roadmap]] (**"candidate"** as the word for a solution under consideration on a roadmap — a de-escalation device for stakeholders who need to see solutions), [[sense-of-ownership]], [[strategic-context]]
- frameworks: [[coaching-the-pm]] (**Jones's three-step recipe for coaching ownership**: is this person capable of it at all? · relentless one-on-ones restating that the decision is theirs · send them to do the homework), [[high-integrity-commitments]], [[pilot-teams]] (go deep with one group, not shallow across many)
- principles: [[learning-over-failure]] (two outputs from every build: the product *and* the learnings), [[outcomes-over-output]]
- entities: [[chris-jones]], [[christian-idiodi]]

## Notes for the coach
- **"If you know, you will care."** Jones credits this to [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and calls it the most useful thing he's picked up from a partner: don't try to conjure caring in a PM: send them to meet twenty or thirty customers, and the opinions — and the ownership — follow. Jones repeats it twice in this interview and again in [[2024-04-05-jones-product-power-play-pmdojo|the PMDojo conversation]]; Idiodi's own version is in [[2023-12-21-idiodi-essence-of-product-management-lennys]].
- **Jones's own coaching story** (the "Bob" story): when he hired his first PM, his manager told him two things — Bob must know more about his part of the product than anyone in the company *including you*, and Bob must have a public win within 30–45 days. Jones initially misread both as advice for Bob. They were advice for him. The canonical IC→manager transition story in this corpus; backs [[product-career-development]] and [[coaching-the-pm]].
- **Transformation caveats worth quoting:** an **overemphasis on predictability** is a poor fit for the model, full stop; a transformation carried by one charismatic champion is fragile if that person leaves before critical mass; corporate IT that mostly integrates and configures vendor tools can adopt *parts* of the model but not the whole (consistent with [[2024-07-09-cagan-the-product-model-in-traditional-it]]); and wartime energy that fades can let a company slide back.
- **On roadmaps, honestly:** *"we can't just say all road maps are bad and blow them up and dates don't matter anymore because we're agile — that's crazy."* Roadmaps do real jobs (sequencing, predictability for others); the problem is that they encode solutions. Pairs with [[2015-09-07-cagan-the-alternative-to-roadmaps]].
