# Product Therapy Ep 38 — Coaching Transformation Anti-Patterns (with Marcus Castenfors)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[marcus-castenfors|Marcus Castenfors]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 38 · SVPG, March 19, 2026 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jHUPl-PzD2I). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the wiki's canonical treatment of transformation *anti-patterns* as a named catalog.** Introduces [[marcus-castenfors|Castenfors]] (external product coach, author of *10 Anti-Patterns When Moving to the Product Model*) as a wiki-primary voice on transformation execution. Complements Ep 37 ([[2026-02-19-idiodi-hickman-coaching-transformations|Coaching Transformations]]) — where Hickman describes the messy middle from her practice, Castenfors provides the *named* anti-pattern vocabulary.

Key material:

(a) **Central premise: anti-patterns are the reason transformations fail.** Not intentions, tools, or ambition — it's the recognizable patterns that leaders and coaches walk into every time.

(b) **The loop of transformation failure (Castenfors's named loop).**
1. *Let's give teams the opportunity to innovate.*
2. *Patience is a virtue.*
3. *Are we there yet?*
4. *Houston, we have a problem — no results.*
5. *Abort mission — back to micromanagement.*
6. *Oh crap, micromanagement stifles innovation.*
Root cause of the loop: the *coaches failed to teach the teams the new capabilities;* the *leaders stepped too far away from the teams;* the *teams failed to be transparent about what they're working on and results.*

(c) **Losing momentum — the anti-pattern that stalks every transformation.** *"You have doubters and you have believers. Doubters are lurking, wanting to see if this is actually going to work. The best thing you can do is show results — results that this new way of working is actually better than the old way."*

(d) **Winning doubters — meet them where they are.** *"They need to understand what's in it for them. If a doubter is from sales and marketing, you need to show them you're going to increase sales and it's going to be easier to market the product in this new way of working."* Transformation-in-a-bubble is one of Castenfors's named traps.

(e) **Define the big *why*.** *"Why are you transforming? The purpose, the objectives, the vision of what that place is like that's better than the status quo. You can sense in some transformations there is lack of energy — as a coach or CPO/CTO driving change, you need to increase that energy."* Naivete of *"we're just going to use OKRs"* is another named trap — you can't use them in isolation.

(f) **Autonomy too soon = chaos.** Castenfors: *"'We're empowered, we can do whatever we want.' What happens? Chaos. You're empowered, given problems to solve, accountable to outcomes — that's new. But what if you don't have the competency? You need to be coached, mentored, given the opportunity to develop those skills."* The *loop* is what plays out when this step is skipped.

(g) **Idiodi's true test of empowerment — a coherent set of preconditions.**
1. Team has the right level and range of skills.
2. Team is given a problem (not a feature to build).
3. Team is measured on solving that problem.
4. Autonomy = leaders give context and the tools to solve, then get out of the way.
5. Accountability comes with autonomy — not "we're empowered so we work on anything."

(h) **The air sandwich — Castenfors's named concept.** *"Picture a sandwich filled with air. At the top: clear vision and direction from leadership. At the bottom: clarity on what we do day-to-day — what's in our backlog for the next sprint. In the middle: a big question mark. That's the air."* The product model is the way to *connect* the dots between the bigger picture and the smaller picture — teams need to understand how their problem-to-solve connects to something bigger.

(i) **17 North Stars problem.** *"I heard from one organization that they had 17 North Stars. How can you make a decision on what's most important when you have 17 North Stars? It is not a North Star at that point."* → Simple, storytelling-worthy strategic context; winning the hearts and minds of teams.

(j) **Don't transform product-and-tech in isolation from the business.** *"Product is what you sell, what you market, what you service. If you forget about what's surrounding product and tech, you're going to fail."* Castenfors's own case: a company trained many teams, created new strategic context — but who wasn't part of the equation? Sales, marketing, customer service. *"'Where's my road map? What happened to my feature?' — you're changing how you work without realizing the impact on many parts of the organization. HR is involved — you hire differently. Legal, finance, sales are involved — you sell differently, drive value differently, negotiate differently."*

(k) **Building trust across the org — the sidelines rule.** Castenfors: *"In big organizations you're quite siloed. You meet in leadership meetings that can be really awkward. It's really about building relationships on the sidelines — as simple as having lunch or being in one-on-one meetings — understanding their needs, co-creating and collaborating."*

(l) **Understanding the three buckets of product work.** Castenfors's frame — teams do three kinds of work; all must be visible to reduce mismatch:
1. **New value** — tackling new customer segments or a new problem to solve.
2. **Existing value** — improving the existing feature set.
3. **Protect value** — keep the lights on: compliance, tech debt, bugs, operational things.
Pilot teams need the opportunity to create new or improve existing value. If they're swamped with protect-value work below the surface, expectation mismatch is guaranteed. And on the org's roadmaps, surface the protect-value work — quantify tech debt (what's the cost? how is it affecting time-to-value?).

(m) **Idiodi's first rule of tech debt: *talk about tech debt.*** *"Value protection is easy for a business to do when they grow — they lose the muscle of value creation. But it's not a competition. You don't debate should we watch Netflix or should we pay our power bill — you understand the cost of living in your house and you have discretionary spend on top."*

(n) **Dependencies as *root of all evil.*** Castenfors: *"A team all excited about a prototype from discovery, tested on customers — then they go into delivery, start coding, and 'oh crap, we're dependent on this system and this team and this and that.'"* Causes slowness like tar. Delivery (changing how you build), discovery (changing how you solve), and strategy (which problems you solve) are all three fundamental to a transformation — you can't neglect delivery.

(o) **The vehicle-for-your-vision concept.** Do you have the *car* to reach the destination? Team topology, capability, and delivery infrastructure are all part of that vehicle.

(p) **The transformation valley of despair.** The middle of the arc where results haven't landed and the doubters are gaining. The counters: **discovery demos** (build momentum by showing what teams learned even without shipping); **leaders moving away from their comfort zone** (executives can't hide in their old habits).

(q) **The golden rule of transformations** — closing frame — meet-them-where-they-are + show results + name the anti-patterns as they appear so people don't slip into them unconsciously.
