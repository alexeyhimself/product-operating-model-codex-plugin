# Product Therapy Ep 2 — Coaching Mindset: The Nature of Product Work

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 2 · SVPG, April 15, 2024 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XnojsZrhk5M). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary.** Frames the *mindset* of an empowered product team through Steve Jobs's 1995 "Lost Interview" clip: *"the disease of thinking that a really great idea is 90% of the work and then if you just tell all these other people, they can go off and make it happen."* Cagan's counter: the idea itself is 10%; the real work — coming up with a solution that customers will buy, that we can build, and that works for our business — is the other 90%. Also introduces the two canonical Steve-Jobs frames the wiki reuses: (a) *the rock tumbler* — polished stones as a metaphor for the friction of cross-functional collaboration; (b) *why outsourced engineering doesn't work* — you burn money at twice the team size, and lose the innovation that comes from engineers seeing what's "just now possible." Closes with the **PRFAQ / anticipate-the-objections** coaching move for stakeholder engagement, credited to Amazon.

## Notes for the coach
Idiodi's practical coaching arc: *don't fight your executives* when they hand you a solution — say *"that's a great idea, help me define success"* and turn the ask into an outcome. On disagreements, Cagan's escalation ladder: (1) defer to expertise (engineer for tech, designer for UX, PM for business viability); (2) run a test; (3) escalate only as a last resort, and always with a recommendation. Attribute the 10%/90% + the rock-tumbler + the outsourced-engineering critique to Cagan; attribute the "invoke care via a prototype, not a PowerPoint" and the closing coaching tips to Idiodi.
