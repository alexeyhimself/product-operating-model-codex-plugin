# Why Traditional Messaging Fails

> **Citation:** [[martina-lauchengco|Martina Lauchengco]] · *Why Traditional Messaging Fails* · SVPG, Feb 28, 2011 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/why-traditional-messaging-fails/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local. Predates [[loved|LOVED]] Chs 22–25 (2022) by 11 years.

## Role
**Primary — Lauchengco's earliest SVPG article on messaging**, describing why the Microsoft Office-era approach (hundreds of thousands on positioning research; store-shelf longevity) fails in the modern world where "the first place people hear about your product is from someone someplace beyond your control."

Key material:
- **The historical shift.** Store shelves gave messaging longevity — get it right, enforce consistency. Today (2011): unless you have an eight-figure marketing budget, first exposure happens beyond your control.
- **Messaging's new job.** Engage at least as much as inform. Concrete, bite-sized, easily conveyed by others. Facts, stories, quotes, ratings, explanations — all essential.
- **The three common problems:**
  1. **"We're new so people care"** — technologist bias; people care about "I love it. It solved my problems. It was great." not features.
  2. **Not tailoring messaging to customer lifecycle** — acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue each have different messaging needs.
  3. **Not adapting to audience and adoption-curve** — early adopters need different messaging than mainstream; consistency belongs to brand and positioning, *messaging* must adapt.
