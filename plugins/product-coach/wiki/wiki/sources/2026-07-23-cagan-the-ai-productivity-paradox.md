# Cagan — The AI Productivity Paradox

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The AI Productivity Paradox" · svpg.com, 2026-07-23 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — names and explains the "AI productivity paradox"**: teams visibly ship faster with AI, yet organization-wide outcomes don't improve. Cagan's argument is that this isn't actually a paradox — it's the [[product-operating-model|product model]] vs. [[product-management-theater|project model]] distinction playing out at industry scale, and it's evidence the [[product-operating-model|product model]] question is now empirically, not just philosophically, decided. **Does not address team headcount or topology** — see the note on [[team-topology]] below.

## What it backs
- principles: [[outcomes-over-output]] (a live, industry-scale demonstration of the principle's stakes: AI makes it cheap to generate *more output* without generating more *outcome* — the gap between product-model and project-model companies is widening, not closing)
- concepts: [[build-to-learn-vs-build-to-earn]] (light supporting touch — reinforces that strong teams apply AI differently to build-to-learn vs. build-to-earn work; full re-rooting of that page onto its own root articles is Batch 3's job, not this one), [[ai-and-product-teams]] (a Jul-2026 checkpoint on the industry-wide adoption thesis)

## Notes for the coach
- **The paradox, in the industry's own words.** McKinsey Quarterly: *"The business world is grappling with an AI paradox: Adoption of generative and agentic AI is growing, investment is accelerating, but sustained impact on performance is elusive."* Atlassian's State of Teams 2026: *"89% of executives say AI has increased the speed of work, but only 6% feel confident they can point to specific organization-wide AI ROI."*
- **Cagan's read: not a paradox, a predictable result.** *"We continue to see most people utilizing AI to simply speed up their old, project model way of working."* The project model's core flaw was never speed — it's that it optimizes for *output*, not [[outcomes-over-output|outcomes]]. AI removes friction from output generation, which just makes the underlying misalignment more visible, faster.
- **Two quotes worth having on hand for coaching.** AI product leader Hilary Gridley: *"It's never been faster to build, which means it's never been easier to run 10 times faster in the wrong direction."* Chip Huyen (author, *AI Engineering*): *"AI makes building easier, but the hardest part remains knowing what to build."*
- **Cagan's own reversal of expectation.** He initially hoped GenAI would be "the great equalizer" — that companies without top engineering talent would close the gap with the leaders. The opposite happened: the companies with the best engineers usually also had the best *product* people, and the real advantage was never delivery speed — it was culture, strategy, and discovery skill. The [[product-operating-model|product model]] is what lets strong companies convert AI-driven speed into outcomes; without it, speed just compounds waste faster.
- **Ties to build-to-learn / build-to-earn.** Strong teams use AI differently depending on which mode they're in — accelerating *discovery* (build-to-learn: testing solutions against value/viability with real users) versus accelerating *delivery* (build-to-earn: production-quality build-out) — rather than using AI to accelerate old-project-model artifacts (business cases, roadmaps, PRDs, code-for-its-own-sake). See [[build-to-learn-vs-build-to-earn]]; the deeper root articles for that page (Prototypes vs Products, Build to Learn FAQ) are Batch 3, not this ingest.
- **What this does NOT resolve.** [[team-topology]]'s open question — whether AI-driven capacity gains get spent on *more* (pursuing the vision faster) or on *the same output with fewer people* — is about headcount and organizational choice. This article is about outcome quality, a different axis entirely: a company could resolve the paradox (get real outcomes from AI) while still choosing either headcount path, or neither. Do not cite this article as resolving the team-topology question.
- **The one line that reads as encouragement, not warning.** *"For those who embrace the different purposes, tools and techniques of build to learn versus build to earn, there has never been a better time to be creating products powered by technology."*
