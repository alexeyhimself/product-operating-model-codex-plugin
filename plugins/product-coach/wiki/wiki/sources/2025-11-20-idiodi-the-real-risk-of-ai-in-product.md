# Idiodi — The Real Risk of AI in Product (The Priceless Conversations)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] with Dr. Satyavati Kharde and Amit Godbole · *The Priceless Conversations* podcast · YouTube, published November 20, 2025 — [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rRwBi2dgzhc). Slim provenance card; the transcript is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — Idiodi's fullest non-*Product Therapy* statement, and the wiki's root for two frameworks that appear nowhere else in the corpus:** the **four buckets of product work** with its disruption-elevation law, and the **chef/grandmother metaphor** for how product sense is actually transmitted. Also the earliest wiki-attested statement of *"business acumen will be the new technical skill"*, which [[2026-07-22-cagan-olsen-how-ai-impacts-product-management|Cagan later credits to him by name]].

Key material:

(a) **The four buckets of product work, and the disruption-elevation law — the framework to cite.** *"Developing products has always been these big four buckets since the history of time: one is **deciding what to do**, whether you call that strategy. The other is **how you do it**, with discovery. Then there's **delivering it** or building it. And then **getting it to the hands of your customers**."* The law: *"**Typically when a big disruptive technology affects one of those buckets, it elevates the importance of the other buckets.**"* Worked through history — the internet disrupted bucket 4 (distribution), which elevated build-well and decide-well. **AI disrupts bucket 3 (delivery)**, so: *"strategy and discovery will take center stage even more than in our past, because building and delivering things will become easier."*

(b) **"Why did you stop at 20?"** *"Someone was boasting to me: 'I asked GPT to give me 20 ideas for my product and it gave me 20 good ideas.' I said, why did you stop at 20? Why don't you ask it for 2,000 ideas? It will give you 2,000 ideas. Now you can really see that your work is not the ideation, but **discerning which of those ideas is good for our customers and our business right now**."*

(c) **Two distinct AI questions, kept separate.** How AI affects *how we build products* vs how we build *intelligent products*. On the second: *"it's still very nascent — we are at the very early stages."* On risk: *"the risks we are seeing are the same, some heightened more than others — particularly viability risk, like ethical risk."* And on judgment under probabilistic systems: *"before, some things are binary — I do this, I expect this to happen. Now you do this and there are many options. So **judgment is not just a function of logic; it's grounded in empathy and consequence**."*

(d) **"AI can generate parts but it cannot assign a purpose."** *"The purpose is assigned by humans. One of my biggest fears around generative AI is that it becomes a substitute for deep thinking."*

(e) **Context IQ over Tech IQ.** Asked what kind of intelligence product people now need: *"I don't think it's about Tech IQ. I think it's really about **context IQ** — organizational intelligence, emotional intelligence, knowing how to influence, how to align teams and align work, how to navigate complexity, the politics of an organization."* And *"EQ over IQ"* as his standing preference; see [[emotional-intelligence]].

(f) **When everyone can write a world-class business case, politics decides.** *"Companies used to hire those big consulting firms to write a good compelling business case. Imagine everybody can do it… now the politics of what to do is very important. Imagine you're a CFO thinking which project should I fund — every business case is world-class, high quality, with good data."*

(g) **"Business acumen will probably be the new technical skill."** Stated here (Nov 2025) in the context of roadmaps becoming less predictable: *"am I going to predict three years from now, with an ever-evolving wall of AI, what we're going to build? Most of our ideas are not going to work, even more so than ever. So discovery is going to take center stage."*

(h) **What AI does and does not supply to a PM.** *"A deep knowledge of the customer, the industry, the business, the data, the product — you can co-pilot all of that. You kind of have your baked-in industry expert, your baked-in product analyst. But do you know what is good for a human within this context and environment right now? Do you understand the nuances and the crazy of our business and who is approving my paycheck and the drama? Those inferences are hard to manage."*

(i) **What he hopes AI buys engineers: cognitive load, autonomy, ownership.** *"I'm hoping we're trading that off for better thinking, not for laziness… I'm hoping autonomy increases because the dependencies are going to reduce. I don't need to work with 50 teams that own different components."* Note the hedge — he frames it as a hope, not an observation. See [[team-topology]].

(j) **The chef / grandmother metaphor for product sense — his own favourite, and an apprenticeship argument.** *"My mom is in the kitchen. She doesn't use any recipe book. There is no step one, there's no process, no framework, no guide. She has a sense of: these are the ingredients, this is the timing, I taste the soup, it needs a little more of this. This is what product sense is. **This is beyond the recipe.**"* And the crucial second half — the transmission mechanism: *"many people try to make it feel like it cannot be learned, it's a magical gift. But you have so many grandmothers that are so good. **Most people learn from their parents** — you're developing your cooking sense right now from your mom because you're spending time with them in the kitchen, looking at all their tricks. So most product sense comes from the manager, should be coached, from getting out of the building."*

(k) **"Businesses don't care about people. Only people care about people."** *"My business is defined by all of the people that work within this company that are protecting our revenue, our brand, our reputation."* The stakeholder-as-asset-protector argument, in Idiodi's voice.

(l) **Frameworks as packaged intuition — and why packaging them fails.** *"The best definition I ever heard about a framework was: you're trying to **package the intuition of another** to accelerate better outcomes. And many people fail to do that because they focus on the packaging and the process rather than on the intuition."* Compare [[2025-11-25-idiodi-doshi-coaching-product-leadership-part-2|Doshi on Ep 33]] (recorded five days later) — same concept, arrived at independently on the record.

(m) **The scaling fork: more people like Amit, not more process.** *"They say 'Amit, you did a great job solving that problem — write down that process you used so we can make everybody do it exactly.' What the best companies do is say: the best thing we can do is create thousands of people like Amit. That feels like a harder way, but it's the best way."* And: *"if you want better engineering, don't work on better engineering process or frameworks — have better engineers."* See [[process-people]].

(n) **"There's no amount of agile or AI that can fix a trust deficit."** *"If I don't trust you, I don't care what methodology or framework you put in."* See [[trust-over-control]], [[cultural-debt]].

(o) **Why leaders fail: most have never experienced good leadership.** *"One of my number-one reasons: most leaders have not experienced good leadership themselves. They are often giving people what has been given to them. My last manager screamed at me — so guess what I do?"* Compounded by a taboo: *"one of the biggest flaws in a corporate environment is that the second you become a manager or a leader, there's this false belief that you can no longer say 'I don't know. I'm not sure. I need help. I made a mistake.'"*

(p) **The consultancy mechanism, explained psychologically.** *"Why can't a leader do strategy? Because they've never done it before. And then people are pressuring them for strategy. You know what they go do? They hire one of those big companies. Because if the strategy is good, 'see, I was smart enough, I brought in the company.' If it's bad, you can blame them. **But what has never happened is that our leader has never gotten good at doing strategy themselves. They've gotten good at hiring a company to do strategy for them.**"* This is the motive behind the hire-a-consultancy anti-pattern the wiki already carries from [[2022-04-13-cagan-common-transformation-pitfalls-producttank-oslo|Oslo]].

(q) **Three leadership modes.** Command-and-control · hands-off delegation (*"do whatever you want and give me your status"*) · **leaning in by context** (*"I'm going to give you as much information to make a good decision, I'm going to empower you, I'm going to create an environment for you to do good work"*). *"Not a lot of leaders have experienced this themselves — 30 years in a company, never experienced a good leader."*

(r) **"Practice is not just about building new muscle. It's about knowing what good feels like."** And the diagnostic that follows: *"when I practice coaching with somebody and they say 'this felt very safe' — so if somebody's not feeling the way you felt right now, **they are not getting coaching**."*

(s) **The non-dominant-hand exercise — a concrete executive-coaching technique.** He has leaders write with their non-dominant hand on paper. *"Doing the right thing means having the courage or willingness to **do it poorly at first**… you're literally saying, we're shifting our culture from writing with this hand to writing with this hand. It's going to be ugly. You're going to learn new muscles. You have to practice."* Paired with the athlete framing: *"I'm not teaching you how to be an athlete — you're the best basketball player in the world, but we're playing football. Different rules, different strategies."*

(t) **"Your products are the people under your care."** His answer to what artifact he'd leave the next generation: *"when you become a leader, a manager, your products are no longer the things you build. Your products are the people under your care. And the same discipline we have for building products — discovery, experimentation, testing, mindset, getting it better — imagine if we could do that for people."* Consistent with his standing answer on what he counts as his best work: not the 200+ products, but the people he coached.

(u) **Africa — the three waves of ecosystem development.** (1) **Talent drain** — smart people leave for North America or Europe. (2) **Hubs** — companies build institutions in the market, *"but they are still building for the world from India"*. (3) **Local builders solving local problems** — *"we can start to solve our own problems in our market for ourselves."* SVPG's Innovate Africa Foundation exists to **accelerate wave 3**, on three levers: **community, capital, coaching**. *"It's not for lack of smart people… the ecosystems don't have enabling technology or access. In our world, our biggest leverage we can bring is knowledge. There's not a Silicon Valley of Africa"* — no local chain of exits to fund the coaching of the next generation. Plus the leapfrog thesis: *"the good thing about early environments is you have the opportunity to start with best practices — can we skip all the learnings of failed agile?"*

## Notes for the coach
- **The four buckets are the most useful thing here.** Use the disruption-elevation law when a leader asks "so what does AI change?" — it answers structurally rather than by listing tools, and it generalises to the next disruption. Attribute to Idiodi.
- Attribute the **chef/grandmother metaphor** to Idiodi, and always include the apprenticeship half — the metaphor's point is that product sense is *transmitted by proximity*, which is the argument for coaching, not a claim that it's innate.
- Attribute **"context IQ"** and **"businesses don't care about people; only people care about people"** to Idiodi.
- The **consultancy mechanism** (p) is the piece worth carrying into leadership coaching: the anti-pattern is not laziness, it is a leader protecting themselves from being seen not to know. Pair it with (o) and the fix is obvious — make it safe for a leader to be a beginner, which is what (s) is for.
- Use the **non-dominant-hand exercise** with executives who are resisting a transformation on ego grounds. It reframes the ask from *"you don't know this"* to *"we are all about to be clumsy for a while."*
- (l) overlaps with Doshi's *frameworks as packaged intuition* five days later — record as convergence, not as either one borrowing from the other.
