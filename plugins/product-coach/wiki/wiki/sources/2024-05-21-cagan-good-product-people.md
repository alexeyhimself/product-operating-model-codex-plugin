# Good Product People

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · SVPG article · May 21, 2024 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/good-product-people/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary.** Prompted by a Shreyas Doshi LinkedIn observation that the best product people mostly aren't active on social media (they're busy practicing the craft) — Cagan extends the point: **many of the best product people are not actually product managers or product leaders by title; a disproportionate number are engineers.** His account: he does not think it's a coincidence, because the best engineers don't limit their thinking to feasibility and enabling technology — they invest real time understanding value, viability, and usability too, precisely because they see technology as *the means* to solve tough problems, not the point of the job.

Illustrates with a named example: a podcast between **Mike McCue** (Flipboard) and **Molly White**, neither of whom would call themselves "product people," discussing the future of the internet — Cagan points to it not for its content but as a demonstration of what real product sense sounds like from two people whose formal title is "engineer."

Defines a strong product person, independent of title, as someone "obsessed with solving tough problems in ways that are loved by their users and customers, yet can sustain a business, and are just now possible" — directly echoing the value/usability/viability/feasibility frame of [[the-four-big-risks]].

Also repeats Cagan's warning (from elsewhere) against social media as a primary source of product education — "increasingly like finding the needle in the haystack."
