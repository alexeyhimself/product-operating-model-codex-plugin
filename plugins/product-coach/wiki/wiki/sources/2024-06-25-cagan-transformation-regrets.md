# Cagan — Transformation Regrets

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Transformation Regrets" · svpg.com, 2024-06-25 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/transformation-regrets/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon)** — Cagan's personal, first-person account of his own coaching mistakes over 20+ years of transformation work, prompted by a reader's question about whether every transformation goes as well as the case studies in *TRANSFORMED*. Distinct in voice from the rest of the transformation canon: this is regret, not prescription.

- concepts: [[product-coaching]] (new "coaching regrets" subsection — a coach's own failure modes, as distinct from an org's transformation failure modes)

## Notes for the coach
- **Five named regrets, verbatim-adjacent:** (1) *Not being tough enough* — hoping serious problems weren't that big of a deal, for lack of the experience/evidence to know otherwise. (2) *Being too tough* — overwhelming a product leader once armed with pattern-recognition, without giving them time to process what transformation really requires. (3) *Agreeing to just provide PM training* — "product managers don't create products; product teams do" — training PMs alone, without the org's designers/engineers/leaders also changing, "just isn't enough." (4) *When the company is not willing to make tough calls* — regret at not calling out avoidance in the moment, even though the decision is ultimately the company's to make. (5) *When the product leader is the real problem* — the hardest case: coaching someone who may not want to be coached, where the leader doesn't believe the changes apply to them.
- **Key line for a coach assessing PM-training-only requests:** *"we've learned that this just isn't enough to cause the outcome they want."* Useful verbatim when a company frames its ask as "just get our PMs some training."
- **When to cite:** this is the article to reach for when a coach (human or model) is calibrating how hard to push, or diagnosing why "just train the PMs" requests under-deliver — it's Cagan's own admission of getting both ends of that calibration wrong at different points in his career.
