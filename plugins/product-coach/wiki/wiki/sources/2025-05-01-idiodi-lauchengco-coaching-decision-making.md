# Product Therapy Ep 19 — Coaching Decision Making

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[martina-lauchengco|Martina Lauchengco]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 19 · SVPG, May 1, 2025 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JU-DReKwZBs). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the wiki's canonical teaching on team- and PM-level decision-making** (distinct from the executive-partnering "Decisions" element on [[partnering-with-executives]]). Grounds the new [[decision-making]] framework page.

Key material:

(a) **A decision's value is relative to the point in time it was made** — Lauchengco brings her org-behavior *garbage-can theory* frame (problems + solutions matching as a function of time, not precise calculation). Empowered teams get afraid of making empowered decisions; the reassurance = *"you're empowered to make the most reasonable decision at that point in time relative to the information you have. Some will look bad in retrospect. That does not mean you used the wrong framework."*

(b) **High-integrity decisions** as SVPG's standard: reasonable, data-informed, rational, defensible — regardless of the outcome. The team + leaders can *respect the rationale even when they disagree*.

(c) **Context: is the decision or the how more important?** Startup = making a decision (any decision) beats being perfect. Big org = HOW you make it is everything, because *"if it's politically unsupported, it doesn't matter if it was the best decision in the world."* Lauchengco's Andreessen-Horowitz story: *"I don't like it, what else you got?"* — Horowitz just said yes. Founder rejection = doesn't matter what the data said.

(d) **Collaboration ≠ consensus.** Amazon's "disagree and commit" restated. Idiodi's rule: it's not about pleasing the majority (voting), not about one dictator (trumping vote), but about *team-sport deference to expertise* on the relevant axis.

(e) **Data-informed vs data-driven — the crucial distinction.** Data reflects the *past*; product decisions impact the *future*. Data should not make the decision; a human should. AI flattens to averages; the *exceptions* are where the signal is (*"the 2s tell you what's broken; the 10s just pat you on the back — we as decision-makers have to know the 2s matter more"*).

(f) **Gut feel is informed intuition.** Trust it when it's built on years of data + product sense + business sense — that's [[founder-style-leadership]]. Lauchengco's own bias: for early-stage decisions she trusts *market-context* intuition more than product-centric intuition (per [[2025-03-06-idiodi-lauchengco-coaching-startups|Ep 16]] on market-product fit).

(g) **Amazon's one-way vs two-way doors** as reversibility framing. **Idiodi's coaching test questions** for evaluating a decision: (i) good for your customer? (ii) good for your business? (iii) goes against morals/values/integrity? (iv) solving a real problem? **Idiodi's crispest single question:** *"what must be true for this decision to succeed?"* — beats "what has worked somewhere else."

(h) **Cagan on relative-prioritization** (Lauchengco cites): no absolute; goals are relatively weighted; the revenue-vs-retention example — companies chase revenue when *retained customers ARE revenue*, and the whole downstream cascade of decisions changes.

(i) **Senior-leader blind spots.** *"The more senior you are, the more your blind spots have impact and the less visible they are to you."* Get a coach; you're already underutilising them. Ex-CEO's biography curriculum for Idiodi (Lincoln, Washington) — *see what courageous decision-making looks like when you have the full context*. Am I making the easier obvious choice here? = the first check.

(j) **"You're not just making decisions, you're creating an environment for good decision-making"** — Idiodi's frame for leaders. Elevation: senior leaders schedule time every week to make sure they're operating at the right elevation, not just executing.

(k) **AI in decision-making** — LLMs are *research assistants in your pocket* (data-gathering, summarizing, analyzing). Never delegate the decision. **Agentic AI** — micro-decisions inside a proven strategy (which segment gets which campaign at scale) are delegatable. Strategic decisions (positioning, market direction 6 months out) are never delegatable. *"The agent's not going to have any clue on whether that is right."*

(l) **Prioritization vs decision-making** — good strategy provides guardrails that make prioritization implicit. *"Strategy should be broad enough that I can decide whether to accept this meeting invitation from Christian relative to my priorities."* Bad prioritization frameworks (RICE, ICE by themselves) are often an excuse for not making the strategy call.
