# Product Therapy Ep 17 — Coaching Product Myths (Part 1)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[chris-jones|Chris Jones]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 17 · SVPG, April 3, 2025 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ICpaylkLH1k). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the first half of Jones's canonical "misconceptions in product" pack.** Four myths crisply named + the correction. Read together with [[2025-06-26-idiodi-jones-coaching-product-myths-part-2|Part 2 (Ep 23)]].

**Myth 1 — "Product decides the *what*; engineering decides the *how*."** Correction: the best ideas come from engineers who *know what's possible right now* — the "what" is a shared team output. RACI/DACI matrices actively harm this; they force everyone to retreat to their corners. In the model, disagreements largely disappear because the team collectively knows it owns the solution. Jones's own admission: as a young PM he took full responsibility for the "what," and later realized *"nearly all the best ideas came from engineers on the team — not from me, not from my bosses, not from sales, not from customers."*

**Myth 2 — "Design is downstream / just makes things pretty."** Correction: design is a discovery role. A good designer produces **~two dozen prototypes per week**, most thrown away; the ~10% of design work that is delivery-asset production is spiky. Cagan's line Jones cites: *"giving a designer wireframes is like giving an engineer pseudocode."* Idiodi's line: *"in the best companies, I often cannot even differentiate between a product manager and a designer."* Steve Jobs's *"design is not what it looks like — design is how it works"* remains the ur-quote.

**Myth 3 — "Product managers should be hired for domain expertise."** Correction: **domain expertise = domain knowledge − domain dogma** (Shreyas Doshi, cited by Jones). Adding domain to a strong PM (~3 months) is easier than stripping dogma from a domain expert (~near-impossible). Jones's own information-security-startup story: only 1 of 3 co-founders had security background; the outsider frame produced both a different technology approach and a different go-to-market (higher ASP, different buying centre). Not naivety — the PM must aggressively acquire domain knowledge on the job — but the starting point matters. Idiodi's crisp rule: *"a good PM takes ~3 years to develop; domain knowledge takes ~3 months if you focus — pick the deeper craft."*

**Myth 4 — "Our job is to give the customer what they ask for."** Correction: customers don't know what they want because they don't know what's *possible* with technology. Bezos in both directions: *"the biggest mistake you can make is listening to your customers … the second biggest mistake is not listening to your customers."* The Walkman-color focus-group anecdote (kids picked bright colors verbally, all took the black one home) illustrates the say-do gap. Managing feature requests / "**specials**": when a sales rep says *"we can't close this deal without X,"* Idiodi's response = *"great, I want to make sure we succeed when we do it — what problem does this solve, and how will the customer know?"* Sometimes you build it anyway (business outcome = winning the deal); Jones's rule: **treat it as a "special"** — high escalation bar, quota'd, everyone understands it's not a good long-term pattern, or the product becomes a Frankenstein that closes deals but doesn't retain.
