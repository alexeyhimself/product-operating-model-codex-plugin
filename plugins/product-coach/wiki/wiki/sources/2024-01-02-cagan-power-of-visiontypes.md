# Cagan — The Power of Visiontypes

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The Power of Visiontypes" · svpg.com, 2024-01-02 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-power-of-visiontypes/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** Cagan's dedicated treatment of the *visiontype* — the prototype-form artifact used to communicate a product vision — prompted by a DesignMap webinar and a conversation in which someone argued Apple "didn't know how to innovate" because Steve Jobs "just copied" the Xerox PARC GUI demo. Cagan uses the story to make a sharper point: a compelling visiontype can *inspire someone else's* product vision, but the number of true innovations required to convert that vision into a shipped product is enormous — and it's whoever actually **discovers and delivers** the product, not whoever demos the prototype, who wins. ## Notes for the coach
- **The PARC/Apple story, correctly told.** The Xerox Alto GUI demo (1979) inspired Steve Jobs's product vision for the desktop computer — it did not hand him a finished product. "Anyone that thinks someone can see a demo of a prototype, and then 'just copy that' on an entirely different computer and ecosystem, into an actual viable product, has likely never tried to build a product before." PARC had the visiontype; Xerox never had the company or product leadership to productize it. Apple did.
- **Cagan's own cautionary tale.** He was an engineer at HP Labs (a short walk from PARC) during the same period; his lab's own visiontype, "the domesticated computer," bet on early AI technology that turned out not to be ready for productization — "falling in love with an enabling technology rather than the problem to solve was one of my early and painful lessons on product."
- **Most of discovery happens *after* the visiontype**, and is mostly solution discovery ([[2020-09-04-cagan-discovery-problem-vs-solution|see]]), plus delivery — the visiontype is the starting gun, not the finish line.
- **The double-edged risk of sharing a vision publicly.** Real recruiting/partnership benefits, but if you can't discover and deliver, someone else may end up delivering on your vision instead — "there is a long history of this happening in our industry."
