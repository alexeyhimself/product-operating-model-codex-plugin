# Cagan — A Fresh Definition of The Product Role

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "A Fresh Definition of The Product Role" · svpg.com, 2026-08-10 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/a-fresh-definition-of-the-product-role/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — Cagan endorsing and translating a non-product-person's implicit definition of the product role.** The source of the implicit definition is technology analyst Benedict Evans's paywalled article "Most People Aren't Tool Builders" (cited here by plain hyperlink only, consistent with the wiki's conservative treatment of non-SVPG voices pending the Batch 8 Part 4 decision — no dedicated entity page created for Evans). Also the article where Cagan tempers [[2025-05-28-cagan-the-era-of-the-product-creator|The Era of the Product Creator]]'s optimism.

## What it backs
- competencies: [[product-manager]] (Evans's three skills mapped directly onto value/viability language — a fresh, non-product-insider articulation of why the role stays essential even as building gets easier)
- concepts: [[product-discovery]] (Evans's first skill — recognizing the general problem behind a specific pain point — reinforces the wiki's existing "market discovery is the easier half; solution discovery is the hard part" framing, from outside the product-management literature)

## Notes for the coach
- **Evans's three skills, mapped to product-model language (Cagan's own mapping).** (1) Recognizing the *general problem* behind a specific pain point → [problem discovery](https://www.svpg.com/discovery-problem-vs-solution/). (2) Discovering an effective *solution* → the [value risk](https://www.svpg.com/value-and-viability/) in solution discovery — Evans, verbatim: *"People that are really good at using the tool are not the same people as those that are really good at creating the tool."* Example: *"You have to know a lot about sales to make good sales software, but being good at sales does not make you good at making sales software."* (3) Understanding your own company deeply enough that the solution also *works for the business* → the [viability risk](https://www.svpg.com/value-and-viability/) — sales, marketing, finance, compliance, legal, regulated data, legacy-system integration, workflow fit.
- **The core argument, verbatim (Evans via Cagan).** *"AI makes it easy for anyone to build tools to automate their work… except most people and most companies aren't tool-builders, don't think like that, and can't and won't do that... AI changes the thresholds but not the problem."* And: *"Writing the code isn't the hard part, and making the tool isn't the hard part — the hard part is knowing that it should exist, and knowing how it should exist, and that's a different person."*
- **The self-correction — track alongside the [[2025-05-28-cagan-the-era-of-the-product-creator|Era of the Product Creator]] card.** Cagan revisits his own May-2025 optimism: *"That's been maybe a little bit true, but not anywhere near what I had hoped for."* His diagnosis, borrowed from Evans: the limiting factor was never the tools — it's whether someone thinks like a product person at all. This is a milder, narrower correction than the Feb-2026 zero-to-one retraction (see [[model-as-product-coach#the-zero-to-one-problem-a-cagan-retraction]]) — that one was about whether newcomers *can learn fast enough with AI coaching*; this one is about whether *access to better tools alone* was ever going to widen who becomes a strong product creator. Two distinct, don't-conflate corrections to two distinct 2025 predictions.
- **Framing device.** Cagan treats "how do you define the product role?" as a career-long informal Rorschach test he's asked candidates for decades — the answer reveals the operating model someone worked in and what they think the role contributes, more than it defines a right answer.
