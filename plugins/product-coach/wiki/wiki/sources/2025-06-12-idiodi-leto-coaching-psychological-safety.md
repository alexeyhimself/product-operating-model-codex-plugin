# Product Therapy Ep 22 — Coaching Psychological Safety

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[kate-leto|Kate Leto]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 22 · SVPG, June 12, 2025 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NxRz8ell-U4). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the wiki's canonical teaching on psychological safety as a distinct discipline** from trust and from emotional intelligence. Grounds the new [[psychological-safety]] concept page. Leto is an executive coach and author of *[[hiring-product-managers|Hiring Product Managers: Using Product EQ to Go Beyond Culture and Skills]]*; she's a two-time contributor to the Product Therapy corpus (also cited on [[product-career-development]] via [[2024-10-24-idiodi-moore-coaching-career-advice|Ep 9]] for her human-skills reframe).

Key material:

(a) **Definition.** *"A shared belief amongst a group of people that it's safe to be able to express their opinions, express their ideas, maybe even make mistakes, without negative repercussions."* Not the same as "we're a family"; not the same as "comfortable." **Psychologically safe cultures are engaging, dynamic, empowering — they are not comfortable.**

(b) **The Google Project-Aristotle finding** (Idiodi restates): the single biggest factor differentiating Google's highest-performing teams was psychological safety — above tenure, PhDs, manager quality, feedback quality, pay. This is the frame that ended the "great managers make great teams" hypothesis at Google.

(c) **Trust ≠ psychological safety.** *"Trust is dyadic — it's between two people. Psychological safety is amongst a team."* You can trust every individual on your team and still not feel safe speaking up in a group meeting. One person with the loudest voice or the highest paygrade can halt the safety of the whole environment even when trust between pairs is intact.

(d) **EQ enables psychological safety; PS is the outcome.** Leto's distinction: emotional intelligence is the individual capability that makes psychological safety possible at the team level. High-EQ teams don't automatically produce PS, but low-EQ teams almost never do.

(e) **Diagnosing lack of PS.** Silence in meetings, no pushback on leader decisions, uniform politeness without candor, no new ideas surfaced by the team. Leto: *"organizations with psychological safety are more dynamic, because people are bringing in their ideas and feeling good about doing that."*

(f) **The fix starts with the leader.** Self-awareness first. Watch your body language in meetings. Watch phone usage. Watch how you handle new ideas — encouraging, curious, questioning, appreciating? Or shooting down before people finish explaining? **The single hardest and most important leadership move: *"I don't know — let's go find out."*** Modeling that makes it safe for everyone else to say it.

(g) **The hero complex** as the primary blocker. Leaders trained by an unwritten cultural rule that they must always know, always save the day, always be the strongest voice. That posture makes it impossible for the team to build its own problem-solving muscle. Leto: *"we have to work with leaders on where the desire to always be right and always save the day comes from — and how to shift focus from 'who is right' to 'what can we learn / what does the team think.'"*

(h) **Sharing ideas without creating a directive.** Present 2–3 options with pros/cons. Frame with *"I don't know if this is a great idea — I'm bringing it to you because I'd love your input; you know this better than I do."* Encourages the team to poke holes rather than execute silently.

(i) **PS + accountability coexist.** *"Just because there's psychological safety does not mean people are not accountable. PS lays the foundation for a healthy type of accountability."* The team can raise their hand halfway through and say *"I need help / this isn't what I thought it was / I can't hit the deadline"* — and that is applauded, not held against them. The old model of accountability (execute silently or bear the consequences) is a legacy pattern that PS replaces.

(j) **Can you have too much PS?** Yes — teams in the middle of calibrating what safe means for them can swing into "everyone talking, no one listening." **Retros** are the ritual that helps recalibrate (*"how are we doing on asking questions, hitting deadlines, listening to each other?"*). PS growth is never linear.

(k) **Practices teams can adopt.** Bring curiosity → ask a clarifying question *before* forming a response. Active listening as a learnable skill. Both are coaching skills that help the team be more present in workplace conversations.

(l) **Idiodi's rehearsed-call-out technique.** Pre-arrange with a team member: *"in the middle of the meeting, raise your hand and say 'that's a stupid idea.'"* Then respond well (curious, seeking their reasoning, ending in a genuine "disagree and commit"). *"What have I just modeled? It's perfectly okay to disagree and still commit."* Unless a leader models this on a real stage, no one will risk it first — the HR document and the newsletter can't do it.

(m) **Vulnerability must be authentic.** Leto: *"if you're just putting on the mask to say I don't know five times today, people are going to pick up on it."* Practice, don't perform. If you can't be vulnerable with your own leader, you won't be vulnerable with the people who report to you — it's contagious both ways.

(n) **Psychological safety is contagious top-down.** Research consistently shows: it starts with the CEO. If the top-most leader can say *"I don't know / I'm nervous about trying this too / let's find out together,"* the culture propagates down. If they can't, no amount of team-level practice will scale.

## Notes for the coach
Attribute the *"trust is dyadic; PS is amongst a group"* distinction to Leto as the crispest single phrasing on record. Attribute the *hero complex* as PS blocker to Leto. Attribute the *rehearsed-call-out technique* to Idiodi. The Google Project-Aristotle framing (Idiodi restates on-air) can be cited as *"Project Aristotle, restated on Ep 22"* — it's a public research finding widely available; the wiki does not need a separate source card for it. **When coaching a leader on PS, always start with self-inventory** (Leto's rule): the pattern is almost always driven top-down. When the org asks "can PS coexist with accountability?", the answer is on Ep 22 — yes, and PS creates a *healthier* accountability where mid-project rescoping/asking-for-help is possible.
