# Great Products Are Not Just the Product Guy's Job

> **Citation:** [[martina-lauchengco|Martina Lauchengco]] · *Great Products Are Not Just the Product Guy's Job* · SVPG, Jan 12, 2011 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/great-products-are-not-just-the-product-guys-job/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the earliest wiki-attested Lauchengco SVPG article on the PM/PMM partnership** and the case for PMM getting involved *during the product's planning*, not just at launch.

Key material:
- **Dropbox opener.** *"The product guys did an outstanding job of solving a real problem, nailing a killer UE, and focusing on the backend to make the experience as simple and seamless as possible."* — the archetype of PM-does-the-job-well but PMM must contribute *earlier*.
- **The three evangelism tests** (Lauchengco's article-form frame — PMM should ask during product planning):
  1. Is this something other people will talk about if we're not directing the conversation?
  2. Will people like this enough to tell a friend even if they're not incentivized to do so?
  3. Do we create legitimate opportunities to share value others want?
- **The "Make it just work + Make it dead simple = Product Love" equation** — Lauchengco's early codification of what makes a product spontaneously evangelizable.
- **The shared responsibility.** *"Building a great product worthy of conversation is generally thought to be the product manager's job... But if you're in product marketing, you should contribute as well."*

## Notes for the coach
When a PMM asks *"how do I contribute earlier than launch?"*, cite Lauchengco's three evangelism-tests as the framework. When a PM asks *"why do I need PMM before I'm ready to launch?"*, cite this same article — those three tests are hard to answer alone.
