# Product Therapy Ep 40 — Coaching the Future of Product and Engineering with AI (Mike Fisher)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[mike-fisher|Mike Fisher]] (CEO, MyFitnessPal; former CTO, Etsy) · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 40 · SVPG, May 21, 2026 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67p_bKrFfS8). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the wiki's canonical counter-argument to "AI means fewer people."** Complements Cagan's Ep 39 ([[2026-04-17-idiodi-cagan-coaching-in-the-age-of-ai|Coaching in the Age of AI]]) — where Cagan focuses on coaching, Fisher focuses on the *macro / economic / role-shape* implications of AI for product-and-engineering work. Introduces [[mike-fisher]] (CEO, MyFitnessPal; former CTO, Etsy) as a wiki-primary voice on AI + engineering leadership. Ep 43 [[2026-08-20-idiodi-fisher-understanding-cultural-debt|Understanding Cultural Debt]] is Fisher's second appearance.

Key material:

(a) **Jevons paradox for product.** Fisher's central argument: *"There was a 19th-century economist named William Jevons who watched coal consumption rise as steam engines became more efficient. Everyone thought efficiency would decrease coal demand — he saw the exact opposite. When things become more efficient, they create demand for more services."* His prediction: **cheaper software means we'll apply software to things we never would in the past.**

(b) **The radiology counter-example** (Fisher). *"Geoffrey Hinton in 2016 said we should stop training radiologists — AI would read all the scans. Fast forward seven years: radiologists had grown 17%. Once imaging became cheaper to read, they wanted to use it for more cases."* Same dynamic will play in product/engineering.

(c) **Single-person billion-dollar companies** — already emerging (Fisher references contemporary reporting of a solo founder running a GLP-1 online business entirely with AI agents). *"That is the magnitude of change here."*

(d) **When routine tasks get easier, the remaining human work becomes more complex.** *"AI takes away some of the coding — I do think we are heading to a place where humans will not code. But it forces us to think the level above. We've got to be really good at architecture, systems-level thinking, asking the right questions."* Programming and coding are no longer synonymous; programming (the thinking) survives, coding (the typing) doesn't.

(e) **The one-pizza rule** (Fisher, updating Bezos). *"Amazon's two-pizza rule was we're going to have product teams that can be fed with two pizzas. I just heard the one-pizza rule — with agentic programming, we probably don't need as many people. We still need the diverse skill sets — design thinking, product management, engineering thinking — but in a smaller team."* Where they come together is on the **prompts** — engineering-background + product/customer-problem person together create a great prompt for the agentic pipeline.

(f) **The flight-is-5-min-but-airport-traffic-remains** (Idiodi quoting a CPO). *"We've always traveled Chicago-to-New-York in an hour and a half. Now with AI the flight time is 5 minutes. But we're not getting rid of the traffic to the airport, the long TSA lines, the wait for the taxi."* Only about 10-15% of engineers' time is actually coding — the coordination, thinking, and meetings remain. AI is a substantial but *bounded* compression on the whole cycle.

(g) **FinOps for AI — the token bill that eats savings.** Fisher's cloud-era comparison: *"When we moved to the cloud, we got the first bill and realized it's expensive — cost control created FinOps in every SaaS. We'll have the same with AI. Tokens are very expensive. We'll have someone specialized in optimization of prompts and agentic agents. New roles will come out of this."*

(h) **Hyper-personalization as the new expectation.** *"Old-style personalization: 'based on everybody else who searched, here's the top lamp.' Now: I want you to know me. I want you to know my history and pick out a lamp for me."* Fisher's MyFitnessPal AI coach example: layer 20 years of nutrition-tracking data with the user's personal journey — *"Christian, for you today, I noticed you love bananas. How about an orange today because I noticed you need some vitamin C? And I noticed you're traveling."*

(i) **Idiodi's expectations-scale rule.** *"If personalization is easier to do, people want more personalization. If automation is easier, I want less errors. If you can fix errors faster, I want fewer errors. Standards increase for people once you start to see things you deemed magical become commonplace."*

(j) **Why product judgment matters MORE, not less.** *"When we can create 10 features in the time that used to take us one, we've got to be really good at asking 'what is the customer problem we're trying to solve? What is the outcome we're trying to achieve?' If we don't, we just create more junk faster."* This is the Cagan-and-Idiodi article Fisher references — *faster AI creating features just creates more junk if we don't do it the right way.*

(k) **Product/design/engineering blending into "builders."** *"We're all creators. We're all building."* Fisher's version of the Cagan-Ep-39 point (creators split by build-to-learn vs build-to-earn but everyone is building), specifically tuned for engineering leadership.

(l) **What good product leadership looks like as throughput explodes.** Fisher's frame: as capacity multiplies, the leader who decides *what is good* and *what we ship* becomes the highest-leverage role — same conclusion Doshi reached in Ep 33.

(m) **Why leaders can't cut headcount and declare victory** — the demand-side argument (Jevons); the *"we now do things we never would have"* rule; the *token-bill and FinOps* offset; the hyper-personalization standards; the *judgment matters more* need.

(n) **What stays stubbornly human.** *"Customers, trust, judgment."* And the strategic decisions of what to build for whom — for the foreseeable few years, that stays with humans. Doshi's Ep 33 line: *"Are you going to be that person?"* is echoed here in Fisher's leadership counsel.

(o) **Fisher's advice for people entering / already in the field.** *"Learn the fundamentals, then embrace the tools."* The fundamentals — customer sense, judgment, architecture thinking, systems thinking — are what stay. The tools change every year.
