# Discovery When Working Remotely

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Discovery When Working Remotely* · SVPG, Apr 13, 2020 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/discovery-when-working-remotely/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the root article for [[remote-collaboration]] on the *discovery* side.** Written four weeks into the pandemic, and the piece Cagan later says he wrote as a warning (see [[2021-08-25-cagan-discovery-vs-documentation]], which reports that the warning went unheeded).

- **The asymmetry that organizes the whole article.** *"In delivery, it's more of a trade-off… Overall, I find teams with remote employees can do quite well in delivery, occasionally even better than when the team is co-located. The real challenge of remote employees is when we consider the discovery work."* Remote is a **discovery** problem, not a general productivity problem.
- **The Bezos co-location quote**, which Cagan says *"sums up my experiences quite well"*: at [[amazon|Amazon]] a product team is *"cross-functional, dedicated, and co-located. Why? Creativity comes from people's interactions; inspiration comes from intensive concentration."* His gloss: *"I don't think it's an accident that Amazon is the most consistently innovative company in our industry."*
- **Mechanics barely change — dynamics do.** *"The overall methods and mechanics are not really very different when working remotely versus co-located."* Prototype, test qualitatively or quantitatively, just more video-based. What changes is *"the dynamics of how the product manager, product designer and tech lead collaborate."*

**The three problems — the article's usable core:**

| Problem | What goes wrong | Cagan's counter |
|---|---|---|
| **Artifacts** | *"A nearly gravity-like pull to start producing artifacts for each other"* — designer asks PM for a brief, tech lead asks designer for wireframes, PM asks engineers for estimates. *"Very soon, the new remote work process has reverted back to waterfall-like passing along of artifacts"* and the discussion moves from outcome to output. | Keep returning to the *"how do we solve this problem?"* discussion on video, even when it feels less efficient. *"During discovery, the main artifact should be prototypes."* |
| **Trust** | Discovery depends on [[psychological-safety\|psychological safety]]. *"When people are separated from one another… the normal filters and sensitivity can fade."* People report *"seeing a different side of their colleagues, and it's not always a good look."* | Coaching, explicitly: *"most people don't intend to be cruel or insensitive, they just don't have as many of the social cues to go on."* Handle anything sensitive over video, not text — a badly phrased Slack message that *"requires hours of damage control… maybe wasn't so efficient after all."* |
| **Time** | Uneven quality time, not uneven hours. Some thrive; others *"suddenly forced to deal with child care or homeschooling"* may struggle to find *"even an hour a day of uninterrupted time."* | Flexibility: if the designer has one solid hour at an odd time, *"if the product manager and tech lead can find a way to accommodate that, it's worth doing."* |

- **Not an anti-remote piece.** He states the talent-access advantage up front and declines to argue for or against remote work; the article is *"trying to help teams dealing with remote employees – especially with remote engineers – do better."*

## Notes for the coach
The artifacts row is the one to lead with: it describes, almost exactly, how a distributed trio silently reverts to waterfall without anyone deciding to. Because Cagan refuses the pro/anti-remote fight, this card is safe to use with a leadership team that has already committed to remote — the argument is about *what to compensate for*, not about going back to the office.
