# Cagan — The Four Big Risks

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The Four Big Risks" · svpg.com, 2017-12-05 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Supporting (SVPG explainer).** The article-length companion to the *INSPIRED* 2nd-edition (2017) reframing of the three original discovery risks — **value, usability, feasibility** — into **four**, adding **business viability** as an explicit peer. Written to accompany the new edition and to make the reframing accessible outside the book.

**Root source: [[transformed]]** — the four-big-risks canon plus the trio's risk-ownership table (PM = value + viability, designer = usability, tech lead = feasibility) live in *TRANSFORMED* Ch 10 as the current canonical form, with the earlier root in [[inspired|INSPIRED]] (Chs 6 + 8 in the 1st ed's three-risk form; the 2nd ed as the four-risk crystallization). The [[the-four-big-risks|four-big-risks concept page]] already carries the full lineage note — 2008 three risks → 2017 viability added → ethics-inside-viability — and this card does **not** re-open it.

The article itself is worth citing for three tight framings:

1. **Why viability was promoted.** "Over the years I've come to believe that [three risks] was obscuring some pretty serious risks and challenges, and making it too easy for product managers to focus on customer value and overlook business value." The article names the *practical* reason viability graduated to a peer risk.
2. **What business viability covers.** A crisp inventory: go-to-market/sales channel; contracts and legal compliance; customer acquisition cost; monetisation; brand promise. Useful when a coach needs to enumerate what "viability" actually contains beyond the label.
3. **The risk-ownership table, article-form.** The **PM** owns *value + viability* → outcomes. The **designer** owns *usability* → experience. The **product lead engineer** owns *feasibility* → delivery. This is the *TRANSFORMED* Ch 10 table stated seven years earlier.

Also carries the "beyond Lean and Agile in three respects" framing that shows up as a running Cagan riff: tackle big risks *early*, solve *collaboratively*, focus on *problems* not features/roadmaps.
