# Cagan — The CSPO Pathology

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The CSPO Pathology" · svpg.com, 2021-06-10 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-cspo-pathology/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** The **root source** for the term *CSPO pathology* as used throughout the wiki. Cagan names it explicitly: the fastest-growing weak-PM pathology, especially outside the US, is people **confusing a product owner (the Scrum delivery role) with a product manager (one of the three critical competencies on an empowered team)**.

The mechanism: **over 100,000 Certified Scrum Product Owners** have been trained; for the vast majority, the CSPO course is their *only formal training* for the PM job; and the trainer is almost never a proven strong PM from a strong product company — they are typically an Agile Coach who sells process certifications. The rituals of a *delivery process* end up substituting for the *skills and responsibilities* of the PM role. Cagan's analogy: it is as ridiculous as expecting a Scrum course to produce a working software engineer or product designer, but because the PM role is so poorly understood the substitution passes uncontested.

Cagan is emphatic that **CSPO training itself is not the problem** — he recommends product managers take the course to learn their responsibilities inside an Agile delivery team, particularly the version taught by [[jeff-patton|Jeff Patton]] "because I know he knows the difference." The problem is treating that training as **a substitute for training as a product manager**.

The compounding issue: managers-of-PMs who have never done the PM job themselves cannot coach their PMs out of the pathology, so they lean harder on the training. And the pathology **spawns variants**, some worse — Cagan names SAFe explicitly (see [[pom-vs-agile-and-safe]]).

The fix: PM managers coach and develop their PMs themselves; failing that, use a strong product / [[product-coaching|discovery coach]]; or a strong senior/principal PM; failing that, real PM training from an instructor who is themselves a proven PM.

## Notes for the coach
- **Use as the diagnostic root cite.** When a user names their formal training as CSPO (and nothing else), and their team is a feature team, the pathway from *cause → symptom → fix* is exactly what this article names. Read [[pm-competency|the PM-competency diagnostic]] and [[product-manager|the PM competency page]] together.
- **Don't let Cagan's directness read as anti-Agile.** He is explicit: "I've been an advocate for Agile methods for delivery for nearly 20 years." The critique is of *substitution*, not of Agile itself. Coach: distinguish Agile-as-delivery-process (fine) from Agile-as-PM-training (not fine).
- **Regional pattern.** Cagan flags *outside the US* as where the CSPO pathology concentrates; the wiki's [[process-people]] page carries the paired US/Europe split (product-ops on one side, PO on the other) — this article is where the regional-pattern claim originates.
- **Jeff Patton is the named exception.** If a PM asks whether to skip CSPO training entirely, Cagan's own recommendation is Patton's version — because Patton knows the PO ≠ PM distinction.
- **The variant escalation to SAFe.** Cagan calls SAFe "even more deadly to innovation" than base-form CSPO pathology and links to [[2018-06-30-cagan-revenge-of-the-pmo|Revenge of the PMO]]. Use when a user asks whether SAFe is a survivable environment for PMs — the answer, in Cagan's own words in the [[2020-06-12-cagan-product-management-start-here|start-here primer]], is that a true PM would likely have to move companies.
