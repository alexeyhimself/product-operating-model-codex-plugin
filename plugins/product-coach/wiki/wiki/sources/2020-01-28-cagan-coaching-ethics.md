# Coaching – Ethics

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Coaching – Ethics* · SVPG, Jan 28, 2020 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/coaching-ethics/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — and the closing article of SVPG's [Coaching Series](https://www.svpg.com/the-coaching-series/)** ("In this final article on the series on coaching…"). Its significance to this wiki is out of proportion to its length: it is **Cagan's own explicit, written proposal of a fifth risk**, and it is the root source of the Rob Chesnut material the wiki had previously been citing at second hand.

Key material:
- **The fifth risk, in Cagan's own words.** After naming the four risks, he writes: *"So, I have been advocating explicitly considering the ethical implications, **by adding a fifth risk: Should we build it? (ethical risk)**."*
- **Why he thought four wasn't enough.** Two stated reasons: (1) business viability already carries "so many different aspects — sales, marketing, finance, legal, compliance, privacy and more — that it's easy for ethics to get lost"; (2) *"unlike the other areas of business viability, there is rarely a stakeholder explicitly responsible for ethics."*
- **Rob Chesnut, Airbnb Chief Ethics Officer** — a long-time Cagan friend from eBay, quoted throughout. This article is the **root** of the Chesnut questions ("If the government knew everything…", "if all the emails and documents were published online, would you be embarrassed?") that [[product-ethics]] had been sourcing via *Product Therapy* Ep 5.
- **The shareholder-vs-stakeholder shift.** Chesnut: *"In the past, companies had one stakeholder — the shareholder… that's an approach that has led a lot of companies to think about everything in the short term."*
- **What to do when you spot an issue.** Raise it "not in a holier than thou nor accusatory way"; Cagan adds that you need a deep understanding of the business economics so you aren't dismissed as naive.
- **When to leave.** Cagan, who says he rarely encourages people to quit: *"when it comes to those companies that are clearly ignoring the ethical implications of their work, I have and will continue to encourage people to leave."*

## Notes for the coach
**⚠️ This card corrects a dated claim the wiki carried in two places.** [[product-ethics]] and [[the-four-big-risks]] both framed the "fifth risk" as a **non-canon, coaching-context framing** — attributed to [[jon-moore|Moore]] and [[christian-idiodi|Idiodi]] on *Product Therapy* Ep 5 (2024), and to a 2018 talk where Cagan merely "floated" ethics as separate. This article shows Cagan **advocating it in writing, as his own position, in January 2020**. The correct lineage is therefore:

**three** (INSPIRED 1st ed, 2008) → **four** (INSPIRED 2nd ed, 2017) → **Cagan explicitly advocates a fifth, ethical risk (2018 talk → this 2020 article)** → **back to four with ethics inside viability** (2021+, his current canonical position, with the "once you get to more than 4 it becomes a checklist" reasoning).

So the four-vs-five tension is **not** wiki-vs-canon, and not Moore departing from Cagan. It is **Cagan's own position changing over time**, and Moore/Idiodi's 2024 "emerging fifth risk" language is a continuation of Cagan's own earlier framing, not a divergence from it. When a coachee cites "five risks," the honest answer is that Cagan advocated exactly that from 2018–2020 and later consolidated to four for anti-checklist reasons — both are his.

**Non-SVPG boundary.** The article closes by recommending Rob Chesnut's *Intentional Integrity* (then forthcoming).
