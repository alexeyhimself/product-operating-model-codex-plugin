# From Managing Products To Managing Product Managers

> **Citation:** [[chris-jones|Chris Jones]] · *From Managing Products To Managing Product Managers* · SVPG, Jan 3, 2017 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/managing-products-managing-product-managers/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the article-form root for the "public win" onboarding discipline** that the wiki has so far carried only second-hand through [[2025-01-23-idiodi-jones-coaching-employee-onboarding|Product Therapy Ep 13]], where it is referenced as "learned from Jones's own manager." This is that source, in Jones's own voice, eight years earlier. It is also the wiki's canonical treatment of the **IC-PM → manager-of-PMs transition**.

Key material:
- **The advice, from Jones's early manager Steve Roop.** Two things to communicate to a first direct report in their first week:
  1. It is **their** job to know more than anyone in the company about their product area and its customers — explicitly including more than their manager knows.
  2. They should have a **publicly visible "win" within the first 90 days** from their start date.
- **The reframe that makes it load-bearing.** "On the surface, it seems to put the focus on what is expected of the new team member. In fact, the advice is actually intended to show the new manager what *they* need to do." For the hire to out-know the manager, the manager must simultaneously train them *and* step out of the way: "it is their job to help the new hire grow larger than themselves." For the hire to score a public win, the manager must work "quietly in the background and downplaying their own contribution," letting the hire be the public face of the win "even to their own managers."
- **Why new managers are fragile.** Before managing, "their value was clear and directly tied to their work product" — and a PM promoted into management was, by definition, a strong IC, so their sense of individual value is high. Now they "must share the stage," leaving behind their old area of contribution "without really understanding what will replace it." Jones names this a "tricky emotional transition" and the advice a guidepost through it.
- **What a "win" should be.** Wins can take many forms, but "business result wins are always the best" — solving a customer problem in a way that meets the needs of the business, improving key business metrics.
- **The payoff, stated personally.** "I experienced the power of truly enabling and empowering a great team of people, and that my success was measured by their success."
- **Jones's own caveat.** The empowerment move assumes "our new hire is competent and that our new manager is not masking a failing new hire."

## Notes for the coach
This is the piece to reach for with a newly promoted product leader who is struggling to let go — especially one who is still the best PM in the room and knows it. The trick of the Roop advice is that it is framed as expectations *of the new hire*, which makes it easy for a defensive new manager to accept, while every clause actually prescribes a behaviour change in the manager. The 90-day public win is also a concrete, checkable onboarding artifact: ask a leader when their last hire's public win landed, and who got the credit for it.
