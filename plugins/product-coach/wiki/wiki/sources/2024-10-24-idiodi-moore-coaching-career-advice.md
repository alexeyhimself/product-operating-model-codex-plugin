# Product Therapy Ep 9 — Coaching Career Advice

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[jon-moore|Jon Moore]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 9 · SVPG, October 24, 2024 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aVfFc4qqvLY). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary.** Two things not in prior SVPG canon at this altitude: (a) **approach your career like a product** — a career vision, a career strategy, and the competencies you need per stage; you *don't* get promoted to learn the job, you get promoted to *do* it, so you must practice the next role before the promotion arrives. (b) **The "human skills" reframe of soft skills** (Idiodi credits [Kate Leto](https://www.kateleto.com/articles/human-skills-wheel), origin: US military officer's manual — hard skills for equipment, human skills for people). The human skills Idiodi rank-orders as making the biggest career difference in product: **emotional intelligence · collaborative problem-solving · critical thinking · trust-building · agency · time management · perseverance**. Moore's arc — from a self-described BBC journalist to product via a 2-metre desk move — grounds the *life-experience-before-product* claim: **master a discipline (engineering, finance, marketing, sales) first**, then move into product. Both hosts explicitly deprecate the *University product-management program* as sufficient preparation, and Moore names **don't rush out of team-level jobs** as one of the most under-appreciated career decisions in product.

## Notes for the coach
Idiodi's *"if you have not experienced getting good feedback, you will not have something to give other people"* generalizes to career advice: the mentors you learn from become the mentor you can be. Moore's Marty-mentorship anecdote (writing an unsent email, then sending it after a bathroom break; the tough-love phone call when he was made CPO — *"if you don't believe in yourself, no one else will"*) is the wiki's canonical case for **mentorship as leverage when your company has no one senior in the model**. Moore's Europe framing: *the standard of product in Europe is dropping* — as more companies adopt product titles without the model, PMs there are especially reliant on out-of-company mentorship.
