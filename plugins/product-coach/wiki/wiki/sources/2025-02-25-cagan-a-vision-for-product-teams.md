# Cagan — A Vision For Product Teams

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "A Vision For Product Teams" · svpg.com, 2025-02-25 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/a-vision-for-product-teams/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — Cagan's Feb 2025 vision article on how Generative AI reshapes empowered product teams over a 3–10 year horizon.** Predates and is partially retracted by the Feb 2026 [[2026-02-04-cagan-product-coaching-and-ai|Product Coaching and AI]] article. This is the piece the wiki already quotes by URL in two places — the retraction ([[model-as-product-coach#the-zero-to-one-problem-a-cagan-retraction|model-as-product-coach § zero-to-one]]) and the source page for the article that retracts it ([[2026-02-04-cagan-product-coaching-and-ai]]). Ingest it so those quotes have a proper root.

The core thesis: team composition shrinks (from ~8 people to ~3 — 1 PM, 1 designer, 1 engineer), **[[product-discovery|discovery]] becomes the primary work** while [[product-delivery|delivery]] compresses toward instantaneous, and the **[[product-team|"discovery trio"]]** — distinct competencies in business/UX/technology — stays essential. The article also predicts what dies: [[product-owner|product owners]] and [[feature-teams-vs-product-teams|feature-team PMs]] whose roles centre on delivery mechanics rather than judgment.

## Notes for the coach
- **Read alongside its retraction.** Two things travel together: this article's *vision* (which still stands — smaller judgment-driven teams; discovery-primary; the trio survives) and the [[2026-02-04-cagan-product-coaching-and-ai|Feb 2026 article]]'s **explicit retraction of its zero-to-one pessimism** ([[model-as-product-coach#the-zero-to-one-problem-a-cagan-retraction|see the passage in model-as-product-coach]]). When quoting this article, always note the retraction — otherwise you're citing a position Cagan has since walked back.
- **The retracted claim, verbatim from this article:** newcomers to product work would find *"entry was blocked because the bar was too high for anyone that didn't already possess the necessary experience."* Kept intact so future coaches can trace the correction back to its source.
- **The still-standing claims.** (a) Team composition shrinks toward ~3 people — 1 PM, 1 designer, 1 engineer. (b) Discovery becomes the primary work; delivery compresses. (c) The **discovery trio** — business, UX, technology — stays essential; role names may change but the three distinct competencies do not. (d) Most vulnerable: product owners and feature-team PMs whose roles centre on delivery processes. (e) *"If this vision becomes a reality, there will be a dramatic disruption to much of the tech workforce."*
- **The framing lines to reach for.** *"The question is not whether the tools can automate tasks; the question is whether this will create successful products?"* — the discipline is judgment-driven, not process-driven. And Chip Huyen (quoted by Cagan): AI *"reveals what actually requires thinking."*
- **The Jobs anchor.** Cagan invokes Jobs' warning about *"the disease of thinking that a really great idea is 90% of the work"* — used here to reject the "anyone with an idea can be a solopreneur" reading of GenAI. Solopreneurs, Cagan writes, remain *"the rare exceptions and not the rule."*
- **When to cite this vs. [[2025-05-15-idiodi-cagan-coaching-ai-impact-on-product-teams|Product Therapy Ep 20]] and [[2026-02-04-cagan-product-coaching-and-ai|Feb 2026]].** This article is the **origin** of the ~8→3 shrinkage and discovery-primary framing; Ep 20 restates the same thesis in podcast form with sharper numbers ("~90/10 discovery/delivery over 2–5 years"); Feb 2026 is where model-as-coach appears and the zero-to-one pessimism is walked back. Prefer this article when you need the *original* vision statement; prefer the later two for the current position.
