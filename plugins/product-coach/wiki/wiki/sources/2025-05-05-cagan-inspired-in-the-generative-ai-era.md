### Two things that changed since the 2nd edition (2017)

**Remote work.** INSPIRED encourages co-located teams; the pandemic made that impossible for many, and many companies already struggled to find enough local talent before that. Cagan's position: remote work is here indefinitely, and accessing global talent is generally a good trade-off — but this doesn't make discovery easier remotely. The effect is **role-dependent**: sales, customer success, CEOs, engineers, PMs, and designers are affected very differently, and most popular commentary fails to distinguish this. For most roles, remote workers are at least as effective as co-located ones — but **product discovery and innovation** are where teams keep struggling remotely, which is why "so many leading product model companies are pushing for a return to the office": a healthy friction, needed for effective collaboration, that hits PMs, designers, and tech leads hardest.

**Generative AI — two dimensions.** (1) How AI-powered products better solve customer problems — precisely what empowered teams are designed to do, though AI-powered products carry especially substantial risk (see [[2024-04-16-cagan-ai-product-management|AI Product Management]] for the detailed treatment), raising the demands on product teams, especially PMs. (2) How GenAI-powered tools/services help teams discover and deliver *better and faster* — already improving productivity for every trio role, differently per role, with second-order effects still emerging. General principle: anything that cuts the time/cost of testing ideas helps discovery; anything that cuts the time/cost of delivering to customers helps delivery.

### Closing framing
People and companies able to rapidly learn and apply new technology to solve real customer problems will be highly valued "well into the foreseeable future." INSPIRED's principles and techniques are offered as the foundation for thriving as a member of an empowered product team, generative AI included.
