# Cagan — The Best Product Management Model?

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The Best Product Management Model?" · svpg.com, 2008-01-05 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-best-product-management-model/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — early foundational article.** One of the earliest SVPG articles the wiki carries. Cagan's answer to *"How can we do product management like Google?"* / *"like Apple?"* — spoiler: **don't**. The article names five factors that shape which product-management model fits which company, and it is the pre-history of the wiki's current [[org-design-models]] framing (which uses the 2024 *Product Model and Org Design* piece as its own root but shares this article's insight: **model must fit the company, not vice versa**).

## What it backs
- concepts: [[org-design-models]] — the **five factors that shape the right product-management model for a company** (verbatim structure):
  1. **The type of product** — consumer internet service vs consumer electronics vs enterprise software vs small-business services all impose different demands.
  2. **The product-development process** — e.g. Scrum imposes specific demands on PMs and designers. *"Every process has limitations and the 'best' product management model will proactively attack these limitations."*
  3. **The role of product management** — where responsibilities are sliced up differently in the org, the model must fit the staff's ability and desire to serve.
  4. **The size of the organization** — 12-person venture-funded startup ≠ 4000-employee public company; different needs, different models.
  5. **The company culture** — *"sometimes this is in fact the dominant factor"*, especially when one or two people effectively make all product decisions and want to continue doing so.
- concepts: [[where-the-model-applies]] — the article foreshadows the *fit-test* thinking that becomes explicit in the 2024 Batch 6 articles about IT / government / outsourcing / commercial-vs-internal contexts.
- case studies: [[apple-role-of-product]], [[google-product-model]] — Cagan's opening claim: he does **not** recommend copying either. *"I have yet to recommend Google's model of product management to other companies. And in the case of Apple, to implement their model you'd have to clone Steve Jobs."* Both models are right *for their companies*, at least while their founders stay involved.
- concepts: [[product-culture]] — the "dominant factor" position for company culture. *"Unless a company has made the decision that they want to try to change their culture, I instead typically focus on getting the company to embrace the strengths of their culture or process, and make sure the product management model they select is proactive in addressing the weaknesses."*
- concepts: [[transformation]] — the "unless they want to change their culture" hedge is the article's earliest hint at Cagan's later [[transformation]] material — if the culture *does* need to change, that's not a product-model question, it's a company-transformation question.
- entities: [[marty-cagan]], [[svpg]].

## Notes for the coach
- **The article is 2008.** Predates *INSPIRED* (2008 first edition, same year), predates all four SVPG books, predates the empowered-teams canon. Read as **the earliest** statement of the "no one model works for everyone" thesis that later becomes [[where-the-model-applies]] and [[org-design-models]] — not as a current operational guide.
- **What has evolved since.** In 2008 Cagan lists company culture as *"sometimes the dominant factor."* In 2024's [[2024-08-13-cagan-kerievsky-the-product-model-in-outsourcing|outsourcing]] and government articles, the culture question is more explicitly named as *the* transformation blocker. The 2008 article's neutrality toward culture — *"embrace the strengths, address the weaknesses"* — becomes more prescriptive later.
- **The sports-team analogy.** Cagan opens with an athlete framing: *"Yes the skills are critically important. If you can't catch a ball, you won't go far as a receiver. However, winning requires more than skills."* Skills matter; game plan, teammates, opponent, playing field, and conditions matter more. Quotable when coaching an org that thinks it can hire its way out of a fit problem without addressing the surrounding conditions.
- **Not "the best is best-practices."** Cagan explicitly does **not** recommend a single best-practice model. This is important: when a company says *"just tell us what the best model is"*, this article is the answer — the framing is the wrong question.
