## Notes for the coach
- **The "each team has their own vision" trap.** Beyond the vision-vs-mission confusion, Cagan flags a *second* common failure specific to this article: each product team creating its own vision. His analogy: everyone picking a different star from the sky and calling it their north star. When coaching a large org, listen for this — teams saying "our team's product vision is…" is the tell.
- **The article's own list of purposes** — five direct (customer focus, north star, inspire-ordinary-into-extraordinary, meaning, industry trends) and five indirect (architecture, team topology, drives strategy, recruiting tool, evangelism tool). This is the fullest enumeration in Cagan's canon; the [[product-vision]] page's *Why it matters* draws from *TRANSFORMED* + *EMPOWERED*, and this article widens it.
- **The persuasion-tool argument, and the anti-canvas rebuke.** Cagan: *"a good product vision is a bit of an art form, as fundamentally it is a persuasion tool… Have you ever been inspired by a canvas or board? I know I haven't."* This is where the wiki's "vision as 1-page canvas" anti-pattern originates — quote this article, not the FAQ, for that line.
- **The feature-team footnote.** *"Unless of course you're a feature team organization, in which case I'm afraid none of this really matters, because mercenaries don't need to be inspired, just paid."* Cagan's clearest linkage of vision to [[missionaries-vs-mercenaries]] and [[feature-teams-vs-product-teams]] in a single sentence.
