## What it backs
- concepts: [[model-as-product-coach]] — **the sample project-instructions prompt** (verbatim in the article, quotable directly):
  > *"I am a product manager at << company >>, working on << product team name >> product team, and you are my expert product coach, assisting and proactively coaching me in my role to reach my maximum potential. You have been provided with our company's strategic context including the product strategy, team topology and team objectives (OKRs) for the product organization. Use this context as your primary data source, as well as publicly available information on the organization in your analysis. There are many prominent voices in the product management space, but for my work, please prioritize the content from SVPG (www.svpg.com/articles), Marty Cagan, Shreyas Doshi, and Teresa Torres. In each conversation, I will provide you with information about a particular objective so you can help me succeed. My goal is always to achieve a successful outcome, which means creating products that our customers love, yet also that work for our business. I expect you to: ask me questions when warranted to gain more context, fill in important missing information, and challenge my assumptions. Ask me questions that will let you most effectively coach and assist me in my role. I am seeking learning and truth, and not affirmation, so don't try to please me."*
  This is the operational template behind the *[[2026-07-22-cagan-olsen-how-ai-impacts-product-management|tell the model which product model you want]]* finding — the prompt names the school (SVPG · Cagan · Doshi · Torres) explicitly. **All three major foundation models are tested**: GPT, Claude, Gemini ("Gems" is Gemini's project term).
- concepts: [[product-vision]] — the **public vision examples list**: Dropbox, Airbnb, Asana, Roblox, Microsoft Productivity Future, HP Safe City, John Deere, Apple, SpaceX. Companion to [[product-vision]]'s canonical guidance from Cagan.

## Notes for the coach
- **This is the SVPG examples hub.** When [[model-as-product-coach]] says *"Cagan's reference example: [svpg.com/examples](https://www.svpg.com/examples)"* — this is the target. Add this card to that page's Sources list.
- **The sample prompt is deliberately generic and explicitly warned against.** The article's own hedge: *"We know it is dangerous to provide sample project instructions or prompts, because it runs the very real risk of people simply copying and pasting, and not thinking about what is appropriate for their situation."* Recommend it as a starting point; require the coach's client to adapt it to their org and objectives.
- **"Don't just accept model results blindly. Question everything and seek deep understanding."** Cagan's closing on the AI coach block — quote directly on [[model-as-product-coach]]'s *critique-seeking* stance.
- **Job-description caveat.** The four JD examples *"do not apply to feature teams"* — the article calls this out explicitly. Do not recommend the PM/Director/VP Product/Delivery Manager JDs as templates for a feature-team org; they will not fit.
- **Feature-team-org disclaimer around principles.** Cagan's earlier [[2020-08-05-cagan-product-vision-vs-mission|Product Vision vs Mission]] extends this reasoning: for feature-team orgs, the vision/principles machinery is not useful because *"mercenaries don't need to be inspired, just paid."*
- **The page is a hub, not a source of new argument.** Attribute *substantive* claims (e.g. Slack's principles, Supercell's) to their own linked destinations, not to this article; use this card only for what the *hub itself* provides — the prompt template, the JD-set framing, and the endorsement-by-listing.
