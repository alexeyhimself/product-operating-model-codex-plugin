# Product Therapy Ep 28 — Coaching Transformation: How a Hospitality Company Adopted the Product Model (Palace Resorts)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] with [[anuar-chapur|Anuar Chapur]] (CPTO, Palace Company) and [[gabrielle-bufrem|Gabrielle Bufrem]] (product coach) · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 28 · SVPG, September 18, 2025 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KP9iOQbZGZM). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the wiki's canonical case study of a *non-tech, non-US, family-run hospitality* transformation to the Product Operating Model.** Complements [[datasite-transformation]] (SaaS, US, PE-backed) and [[trainline-transformation]] (established European B2C tech). Together they now span three very different starting points for the model — with Palace being the strongest evidence yet that the model applies outside tech and outside the US. This ep is the primary source for a new [[palace-resorts-transformation]] case study; it also introduces [[anuar-chapur]] and [[gabrielle-bufrem]] as person entities.

Key material:

(a) **Company profile.** Palace Company (Palace Resorts + Le Blanc Spa Resorts + Beach Palace, Moon Palace, etc.) — Mexico-based, 15,000 employees, 6,000+ rooms across 18+ properties in 7 countries, family-run. Business was doing well post-COVID (record years), but *the technology work was not the reason*. ~150-person product/tech org that was effectively a *software factory*: intake → prioritize → project-fund → design prototype → convert to story points → build → deploy → team disperses → no maintenance. All the agile ceremonies wrapped around fundamentally waterfall work — "transformation theater" (Bufrem).

(b) **The trigger.** [[anuar-chapur|Anuar Chapur]] (from the founding family, not a tech background) took over technology, attended [[transformed]] workshop in NYC. Idiodi's frame at the top: *"Everything is broken"* is not the transformation trigger here — Palace shows that a *doing-well* business also transforms, precisely because it *"has earned the space to make the changes it wants."* Cagan introduced Chapur to Bufrem at the workshop because Bufrem spoke Spanish like Chapur's team.

(c) **Trust before assessment.** Bufrem's first question to Chapur: *"What's plan B here?"* Chapur, straight in the eye: *"There's no plan B. This is it."* → Bufrem: *"That's one of the keys to where we are today."* Then Chapur told her: *"If this is going to work, you have to come here. You are not a screen on Zoom. You are a person and they need to trust you."* → Two days in Mérida answering *every* question from Chapur's brothers and the team, before assessing.

(d) **Bufrem's assessment framework — three areas.** How you *build* / how you *solve problems* / how you *decide which problems to solve.* Verdict for Palace: build wasn't the best but not the worst — not the highest-ROI investment. And *"we don't have trust yet to be telling people we're going to change which problems we solve — we have not earned that right."* → Start with **how you solve problems**. (Frame maps directly to the SVPG *changing how you build / changing how you solve problems / changing which problems you solve* triad.)

(e) **The pilot team as poster-child, not prototype.** Best PM (or PM-shaped person), best designer, best engineers. Area: customer-facing enough that a win is visible, but not so business-critical that a stumble breaks the business. *"When we started, our clock started ticking. We need to deliver results like yesterday."* Anuar didn't have a real PM — but had someone who "will become" one; after a leadership overhaul, he's the one remaining. Deeper pattern: "product owners" at Palace were **translators from business to IT** who prioritized features in a list — no problem discovery.

(f) **The App Team saga — a multi-million-dollar mistake avoided.** Backstory: a survey years earlier said 40% of members would like to book via an app. The team was literally called the "app team." Bufrem's weekly 1.5-hour coaching. First meeting: *what it is to be a team, what roles are.* Design had been agency-model; PM was list-taker; engineers were barely present. Then the team did real discovery — went to the hotels, talked to guests, understood how they book — *and concluded the app was not the solution.* They pivoted to the existing web page (which had been shipped as done-in-Jira months earlier and stopped being maintained). Result: **booking conversion 1% → 5%+ over ~18 months.**

(g) **From features to problems — the shift in the team's mindset.** Bufrem's coaching frame: *"customers are not going to tell you what to build, and your teams are not going to tell you what to build, because they don't know. This is your job — customers know their problems, the business knows its problems. It's on us to almost be a detective and figure out the clues."*

(h) **Data as trust builder — political win.** Palace hired a head of data. Whenever a company myth arose ("no one wants X" / "we need an app"), the data team could produce a fact that trumped the myth. *"Data was not only crucial for the product teams to do their work but also a very big lever for us to gain trust with the company."* Framework: **moving from opinion-fueled to data-driven decision making.** Under the old model, only sales projects got funded because they were the only ones you could put a plausible ROI number on; hotel-operations, guest-experience, and conversion problems (waiting for restaurant tables at all-inclusive hotels, room-service time, discovery of restaurants) were invisible to funding.

(i) **Team topology moves.** Verticals: sales operations, vacation club, HR, hotel operations, etc. — divided so each team owns a real business area rather than a project. Recruiting shifted to bring in people who *wanted* to work this way — product leaders, head of data, engineering leaders. Structure: one PM + one tech lead + one designer per team ideally (or one designer per two teams).

(j) **Biggest challenge: convincing his brothers.** *"My brothers were the last people I got to convince."* After many opinions and after a year of transformation, the brothers wanted to add stakeholder sign-off (stakeholder receives / approves) on top of the outcome measurement — which would have collapsed the whole model. Chapur pulled all four brothers into a full day: what has been happening, why, what has to change on both sides. *"After so many broken promises from the past, the fact that they trusted me on this speaks a lot of the dynamics between us."* Family-company principle: *"protect the company from ourselves if we are ever in disagreement."*

(k) **Results (~2 years in).** Booking-engine conversion **1% → 5%+**; the cart-abandonment call-center handoff feature drove **$600K in bookings in 2 months** (March–April) while also generating continuous-discovery insight into why guests weren't booking. Cross-department OKRs replaced isolated tech goals — marketing, sales center, and product all rallied around one objective per period. *"We are more data-driven, more customer-centric, more collaborative, more aligned, and more discovery-centric as a company."*

(l) **Chapur's advice to leaders starting the journey.** *"Stop having thoughts of your own and start trusting [[marty-cagan|Cagan]]'s thoughts on everything that has to be done. Then get a coach like I did with Gabby — one small derailing can set you in a whole different direction. They're the guiding light like a boat going to an island. You may get distracted, a wave comes in, but you know how to correct course."* Bufrem's add: Chapur's *unwavering commitment* and *ability to ask for help* were huge; he emailed her back on articles by 7am after she sent them at night. Chapur's own line: **"I hired a team that allows me to be bold."**
