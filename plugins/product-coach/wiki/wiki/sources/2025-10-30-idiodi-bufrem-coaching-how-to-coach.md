# Product Therapy Ep 31 — Coaching How to Coach (with Gabrielle Bufrem)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[gabrielle-bufrem|Gabrielle Bufrem]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 31 · SVPG, October 30, 2025 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OtueL_XvD1Y). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the wiki's most complete podcast-form teaching on coaching *as a craft*.** Deepens [[product-coaching]], [[coaching-mindset]] and [[types-of-product-coaching]] with distinctions between mentor / manager / advisor / coach, the trust-building sequence, feedback-as-questions technique demonstrated live in role-plays, and the *tennis-vs-fresco-ball* communication metaphor. Together with Ep 28 ([[palace-resorts-transformation]]) and Ep 34 ([[2025-12-11-idiodi-bufrem-coaching-pilot-teams|pilot teams]]), this ep completes the wiki's Bufrem-as-external-coach grounding.

Key material:

(a) **Bufrem's own definition of coaching** (via what her clients tell her): *"first I'm their coach — to help them see what they can't see, give perspective, show there's more open to them. Second I'm their advisor — I've been there, done that, here's a shortcut if you want it. Third I'm their cheerleader — every time we talk, I'm there to help them win."* Coaching is *"only as great as the results you get together with the clients you work with."*

(b) **Mentor / manager / advisor / coach — the crisp distinctions.**
- **Mentor** — you give advice from your own experience; you meet occasionally, care about them at that level. Bounded by *what you have done before.*
- **Manager** — the best managers ARE coaches; but the role is about taking someone's career to the next level, giving them context, plus the bureaucratic things that keep the row.
- **Advisor** — you've done something before and can show them how, or give insights into how they might solve it.
- **Coach** — *enables people to see their best potential; gives perspective; challenges; asks the questions they never wanted to answer but that unlock possibilities.* Has skin in the game — measures success by the client's results.

(c) **"Everyone deserves a coach"** (not "needs" — deserves). Steph Curry has five or six coaches. Bufrem's frame: *"the best players in the world have multiple coaches — and everyone deserves that at work."* Idiodi's canonical restatement: *"doing something is different from getting better at something."*

(d) **The single most important thing a manager can do to get better products, engineers, PMs.** Idiodi: *"If you want better product management in your organization, don't work on products. If you want better products, don't work on products. Work on the people who build the product. If you want better engineering, don't work on some engineering process or framework — work on the people who are doing the engineering. This is how the best companies scale."* Everything can be coached: mindsets, behaviors, competencies, decision frameworks.

(e) **Trust as the currency; how Bufrem invests in it.** *"Without trust you don't have anything."* Small talk about the cat matters because the cat matters to them. Idiodi's paraphrase of Shreyas: *"we need to get to 10 years of getting to know someone in like an hour."* Bufrem: changed her own intro from work-focused to life/passion-focused to encourage the same in clients. Meets in person for any big engagement — *"if we spend two days getting to know each other, those two days yield so much in the future."* Rule: **remember the things that are important to them.**

(f) **Rules of engagement design.** Bufrem plays them back: *"what is helpful to you? What do you want from me? How do you like to be kept accountable? Any tone I should be aware of?"* Set the frame that the coaching space is where they can be *themselves*, not the version they present to the CEO / all-hands / board.

(g) **"Leave some space for magic."** Bufrem's design principle: clients often want something different by the end than what they set out to want. Let the plan bend when a real opportunity arises.

(h) **The workplace-as-game-time / coaching-as-practice frame** (Idiodi). *"Every day at work is game time, showtime, deadline time, 'I want the best of you' time. So I ask people: where did you have a chance to practice?"* You are promoted to do the job, not to learn the job — the best place to learn the skills is when everyone gives you grace to fail, which is when you're *not* doing the job yet.

(i) **Coaching feedback as *questions*, not judgment — demonstrated live.** Bufrem's technique through two role-plays (Idiodi playing a client who screamed at his CEO, then a PM who can't build a vision):
- **Reserve judgment**; acknowledge the emotion (*"I can see you are frustrated"*).
- **Ask questions that guide the client to their own clarity** (*"what were you trying to do with that statement? What action do you think he'll take? Are those the goals you have for the conversation moving forward?"*).
- **Never write it for them.** On "can't you just write the vision for me?" — *"I am not the right person to write the vision for you, because you're the one living this every day. But I'm happy to be your co-pilot — assistant director to your director."*
- **Idiodi's summary of the demonstrated principle: my job is not to fish for you — it is to teach you how to fish.**

(j) **Removing self-imposed limiting boxes.** *"Very accomplished people place themselves in imaginary limiting boxes … a lot of my job is to ask 'is that true?' Sometimes they say 'no, not really.' Then I ask 'what would you do if it wasn't? What other options are available to you?'"*

(k) **Communication mindset — tennis vs fresco ball.** *In tennis you play to beat the opponent — winning means the other person loses.* *In fresco ball (Brazil beach game) your entire goal is to rally with the person — you hit the ball so they can respond.* The coaching frame: **"how do I hit so they can respond?"** In workplace communication (especially with a difficult stakeholder), we're communicating to *keep the rally*, not to *win the point.* Idiodi's paraphrase: *"we're not reacting, we're responding — teeing the ball where they can hit it well."*

(l) **Coaching leaders vs coaching ICs — what changes.** For a **leader**, their product is their people, so coaching is *coaching them to coach* (meta). Topics: vision, strategic context, product strategy, team objectives, team-topology, hiring, onboarding. For **ICs / creators**, coaching is on the skills to make the product great: discovery, execution, delighting customers, making business goals work — different topics, equally fun.

(m) **How Bufrem measures her own success.** (i) The client hits the outcome you designed together. (ii) The **butterfly effect** — client starts being able to do things they couldn't before, and it spills into other parts of their life. The extreme example she shares: *"the husband of a client contacted me on LinkedIn: 'I want to gift my wife you for a month, how do I do that?'"*

(n) **Idiodi's grandfather-effect measure.** *"As a parent, you measure your parenting not by what your kids take but by what they give their kids. That is the magic of it. I'm now old in the profession that I go into a company, somebody says something, I ask 'where did you learn that?' — 'from my manager' — turns out their manager worked for me. The graduation gift for anybody who has been coached is what they are able to pass on to another person."*
