# Cagan — Preparing For The Future

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Preparing For The Future" · svpg.com, 2023-06-09 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/preparing-for-the-future/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — the earliest SVPG article on generative AI's impact on how products are built**, and the true root for several claims the wiki previously (as of this morning's Batch 1 ingest) attributed to 2024–2025 articles. Written as the narrative behind a 2023 keynote for Norway's Future Week conference, so it is explicitly speculative in tone ("everything in here should be taken with a large grain of salt") — but its ten numbered predictions turned out to seed nearly every later SVPG AI article. Four of Batch 1's six articles cite it as foundational context; Batch 1 flagged it as un-clipped and un-ingested. This ingest closes that gap.

## Notes for the coach — organized by the article's ten numbered points

1. **Product teams and team topology.** Cognitive load (how much engineers can keep in their heads) is the binding constraint on team topology today; AI-powered engineering tools lower it, which lets team scope grow — "fewer product teams, with smaller product team sizes." Cagan explicitly poses the headcount question the wiki's [[team-topology]] page later inherited as an open one: "one possibility is that a company simply reduces the number of product people they need... But historically, over time, companies pursue more products, and more new companies are created, so the industry experiences overall growth. There's no law that says this will continue to happen, but I believe it will. Although... it's never a smooth transition. There are always casualties." **This is Cagan's own earliest framing of the more-vs-fewer-people tension** — a belief, explicitly hedged, not a resolved claim. It predates rather than answers the June-2026 Modern CTO framing of the same question; treat it as prior context on [[team-topology]], not a resolution.
2. **Team autonomy and initiatives.** Depending on other product teams to get anything done is a major frustration/disempowerment source; AI-powered tools raise autonomy, potentially to the point a single team can own not just features but larger cross-team initiatives end-to-end.
3. **Team social dynamics.** An open question, not a prediction: will AI tools help build trust across a cross-functional team, or let people retreat into AI-agent interaction and avoid the "necessary friction" of healthy disagreement?
4. **Thinking and judgment.** "The greatest potential, but also the greatest risk." The core coaching skill — thinking through hard tradeoffs — is exactly what's easiest to abdicate to a tool that answers instantly. Verbatim: *"if people end up abdicating actual thinking and judgment to these tools, then I fear we are headed in a very bad direction."*
5. **Artifacts.** Faster artifact creation (PRDs, roadmaps, user stories) is a trap if it substitutes for the discovery work the artifact is supposed to document: "the artifact may look the same superficially, but the artifact's value will not be."
6. **Quality.** The deterministic-vs-probabilistic argument, two years before [[2025-06-09-cagan-creating-intelligent-products|Creating Intelligent Products]] gives it a name and a history: "our current approach to quality is largely based on deterministic products... Yet for many new products built on generative AI, our products are no longer deterministic, but rather probabilistic."
7. **Product engineers.** Predicts a "golden era of engineering" — AI tools give ordinary engineers "10X engineer" superpowers, shrinking per-team headcount while raising per-company and per-industry engineer demand overall. Lower-level, oft-repeated engineering work is most exposed; architecture work reflecting an org's unique legacy systems and vision is not.
8. **Product designers.** The origin of the visual-vs-service/interaction-design split later formalized as five disciplines: visual-design-only designers are already being disrupted (layoffs cited as already underway in 2023); designers skilled at service and interaction design become *more* essential, not less, because AI is exposing technology to users in new and unfamiliar ways.
9. **Product managers.** The origin of the three-way distinction: an empowered-team PM owns *value and viability*; a feature-team PM is really a *program/project manager*; an Agile product owner is an *administrative role* for the backlog. Verbatim: *"For feature team product managers, and especially for administrative product owners, I will be surprised if the new generation of tools does not seriously disrupt those roles... Personally, I consider that a good thing, as these are not roles that I consider worthy of the minds of the people that serve in those roles."* PMs on empowered teams become *more* critical, with execution work reduced and more mental energy freed for value/viability.
10. **Ethics.** Ethics as a proactive product-team responsibility, not an afterthought — "let's build this cool thing first, then maybe consider ethics later" named as a real, common failure. Predates and anticipates the viability-includes-ethics framing later canon.

**The three closing quotes.** Shreyas Doshi: *"The best way to prepare yourself for the future is to invest in your empathy, thinking, and creativity."* Dr. Marily Nika: *"Don't do AI for the sake of doing AI. Make sure there is a problem there."* And Cagan's own closing first-principle reminder: *"know what you can't know."*
