# Cagan — The VP Product Role

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The VP Product Role" · svpg.com, 2022-05-03 (site's canonical VP Product / CPO reference; earlier drafts of this material predate the page date) — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-vp-product-role/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** Cagan's clearest single-article statement of what to look for in a **VP Product / CPO / head of product** — the most senior product role in a company or business unit. Written for three audiences (CEOs/recruiters hiring, incumbents evaluating themselves, aspirants planning a path). Titles vary — Director of Product Management → VP Product → CPO — but the accountabilities do not.

## What it backs
- competencies: [[product-leadership]] — the four core competencies for the VP Product role: **Team Development, Product Vision, Execution, Product Culture**, plus **Experience** and **Chemistry** as secondary criteria. This complements the *EMPOWERED* Ch 3 two-halves-of-the-role framing already on the page: this article is the *hiring-and-evaluation* form of the same competencies.
- concepts: [[founder-style-leadership]] — Cagan's two-scenario framework for VP Product hiring: (a) *strong visionary CEO* — VP Product should complement, not clash, and often lands as an *execution partner*; (b) *no clear product visionary* — VP Product must supply the vision themselves. The two clearest failure modes are (i) visionary CEO hires "in her own image" (short tenure, revolving door) and (ii) non-visionary CEO hires "in her own image" (no clash, but a vision void).
- concepts: [[product-culture]] — the fourth competency: strong product culture = testing/learning discipline + a bias to continuous innovation + trust of designers and engineers. Cagan asks candidates for *concrete plans* for instilling this in the hiring company, not just theoretical answers.
- frameworks: [[hiring-and-onboarding]] — the role-based interviewing questions ("give real examples of your own product culture experiences", "walk me through your track record of developing others") and Cagan's own recommended step: **a long dinner with CEO + CTO** for personal chemistry, since the CEO/CTO/VP Product triangle is where the role will actually fail if the personal fit is wrong.
- competencies: [[product-manager]] — the "one of the worst things you can do" warning: promoting a poor-performing PM into head-of-product to make them a manager, "because she works well with people," destroys the coaching-of-strong-PMs chain.

## Notes for the coach
- **The four-competencies frame is the operational counterpart to *EMPOWERED* Ch 3.** The book gives you two halves (leadership + management, with four leadership responsibilities and three management ones); this article gives you the *interview rubric*: Team Development, Product Vision, Execution, Product Culture. Use them together.
- **On MBA.** *"I absolutely do not consider an MBA a requirement, but I personally would not avoid hiring someone just because they had an MBA."* Cagan doesn't have one. Same posture as [[2012-03-18-cagan-the-product-manager-contribution]] uses for PMs — mindset, not credential.
- **The revolving-door tell.** If the VP Product / CPO position at a company is a revolving door, Cagan's diagnosis is almost always the *"visionary CEO hires a would-be visionary VP Product and they clash"* pattern (a); recommend investigating the CEO's own product-visionary self-assessment before recruiting the next one.
- **"Team Development is the single most important responsibility."** Aligns with *EMPOWERED* Ch 3's naming of coaching as "the single most important, yet most often overlooked, element to capable management." Same claim from two directions.
- **Peer-to-CTO / VP-Marketing.** *"With some exceptions, it is important that this role be a peer to the CTO and the VP Marketing."* Structural. If a company has VP Product reporting into CTO, that is *usually* the exception Cagan means, and it constrains what the role can do.
