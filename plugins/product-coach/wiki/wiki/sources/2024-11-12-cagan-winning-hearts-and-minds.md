# Cagan — Winning Hearts and Minds

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Winning Hearts and Minds" · svpg.com, 2024-11-12 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/winning-hearts-and-minds/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon)** — the positive-pattern companion to [[transformation-as-a-project|Transformation as a Project]] (Cagan's prior article, the anti-pattern of managing a transformation as a big project). Framing originated with SVPG Partner Christian Idiodi in conversation with Cagan, who adopted it for senior-leadership discussions.

## What it backs
- concepts: [[transformation]] (new subsection — the seven-behavior "earning trust" pattern)

## Notes for the coach
- **The framing, verbatim-adjacent:** a successful transformation is "a race to win the hearts and minds of the executive team and stakeholders before they lose confidence." Executives won't extend trust in advance as a matter of course — they protect company assets and take calculated risk, not blank checks.
- **Seven behaviors that earn trust, named in the article** (attribute to this article when quoting any of them individually): (1) product leaders take responsibility for staffing and coaching capable teams — "you will be judged by your stakeholders... by your weakest people"; (2) product managers who understand business, data, and customers, not retitled feature-team PMs/POs; (3) product teams solving problems in ways customers love *and* that work for the business (not customer-only); (4) consistent delivery on [[high-integrity-commitments|high-integrity commitments]]; (5) delivering real, measurable business outcomes, not just activity; (6) responding quickly and competently to customer issues — instrumentation and monitoring in place *before* customers notice; (7) understanding the obligation to keep the lights on — unglamorous but trust-destroying if ignored or resented.
- **When to cite:** the positive mirror to use alongside [[transformation-as-a-project]] — pair "here's the anti-pattern" with "here's what actually earns trust," rather than only naming what not to do.
