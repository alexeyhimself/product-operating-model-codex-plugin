# Cagan — Creating Technology Products That Your Customers Love (LeadDev)

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan, *LeadDev Bookmarked* book club (10th edition), moderated by Susan Bond · YouTube, published February 1, 2021 — [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MxvMsDjfVAs). Slim provenance card; the transcript is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — reinforcement, with one useful lineage passage.** An engineering-leadership audience (LeadDev), so the framing leans toward what tech leads need from product. Its distinct contribution is Cagan's own account of **where the techniques came from and why *INSPIRED* needed a total rewrite.**

Key material:

(a) **The essence, in one line.** *"Products customers love that work for the business — that's the essence. Talk to any great engineer, designer or product manager: that's really why they're doing what they're doing."*

(b) **Where the techniques came from — and what they replaced.** Cagan credits working with *"early Google, early Amazon, Twitter"* and other teams, and says *"the way they solved those problems was often so different than the way most companies did."* The origin point is explicit: *"a lot of those techniques really emerged in the Netscape era."* And what he was taught before that: *"the way I was taught to build products was really **the Microsoft way** — and it's not bad necessarily, but it changed pretty dramatically with the internet."* Useful lineage for [[pom-vs-agile-and-safe]] and for anyone who assumes the model is a post-Agile invention.

(c) **Why the 2nd edition of *INSPIRED* was a 100% rewrite.** *"So much had changed over the 10 years — like agile, like lean startup. I had written about those things **coming** in the first edition, but now they were already there for most teams for many years."* See [[inspired]].

(d) **The audience *INSPIRED* was written for, and the gap it filled.** *"Very specifically for product teams — in particular the product managers, the product designers and the tech leads."* And the asymmetry that motivated it: *"there are many good resources for lead devs, many good resources for designers, but there really wasn't much for product managers. In fact, talk to a typical dev and they'll typically complain about their product managers, because **that's the weak link in the chain**."*
