# Cagan — The Moral Case for the Product Model

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The Moral Case for the Product Model" · svpg.com, 2024-08-27 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-moral-case-for-the-product-model/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — Cagan's only dedicated statement of the *moral*, as distinct from the *business*, case for the product model.** He is explicit that he normally avoids this angle with executives ("I don't have the confidence... to go to a CEO and argue that their employees deserve better") and still recommends leading with the business case and pilot teams — but the moral case is presented here as a genuine, separate argument worth knowing, especially for coaching individual contributors who've stopped looking for meaning in their work.

## What it backs
- concepts: [[missionaries-vs-mercenaries]] (new — the moral-case framing of *why* missionary teams report higher satisfaction: purpose, full use of one's skills, real ownership of the outcome; Seth Godin's *The Song of Significance* as a companion read; the Ray Anderson / Interface carbon-negative transformation as a worked illustration of a leader naming a meaningful problem and empowering the team to solve it, without also dictating the solution or the date)

## Notes for the coach
- **The moral case, stated plainly.** Employees on empowered teams of missionaries are happier, find work more fulfilling, and feel proud of improving customers' lives (or society, or the environment) — distinct from, but related to, the business case (most companies underutilize the talent they've hired).
- **The coaching caveat — important.** Cagan explicitly warns against marching into a CEO's office to lecture about "doing the right thing" — that will "very likely not end well." The recommended sequence stays: business case first, proven with [[pilot-teams|pilot teams]], to executives; the moral case is most useful with individual contributors who have already given up hope of finding meaning in their work and are "just looking for a reliable paycheck" — some of them respond to the moral case more than the business case.
- **The Ray Anderson / Interface story (the worked example).** In 1994 Interface's founder challenged his leadership team to make the company carbon-neutral without knowing how — *"you must fill in the year"* — then empowered the team to figure it out. Interface is today carbon-negative, a recognized sustainability leader, and valued over $1B. Cagan's own gloss: the leader named the meaningful problem and empowered the team; he did **not** also hand them a solution and a date, which "would have undermined their empowerment." A clean, non-tech illustration of vision + empowerment operating together.
- **Seth Godin's *The Song of Significance*** — recommended as a companion to *[[transformed|TRANSFORMED]]*, making from the moral angle much the same argument the wiki grounds in SVPG canon.
