# Cagan — Commercial vs Internal Products

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Commercial vs Internal Products" · svpg.com, 2026-04-09 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/commercial-vs-internal-products/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon)** — clarifies that internal products carry the same four risks as commercial products but at a structurally lower bar (captive users), while commercial products must win a competitive marketplace — and argues product discovery is the actual differentiator for commercial success, "especially in the AI era."

## What it backs
- synthesis: [[where-the-model-applies]] (a main pillar — internal vs. commercial as an orthogonal axis to "does the model apply," not a fit test itself)
- concepts: [[the-four-big-risks]] (light supporting citation — same four risks, lower bar internally: value is easier with a captive user, usability tolerates training/documentation, feasibility is usually lower-scale, viability is narrower and more insulated)
- synthesis: [[product-myths]] (nuances Myth 9, "PM as CEO" — internal PM "CEO of the product" framing "sounds tone-deaf and arrogant"; for a commercial PM, deep immersion in marketing/sales/funding/legal/compliance genuinely does start to look CEO-adjacent, in the value/outcome-responsibility sense the myth's correction already draws out, not the authority sense)
- concepts: [[build-vs-buy-and-vibe-coding]] (footnote cross-reference — internal users increasingly free to vibe-code their own tools is a new, low-stakes form of internal-product "competition")

## Notes for the coach
- **The core distinction, verbatim-adjacent:** with an internal product, the user can't choose a competitor — "the company is literally paying them to use these products" — so it's enough to solve the job-to-be-done. With a commercial product, it's not enough to solve the problem; the product must be *so much better than the alternatives* that customers are willing to switch.
- **Why this matters "especially in the AI era":** competitors for commercial products are emerging faster than ever; product discovery is "usually the difference between success and failure" for a commercial product in a way it historically wasn't emphasized to be for internal tools.
- **What's explicitly excluded from "internal products" here:** Traditional IT is called out by name (footnote) as a *different* category from the internal products/services this article discusses (customer-enabling tools, employee productivity tools, internal platform services) — don't conflate the two when cross-referencing [[it-to-product-organization]].
- **When to cite:** whenever a coach needs to calibrate expectations for an internal-tools team versus a commercial-product team — same model, same four risks, genuinely different bar and different centrality of discovery.
