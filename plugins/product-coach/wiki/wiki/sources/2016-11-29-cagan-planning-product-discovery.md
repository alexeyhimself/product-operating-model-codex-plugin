# Planning Product Discovery

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Planning Product Discovery* · SVPG, Nov 29, 2016 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/planning-product-discovery/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — how to frame a discovery effort when it is big enough to need framing**, and — see the contradiction note below — **the earliest written appearance in the corpus of *ethical risk* as a named risk category.**

- **When planning is warranted.** *"Much of product discovery work doesn't actually require a lot of planning."* Planning matters for *"big projects and especially initiatives (projects spanning multiple teams)."* [[2016-03-01-cagan-discovery-sprints|Discovery sprints]] have planning built in; this article covers the other cases.
- **The two goals.** (1) **Alignment** — agree the specific problem, which users/customers it is for, and how you will know you succeeded; *"not accidentally, these should align directly to your OKRs"* ([[okrs]], [[team-objectives]]). (2) **Identify the big risks** to be tackled.
- **The comfort-zone trap.** *"Most teams tend to gravitate towards a particular type of risk that they might be most comfortable with"* — usually technology risk (performance, scale) or usability risk. *"Those are both legitimate risks, but they're far from the only risks, and at least in my experience, those are often the easier risks to tackle."*
- **Stakeholder risk, decomposed.** The article's main contribution — the *"often messy stakeholder risk"* broken into six checkable questions:
  - **Financial** — can we afford this solution?
  - **Business development** — does this solution work for our partners?
  - **Marketing** — is this solution consistent with our brand?
  - **Sales** — is this solution compatible with our go-to-market strategy?
  - **Legal** — is this solution something we can legally actually do?
  - **Ethical** — *"is this solution something we should do?"*
- **The proportionality rule, again.** If the trio sees no significant risk in any dimension, proceed to delivery, *"fully realizing there's a chance the team will occasionally be proven wrong. However, this is preferable to the alternative of having the team be extremely conservative and testing every assumption."*
- **The worked example: Facebook and fake news** (topical in Nov 2016). Cagan lists the non-technical risks a team would hit — *"Who gets to define truth? Is it even appropriate for Facebook to take on that role?"* — plus freedom-of-speech concerns, cultural norms and censorship, monetization impact, sales-channel implications. His point: *"this gets to the heart of what makes product difficult."*

## ⚠ Refines an existing lineage — flagged, not silently overwritten
[[product-ethics]] and [[the-four-big-risks]] carry a dated lineage table whose "ethics named" row begins at **2018** ([[2018-05-31-cagan-root-causes-of-product-failure-mtpcon-sf|mtpcon SF]] floated → [[2020-01-28-cagan-coaching-ethics|*Coaching – Ethics*, 2020]] advocated). This 2016 article predates both and already names *"Ethical risk – is this solution something we should do?"* in a published list.

**It does not overturn the lineage — it refines it**, and the distinction matters: here ethical risk is one of **six sub-dimensions of stakeholder risk**, *not* a proposed fifth peer to value/usability/feasibility/viability. So the accurate reading is: **ethics was a named discovery risk from 2016**, and what 2018–2020 added was the argument to **promote it to a first-class fifth risk**. Both pages have been updated with a 2016 row reflecting that reading.

## Notes for the coach
The six stakeholder-risk questions are the most directly reusable thing in the Discovery Series — they work verbatim as a pre-discovery checklist, and they catch exactly the risks engineering-led teams skip. Use the comfort-zone observation diagnostically: ask a team which risk they started with, and you usually learn what they are avoiding.
