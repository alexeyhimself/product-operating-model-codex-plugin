# Discovery vs. Documentation

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Discovery vs. Documentation* · SVPG, Aug 25, 2021 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/discovery-vs-documentation/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the article that makes the PRD question a matter of *sequence*, not of artifact.** The nuance here is easily lost and is why this card matters: Cagan does **not** say PRDs are bad.

- **The actual position.** *"PRDs are not inherently bad."* Document after discovery to communicate with remote engineers and *"that's fine."* The failure is ordering: *"in nearly every case I see, the PRD is written instead of the product discovery work, rather than after."*
- **Same logic applied to roadmaps.** *"The same is true with product roadmaps. They are not inherently bad."* If the deliverables are the *result* of discovery, a roadmap is *"simply a useful communication tool about what's coming and when."* If created before or instead, you get commitments that likely won't deliver outcomes. He notes the [[outcome-based-roadmap|outcome-based roadmap]] exists but is *"unfortunately the exception and not the rule."*
- **The three things that matter** — restated from [Beyond Lean and Agile](https://www.svpg.com/beyond-lean-and-agile/) *(uncarded)*: address the **risks up front** before production code; solve problems **collaboratively** rather than sequentially; focus on **outcomes not output**. *"A PRD addresses precisely none of these problems."*
- **Why user stories are useful *because* they are useless.** *"User stories themselves are pretty useless. But that in fact is what makes them useful. They make no pretense about describing requirements. They are simply a placeholder for a discussion."* Contrast with a PRD: *"there is a certain gravity to that document, and people are much less likely to challenge or question."*
- **Cagan on his own past.** *"I have to admit I got pretty good at writing these PRDs. Sometimes I even found myself believing what I wrote myself, despite having no real evidence."*
- **The stakes, stated as a leadership charge.** *"When you just provide those engineers requirements instead of collaborating with them in determining what the right solution is, then you are effectively neutering those engineers… And to those of you that are product leaders, this is really on you."* Closing barb: *"you may as well just give up on innovation and hire Accenture."*
- **The pandemic framing.** Written as a follow-up to [[2020-04-13-cagan-discovery-when-working-remotely]], warning that remote work would drive teams back to heavy artifacts — which he says it did.

## Notes for the coach
Reach for this when a team has been told to "stop writing PRDs" and has become dogmatic about it — Cagan's position is more useful and more defensible than the slogan. The user-story insight (useless on purpose, therefore safe) reframes a debate teams waste a lot of time on. The "gravity of the document" idea is the practical core: the risk of a PRD is that it stops being questioned.
