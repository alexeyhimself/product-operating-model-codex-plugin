# Cagan — Behind Every Great Company

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Behind Every Great Company" · svpg.com, 2020-11-23 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/behind-every-great-company/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** The **leader-profile companion** to [[2016-10-01-cagan-behind-every-great-product|Behind Every Great Product]] (2016) — same idea (introduce real people, not just principles) applied to **product leaders** instead of PMs. Published a few days after [[2020-11-19-cagan-product-leadership-is-hard|Product Leadership Is Hard]] to give that abstract argument concrete faces. Draws its subjects from the eight *EMPOWERED* leader profiles (two of PM, two of design, two of engineering, two of companies).

## What it backs
- competencies: [[product-leadership]] — the article's **five common patterns across strong product leaders** (verbatim structure of Cagan's closing takeaways):
  1. All have experienced the magic of strong empowered product teams and strive to replicate it.
  2. All are committed to **coaching and developing others**; measure their own success by the success of those they help.
  3. **None are generic people managers** — all are accomplished product professionals in their own right, giving them the foundation to coach from first-hand knowledge. (This is a strong claim about *who to hire* into product leadership.)
  4. All understand that *"their 'product' is now their people."*
  5. All are genuinely good human beings — the kind most people would want to work for.
- concepts: [[transformation]] — the article's closing frame: *"if your organization hopes to transform from feature teams to empowered product teams, then what we've learned is that strong product leadership is key."*
- concepts: [[product-coaching]] — the profiles collectively make coaching the through-line for the head-of-product / VP Product / CPO role.
- entities: [[empowered]] — the article is the public-facing preview of the eight leader profiles inside *EMPOWERED*, name-by-name.
- entities: [[lea-hickman]] — cross-referenced via *EMPOWERED*'s Ch 25 profile; the Adobe story from [[2016-10-01-cagan-behind-every-great-product]] gives her the product-manager back-story.
- entities: [[marty-cagan]], [[svpg]].

## Notes for the coach
- **Named leaders in the article.** All eight are also profiled fully in *EMPOWERED*; the essay is the free-to-read preview:
  1. **Avid Larizadeh Duggan** — engineer → PM → GV partner → Code.org champion. Quote: *"A leader needs to make her team feel safe… no one is smarter than everyone else."*
  2. **April Underwood** — engineer → PM at Twitter → founding head of product at Slack. Quote: side-steps across function are her *"most valuable asset"* as an exec.
  3. **Lisa Kavanaugh** — HP engineer → Ask.com CTO → coach. The *"personal effort doesn't scale"* diagnosis for new managers.
  4. **Debby Meredith** — Netscape VP Eng → interim CTO across 50+ startups. On CEOs with fundamental misunderstandings of engineering's role.
  5. **Audrey Crane** — designer under Hugh Dubberly → design leader → team-designer. On identifying latent talent.
  6. **Christina Wodtke** — designer → design leader → Stanford faculty. *"I realized I needed to stop designing products, in order to start designing a place where good design could happen."*
  7. **Shan-Lyn Ma** — Gilt PM → Zola co-founder. On diversity as an explicit hiring axis from day one.
  8. **Judy Gibbons** — Apple → Microsoft → Accel → transformation board member. The **product-team empowerment claim from the board seat**: *"there needs to be obsession with the customer from the top… highly-focused and effective cross-functional teams led by a capable product manager, empowered to deliver on the product vision."*
- **The "not generic people managers" claim is load-bearing.** When a company is deciding whether to hire a non-product person into a head-of-product role, this essay is the direct rebuttal. Cagan is uncompromising here — every one of the eight is *"an accomplished product professional in their own right."*
- **Positioning vs [[2020-11-19-cagan-product-leadership-is-hard]].** *Product Leadership Is Hard* is the theory ("what it takes"); this essay is the practice ("who exemplifies it"). Cite them together when coaching a leadership team on what to look for.
