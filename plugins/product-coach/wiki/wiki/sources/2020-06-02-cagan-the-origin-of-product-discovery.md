# The Origin of Product Discovery

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *The Origin of Product Discovery* · SVPG, Jun 2, 2020 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/the-origin-of-product-discovery/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the etymology root for [[product-discovery]].** Cagan's own account of where the term came from and why he chose it. The wiki's `## Origin of the term (2007)` section on [[product-discovery]] previously rested on [[2007-09-25-cagan-product-discovery]] (the article that went all-in on the term) without the retrospective that explains the *choice*.

Key material:
- **The two eternal problems.** *"We need to figure out the right product, and then we have to build the product right."* Discovery is the first; [[product-delivery|delivery]] the second. Cagan states he focuses on the first because *"the first is harder and that's where most innovation happens."*
- **The dates.** Trying the term out from **~2005**; went **all in in 2007**, forced by the publication deadline for the first edition of *[[inspired|INSPIRED]]*.
- **Why not "requirements."** In 2007 *"just about every product manager talked about 'gathering and defining requirements'"* — which Cagan considered *"not only misguided, but arrogant."* He wanted a term that conjured a different image: an **open mind**, knowing what you can't know, admitting what you don't know.
- **Where the word came from — pharmaceuticals.** *"I found the term 'discovery' from the pharmaceutical industry for the process they used to come up with a viable medication."* The attraction: that industry *"acknowledged the risk up front"* and expected most candidates to fail before manufacture.
- **Deliberately technique-agnostic.** *"I also liked the term 'discovery' because it was technique agnostic… An MVP is a discovery technique, as is design thinking, as is customer development, as is 'Jobs To Be Done.'"* He names this as intentional: *"I have seen so many techniques and methods come and go, so I didn't want to explicitly associate with any of them."*
- **And risk-shaped.** *"I also liked that the term 'discovery' encouraged product teams to think about the risks of value, usability, feasibility and viability"* — see [[the-four-big-risks]].
- **The closing shift.** *"Now my focus is more on making sure product teams are working in an environment where they are empowered to do product discovery, rather than just given roadmaps to build"* — the 2020 pivot from technique to [[empowered-product-teams|empowerment]].

## Notes for the coach
The single most useful card when someone asks *"isn't discovery just a rebranded waterfall analysis phase?"* or *"why not just say research?"* Cagan's answer is that the word was chosen against **requirements**, borrowed from an industry that **budgets for failure**, and kept **technique-agnostic on purpose** — so a coachee who says "we do discovery, we use design sprints" has named a technique, not the discipline. Useful too for dating: discovery is a 2005–2007 coinage, not a post-Lean-Startup one.
