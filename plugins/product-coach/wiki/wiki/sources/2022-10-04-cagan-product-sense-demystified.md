## Notes for the coach
- **The core reframe.** Cagan argues product sense is better described as **deep product knowledge** — "the result of truly immersing yourself into a specific product space," not an innate faculty. His stated objection to the term is that "sense" implies something you're born with, and he had seen it become an excuse in both directions: *"Trust me because I have great product sense"* and *"I could never be a successful product person because my manager says I don't have good product sense."*
- **How it's actually built (the four inputs).** Serious quality time with customers; analyzing the competitive landscape; immersing yourself in the product data; consuming everything available about the industry and the **enabling technologies**. Cagan's own worked example is the software-tools space — hundreds of product-team visits, testing every tool he could find, relationships with thought leaders, industry analysts, conference speaking — and his claim is that the resulting 5–10-year foresight came from that effort, not from any gift.
- **The counter-intuitive bit worth surfacing in coaching.** Long domain experience is *not* a reliable advantage: "A disproportionate amount of innovation comes from people that don't bring the baggage of the domain – they bring fresh perspective and the willingness to question dogma." Deep product knowledge ≠ long tenure in the industry.
- **Two guardrails Cagan states explicitly.** (1) Product sense **does not transfer between spaces** — believing it does "is just arrogance, not product sense." (2) It is **not a substitute for testing ideas**: "Product sense can definitely accelerate product discovery, but it's more like having a compass as you navigate the product risks." Both are useful when a senior leader invokes their own judgment to skip discovery.
- **The coaching line.** "There is no substitute for doing the homework" — non-negotiable for product leaders and founders, and the most meaningful differentiator between an entry-level PM and senior/group/principal levels.
