# Product Leadership Archetypes (Cagan's commentary)

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · SVPG article · August 27, 2025 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/product-leadership-archetypes/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Supporting — Cagan's own commentary on a framework the wiki already treats as primary elsewhere.** The three-archetypes framework itself (craftsperson / operator / visionary) is [[shreyas-doshi|Shreyas Doshi]]'s, first published as a 2021 Coda post and restated on *Product Therapy* Ep 26 ([[2025-08-21-idiodi-doshi-coaching-product-leadership-part-1|the wiki's existing primary source]], published six days before this article). This article is Cagan writing *in response* to that episode — do not treat it as re-rooting the archetypes framework; it adds Cagan's own opinion on top of Doshi's descriptive taxonomy.

Cagan's added opinion, not present in the Doshi episode:
- **Craft is table stakes, not one of three equal choices.** Cagan: he doesn't care how strong a leader is as an operator or visionary if she isn't first "absolutely skilled and passionate about the craft of product" — weak craft in the leader is, in his view, the root cause of a large share of weak product organizations.
- **The visionary archetype is mainly a problem when there are two of them** — a visionary founder *and* a visionary product leader in the same room rarely lasts; most craft-strong leaders can supply vision themselves when needed (or exercise the muscle later, at their own company).
- **Operators split into two very different types**, and this is the nuance Cagan says the discussion usually misses: those who scale through **coaching people** (Cagan: "absolutely gold") versus those who scale through **process** — Steve Jobs's *Lost Interview* riff on "process people" as a substitute for thinking is Cagan's reference point here. See [[process-people]] for the wiki's fuller treatment of that distinction.
- **A practical decision guide.** Always require real craft first. If growth-stage-or-larger, add coaching/organizational-alignment skill for scaling the craft. If the founder is a strong visionary and wants to stay in that seat, coach the product leader to realize that vision; if not (or the founder has left), a strong-craft leader can usually supply the vision too.
- Closes with an explicit link back to [[founder-style-leadership|Cagan's own "founder-style-leadership" article]]: "the key to successful and effective product leadership is strong craft plus coaching."
