## Notes for the coach
- **The definition of a "good decision" is social, not just analytical:** one the team, executives, stakeholders and customers *can support and understand, even if they disagree*. Cagan is explicit that being right is insufficient.
- **The five behaviors:**
  1. **Right-size decision analysis** — weigh [[the-four-big-risks|risk]] × *consequence* (recoverable in hours vs. bet-the-company). Novices either under-estimate risk or over-estimate it and burn discovery time on things that don't matter. This is where Cagan says he spends most of his coaching.
  2. **Collaboration-based decision making** — not consensus, not voting, not benevolent dictator. Defer to the tech lead on enabling technology, the designer on experience, the PM-with-stakeholders on viability. *"The hardest decisions are usually around value, as value is a function of the whole."*
  3. **Resolving disagreements** — disagreement is normal and healthy; the PM's skill is knowing **when and how to run a test**, usually via the cheapest appropriate prototype. Do this and escalation becomes rare.
  4. **Transparency** — a short note for minor calls; the **written narrative with its FAQ section** for consequential ones. Cagan warns that PMs resist the narrative's rigor, and that resistance is precisely the signal it's warranted.
  5. **Disagree and commit** — the PM must commit publicly even when she personally disagreed, and may state her view; what she may not do is tell leadership she deferred to her tech lead but disagrees, or grumble to her team about a leadership call. He names both as *toxic*.
- **Contrast with feature teams:** in [[feature-teams-vs-product-teams|feature teams]] the meaningful decisions were already made upstream — pushing decisions down to the team is the point of empowerment.
