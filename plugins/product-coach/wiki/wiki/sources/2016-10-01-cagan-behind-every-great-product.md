# Cagan — Behind Every Great Product (essay)

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Behind Every Great Product" · svpg.com, 2016-10-01 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/behind-every-great-product/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — the root essay.** The 2016 article that Cagan describes as *"the one I'm most proud of"*, drawing on the *[[inspired|INSPIRED]] 2nd ed.* PM back-stories he compiled. Inspired by Ben Horowitz's *Good Product Manager / Bad Product Manager*. This essay is the **root** of the "three ways teams run a PM" canon and of the six iconic PM stories that the later talks and books recycle. Previously the wiki's [[2018-06-07-cagan-behind-every-great-product-mtpcon-london-2016|2018 talk card]] carried the material as if it were root; the 2018 talk should now be treated as a spoken **restatement** of this essay.

## What it backs
- competencies: [[product-manager]] — the **three ways PMs work** framework (only one leads to success):
  1. Escalate every issue/decision up to the CEO → *"backlog administrator"*, the CSPO trap.
  2. Call a meeting with all stakeholders and let them fight it out → design-by-committee; the PM is really a project/roadmap manager.
  3. **Actually do the job** → the only one that leads to success.
- competencies: [[product-manager]] — the **PM-as-CEO analogy** (with the difference that no one reports to the PM), the **four qualities** of a strong PM (*smart · creative · persistent · plus grit*), the **deep-knowledge areas** (customer / data / business / product), and the *"no MBA required"* observation (*"actually not one of the impressive product managers I described has an MBA"*).
- concepts: [[product-management-theater]] — the "three ways" list is the **origin** of the theater taxonomy: modes (a) and (b) are what [[2024-01-30-cagan-product-management-theater|the 2024 theater article]] later names Product Management Theater. Attribute the *label* to 2024; attribute the *diagnosis* to this 2016 essay.
- case studies (six iconic PM stories, all told for the first time in public here):
  - **Word for Mac (1993, Microsoft) — [[martina-lauchengco|Martina Lauchengco]]**: turning around the beta-slow, hate-mailed release; the 6.1 patch signed by Lauchengco. *"There's little as powerful as a marketing person that's also strong at product."*
  - **Netflix (1999) — Kate Arnold**: subscription pivot, queue + rating + recommendation engine; 3-month redesign; billing rewrite hidden behind the 30-day free trial. Cagan's "Netflix as we know it" attribution to product judgment. Also: the Blockbuster $50M refusal.
  - **Google AdWords (2000) — Jane Manning**: side-of-results placement + click-through-rate weighted auction; got Georges Harik on board to break engineering resistance; Manning wrote the first spec.
  - **BBC Mobile / BBC Out Of Home (2003) — Alex Pressland**: IP-syndication → out-of-home displays → BBC Mobile; force-of-will change at an editorial-heritage institution; done *as an IC PM*.
  - **Apple iTunes / American Idol integration (2008) — Camille Hearst**: DRM-free transition, Idol partnership with the vote-influence problem carefully engineered around.
  - **Adobe Creative Cloud (2011) — [[lea-hickman|Lea Hickman]]**: perpetual-license → subscription transition; the ~$2B Creative Suite → Creative Cloud pivot; sustained internal evangelism campaign; prototypes with CTO Kevin Lynch. Backs [[adobe-creative-cloud-transformation]] directly.
- concepts: [[the-product-team-trio]] — the essay's closing insistence: *"the winning solutions didn't come from users or sales; rather great products require an intense collaboration with design and engineering to solve real problems."*
- entities: [[marty-cagan]] (the essay he calls his most-proud-of), [[martina-lauchengco]], [[lea-hickman]].

## Notes for the coach
- **Root vs restatement.** The [[2018-06-07-cagan-behind-every-great-product-mtpcon-london-2016|2018 MTP London talk]] uses this essay's material live; treat that card as **supporting** for the *three-ways* framework and the PM stories, and this article as the **root**.
- **The five closing takeaways** Cagan lists at the end of the essay are the cleanest single-source statement of what the PM role is:
  1. PM is *distinct* from designer and project manager — stop debating "overlap" with design.
  2. PM must deeply understand the business (the CEO analogy) — but without needing an MBA.
  3. Winning solutions come from *design + engineering + product* collaboration, not from users or sales.
  4. Strong PMs are the best versions of *smart, creative, persistent* — plus grit.
  5. **True leadership is a big part of what separates the great from the merely good** — regardless of title/level.
- **The six stories back several case-study pages.** Explicitly for [[adobe-creative-cloud-transformation]]. Implicitly for anywhere the wiki cites iconic PMs by name — Lauchengco's Word for Mac and Hickman's Creative Cloud are on their people pages already; check that they cite this essay as their root, not just the 2018 talk.
