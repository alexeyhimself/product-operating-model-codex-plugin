# Product Therapy Ep 23 — Coaching Product Myths (Part 2)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[chris-jones|Chris Jones]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 23 · SVPG, June 26, 2025 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8lpoyCmCBBg). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the second half of Jones's "misconceptions in product" pack.** Together with [[2025-04-03-idiodi-jones-coaching-product-myths-part-1|Part 1 (Ep 17)]], the two episodes ground the [[product-myths]] synthesis page with 9 myths total.

**Myth 5 — "Product discovery will give us the answer."** Correction: **discovery is not a formula, recipe, or process — it's a set of tools that inform your judgment.** People love this misconception because it lets them *replace* their judgment with a process ("I followed discovery, product didn't work, must be the process"). Jones: *"you right-size discovery based on the actual risks present and the level of confidence you need — sometimes qualitative validation with a half-dozen customers is enough; sometimes an internal opinion is enough."* Idiodi's illustration: PM asks "how do I do discovery?" — actual issue = the site is down. Direct manager's job = spot when a PM is *grinding metal* (weeks of tweaking a prototype) and call it: *"you're grinding metal right now — let's step back."*

**Myth 6 — "We need to iterate on a solution until it succeeds."** Correction: some grit is valuable; runaway sunk-cost pushing a rock uphill is not. Product teams don't recognize they're running against a **clock** — the moment the business gives you a problem, there's a race for the value of the team. Idiodi's diagnostic: *"when a company says 'we have an innovation team,' that literally means my product team is not innovating."* Acquisitions are the same signal: *"I've not met a single CEO who tells me 'if I thought we could build this better and quicker ourselves, we wouldn't have bought this company.'"*

**Myth 7 — "Product managers need product owners."** Correction: the PO/BA role is a **historical artifact of the waterfall→agile transition** — companies changed the title but not how they worked. **Two people doing one job = handoffs = command-and-control by another name.** Jones's crisp reframe: **inside game vs outside game.** Inside game = engineers, stories, technical decomposition. Outside game = customers, markets, discovery, stakeholders. Both must live *in a single skull.* Splitting introduces communication artifacts, which is exactly the waterfall pattern the shift to agile was supposed to escape. **The PO role has no career path in a serious product company** — Idiodi's rule: *"if your title is Product Owner, no serious company in the product model will hire you — because it's a role on an agile team, not a job."* Idiodi's cost argument: 10-person team = ~$1.5–2M/year in salaries = ~$55–65k/sprint — that is not a decision to leave to a junior person or split across two roles. Jones's Part-3 addendum: PM's job isn't 4 jobs just because you now own discovery too — the *delivery load should be less* under the product model, because well-informed discovery removes wasted delivery.

**Myth 8 — "If you build it, they will come."** Correction: product teams are responsible for the *whole* solution, not just the product's functionality. **Go-to-market is part of the solution.** How do customers learn the product exists? How do they get it? What entices them to buy? These may involve sales, marketing, or the product itself (product-led growth). Applies to internal products too (how do employees find and adopt an internal tool?). A PLG product and a direct-sales product are *dramatically different products* — ignore GTM at your peril; it changes what needs to get built. Jones's admission: *"I was an idiot on this when I started — I got coached into being much more engaged with sales and marketing."*

**Myth 9 — "The product manager is the CEO of their product."** Correction: **the *idea* behind it is right; the *power* interpretation is wrong.** Idiodi's flip: *"the PM is the opposite of Spider-Man — with great responsibility comes no power."* The right reading: PM has holistic understanding of the company, deep sales/marketing/stakeholder relationships, an outcome-oriented mindset — like a CEO would. The wrong reading: PM has license to bark orders, control the team, be the boss. Everything on this podcast is a *team sport*; the PM's role is elevated because of value responsibility, not because of authority.
