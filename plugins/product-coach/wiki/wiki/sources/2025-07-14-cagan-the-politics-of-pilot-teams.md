## Notes for the coach
- **Why pilots matter, restated with more force than earlier canon:** a pilot team is "the MVP for a full transformation" — proves the model can deliver business results, in a quarter or two, cheaply (1-2 teams), safely (without risking the bulk of revenue). Its secondary purpose is showing the rest of the org "what good really looks like," since most people assume they already have the competencies/concepts/techniques because they don't know what they're missing.
- **Staffing, in this article's own words:** hand-pick the team as a "bar-raiser," not an average team. Bring in outside skills if needed, or fast-track high-potential internal people who *want* to work this way — people who hate change are disqualified. Keep the team small: role clarity, coaching, communication, decision-making, and dependencies are all easier and faster smaller. A large "new-roles" pilot team reads to stakeholders as cost-insensitive.
- **Problem-scoping, in this article's own words:** the sweet spot is "impressive but not impossible" — a problem the CEO would agree was unlikely under the old model, but not so hard a strong team would still likely fail. Trust stakeholders on the *problem* (they've lived with it for years); the team earns trust by discovering and delivering the *solution*. Avoid problems dependent on undone tech-debt/replatforming work outside the team's control.
- **Beyond the pilot — the new point this article adds:** if the pilot succeeds, the natural next request is company-wide rollout — but in large companies, **treat each business unit as essentially its own transformation**, especially where business units are the product of earlier acquisitions. One successful pilot does not automatically transfer trust across business-unit boundaries.
