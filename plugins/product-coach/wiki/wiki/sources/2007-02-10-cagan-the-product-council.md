## ⚠️ Contradiction flag — do not cite as current practice
This article describes a **stage-gate governance process** that the product operating model explicitly rejects. Specifically, its four milestones are:
1. review proposed product strategies and roadmaps; initiate opportunity assessments;
2. review the opportunity assessment; **go/no-go decision to begin creating spec**;
3. review prototype, user-testing results and detailed cost estimate; **go/no-go to begin engineering**;
4. review final product, QA status, launch plans; **go/no-go to launch**.

Each of these conflicts with current canon: the model has **no spec phase** (see [[prototypes]] on prototype-as-spec and [[2010-10-14-cagan-requirements-are-not|Requirements Are Not]]), **no discovery→delivery gate** (see [[2020-10-30-cagan-discovery-delivery|Discovery – Delivery]] — one team owns both, continuously), and **no committee issuing build authorisations** to an empowered team ([[empowered-with-problems-to-solve]], [[trust-over-control]]). The article also assumes a **roadmap of committed releases** ([[outcome-based-roadmap]] supersedes) and uses masculine-default pronouns for the product manager throughout, which dates it further. Cagan's own later positions — [[2019-08-29-cagan-product-vs-feature-teams]], [[2018-06-30-cagan-revenge-of-the-pmo]], and the retrospective [[2026-09-11-cagan-strong-opinions-loosely-held]] — are the current word.

## Notes for the coach
- **What survives.** The article's *underlying* problem is real and still current: "Every product company needs a mechanism to get the key stakeholders and decision makers together to make timely and informed product decisions," and the stated design constraint is strikingly modern — provide "the visibility and oversight that management needs and is responsible for, without the micro-managing (and disempowerment) that comes with company executives trying to design products." The modern answer to that same problem is the strategic-context + [[the-pledges|pledges]] + quarterly-review pattern, not a council with gates.
- **Also still sound:** keep the group to ten or fewer; "this is not a group to design or build products"; "these are not product design sessions — if there are issues with the product then the team should work on them and return"; encourage employees to bring ideas even unsolicited ("these organic product ideas are often the best kind"); and review business results 3–6 months post-launch so the group learns which of its own decisions were good.
- **Attribution.** Cagan credits the concept "as described here to **Maynard Webb**, the former COO of [[ebay|eBay]]," refined with several companies afterwards.
- **Estimation nuance worth keeping.** At milestone 2 nothing beyond "small, medium or large" is fair to ask of engineering because the solution isn't outlined yet — an early articulation of the idea that matured into [[high-integrity-commitments]].
- **Why card it at all.** It gives [[your-evolving-thesis]] a concrete, dated example of SVPG moving from process-and-governance thinking toward principles-and-empowerment thinking, and it prevents a future reader from finding the live URL and mistaking it for current guidance.
