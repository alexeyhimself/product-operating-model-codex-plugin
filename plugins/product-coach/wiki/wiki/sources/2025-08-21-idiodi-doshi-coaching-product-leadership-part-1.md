# Product Therapy Ep 26 — Coaching Product Leadership with Shreyas Doshi (Part 1)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[shreyas-doshi|Shreyas Doshi]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 26 · SVPG, August 21, 2025 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=caoT8B1-ccg). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the wiki's first external-guest teaching on product leadership as a role, with the three-archetypes framework and an interview method for hiring product leaders.** First of a two-part Doshi conversation (Part 2 → [[2025-11-25-idiodi-doshi-coaching-product-leadership-part-2]]). Introduces [[shreyas-doshi|Doshi]] as a wiki-primary voice on product leadership hiring alongside [[marty-cagan|Cagan]] and [[lea-hickman|Hickman]].

Key material:

(a) **Central thesis: nearly every product problem is a product leadership problem.** Doshi's frame: all problems are people problems, and people problems are leadership problems. Leadership is three buckets: *context* (clarity of where we're going, what's important, how we measure success), *culture* (the environment — language, behaviors), and *coaching* (getting people better at their job).

(b) **The signing-up vs feeling-responsible distinction.** *Signing up for outcomes* is a situational choice ("depending on your org's culture, don't sacrifice your career to an ideology — that's up to you"). *Feeling responsible for outcomes* is permissionless. Most leaders conflate the two: when they can't sign up, they wash their hands of feeling responsible — and their whole leadership style shifts.

(c) **The PM-leadership role definition** (specifically for PM leadership, not "product leader" broadly): *"make successful products via others and build self-managing teams."* Product leader vs PM leader can overlap or be distinct — a product leader may be an engineer or designer or business-side person. Core tasks: **create clarity** at macro and micro; **lead strategy and vision** (may not be sole formulator, but must own it); **product editing** (editor, not creator); **meta-execution** (structure the team can operate effectively within); **PM coaching**. Consistent-success skill spikes: critical/analytical/strategic thinking, product sense, influence, **listening** and cognitive empathy.

(d) **Head-of-product tenure data.** Roughly half are out of the role in 12–18 months. *"Assume the default outcome of this process is failure. Whatever actions you take need to prevent that default from manifesting."*

(e) **The three archetypes / three hats — the wiki's primary source for this framework.** Each product leader has one *preferred hat* (superpower + where they like to spend time):
- **Operator** — superpower: communication and influence. Excellent at scaling teams, cross-org alignment, unblocking execution. In product review, gravitates to *stakeholder validation* as proxy for correctness.
- **Craftsperson** — superpower: product insight. Understands customer needs faster/more accurately than anyone else and translates them into creative solutions. Obsesses in product review over the qualitative and quantitative insights informing the proposal.
- **Visionary** — superpower: sees what others cannot. Usually founders. Often frustrated in product reviews because they can see what's missing but struggle to articulate it (which is why the visionary often needs the craftsperson as a bridge to the team).

(f) **Product stage matters more than most CEOs realize.** Three stages: very early / growth / mature. A leader excellent at *mature* stage — great brand names, hundreds-of-millions revenue scale — brought into a *growth-stage* startup often stalls: they set up all the "right" systems (OKRs, review meetings, cross-functional org, staffing) and quarters go by with no progress. In a startup, a few quarters is like five years in a larger company.

(g) **How to identify a candidate's archetype — pay attention to the QUESTIONS they ask.** The first several questions reveal the hat they prefer, because we gravitate to what interests us most:
- Questions about **team structure, success criteria, goal-setting, planning processes** → **operator**.
- Questions about **the core product, segments, positioning, what's working vs not** → **craftsperson**.
- Excited **ideation about what the product could be**, big-picture "how we take over this market" → **visionary**.
Also watch **how the depth/quality of their questions improves** across the 2–3 month hiring process — which dimension deepens tells you the same thing.

(h) **The like-archetype trap.** *"I've made this mistake myself — when I didn't have appreciation for these finer points, I gravitated toward individuals of the same archetype as myself, in a way that let me validate to myself that I am good at the job."* Guard against hiring your own reflection when the company needs the complementary hat.

(i) **The MSN list — Doshi's replacement for the four-page JD.** Three bullets each under **Must have / Should have / Nice to have**. Constraints:
- Every bullet must be **concretely evaluatable** in the interview process or by background/reference check (or both). "Passion for building delightful products" is fluff — cut it.
- **Exclude table stakes** you want in every employee (high integrity, etc.). Include only what's specific to *this* hire.
- **Assign specific bullets to specific interviewers**. Each interviewer owns evaluating one M or S — interviews become focused, not "let's ask a bunch of questions we Googled 5 minutes ago." Some startups now require the CEO to sign off on the MSN list before any hiring req is opened for the entire company.

(j) **The 11-out-of-10 question.** Ask the hiring manager: *"9–12 months in, what is the one thing they should have absolutely done a spectacular job on — an 11 out of 10?"* It has to be significant (related to outputs, outcomes, or team; not "set up a good sprint process"). Most hiring managers admit they've never thought about it. Then communicate that one thing to candidates at the right stage in the process, so they know what you're actually expecting.

(k) **The founder-still-owns-what question.** Before hiring, get clear on *what aspects of the product you (as founder/CEO) will continue to own, consult on, or expect to be consulted on.* Don't assume "they should own strategy" — ask honestly whether *you* are great at strategy and whether the company can spare you delegating it. Communicate the split to candidates in the interview process (not once they're hired). This is the main friction point that ends leadership tenures early; clarifying it up front prevents the downstream conflict.

(l) **Idiodi's closing recap** (worth preserving as a coaching sequence): start by forgetting the person and figuring out **why** you want this role → identify the **product stage** → identify the **archetype** that fits it → **test for that archetype** in the interview → write the **MSN list** → answer the **9-12 month 11/10 question** → decide **what you as founder will continue to own** and be upfront with candidates about that friction.
