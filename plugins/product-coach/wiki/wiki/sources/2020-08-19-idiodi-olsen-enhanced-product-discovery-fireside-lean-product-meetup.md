# Idiodi & Olsen — Enhanced Product Discovery Fireside Chat (Lean Product Meetup)

> **Citation:** Christian Idiodi (SVPG) in conversation with Dan Olsen, *Fireside Chat on "Knowing What You Can't Know: Enhanced Product Discovery"*, Lean Product Meetup, delivered 2020-07-23, published 2020-08-19 · YouTube — [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jYRqfIOqg1o). Slim provenance card; the Q&A transcript is **not reproduced**.

## Role
**Supporting** — the fireside/Q&A companion to [[2020-08-12-idiodi-enhanced-product-discovery-lean-product-meetup]] on the same evening. The talk itself is the primary root; this session is the audience-question follow-up. Kept as a separate card (not merged into the talk card) because the Q&A introduces several distinct points the prepared talk does not — most useful when a coach is looking for Idiodi's answer to a specific *how do we apply this* question.

## What it backs
- frameworks: [[reference-customer-program]] (the **recruiting funnel** — needing to talk to ~50 people to end up with 6–8 references; "cast a wide net"; **pricing discipline** — whatever you charge references becomes your market price, so the pricing team must discover pricing with the same reference customers, using non-binding letters of intent for B2B and taking-a-credit-card-and-handing-it-back for B2C; the technique applied to **platform / non-customer-facing teams** — your customers are the other product teams, the Apple back-end example he did not have time for in the main talk)
- concepts: [[prototypes]] (the **MVP-as-prototype clarification** — *"an MVP is not a product, it's actually the smallest experiment that you can come up with to validate things"*; better named a *minimum viable prototype*; the whole spectrum of what B2B teams argue counts as an MVP — sellable / usable / merely showable — with only the last two actually being MVPs), [[product-discovery]] (**discovery vs delivery balance for small teams** — no more than an hour a day in delivery for a PM; **30 minutes/day for engineers in discovery** playing with next-quarter prototypes and asking two questions: *"is there a better way to do this?"* and *"what concerns do I have?"*; the fireside is where the specific number comes from — the prepared talk asserts engineer involvement without quantifying it)
- competencies: [[engineers]] (the *"engineers up here"* frame — Alexa and 1-Click as engineer-originated ideas; the *"you hire smart people so they can tell us what to do"* Steve Jobs argument as the coach's move for engineer-in-discovery; the corollary — *"if all your engineers do is ship code, we're not getting half of the value of them"*), [[product-manager]] (**framing questions to leaders**, the coaching move: *don't argue for discovery, ask what they want → why → what does success look like*, which anchors them on value and never lets them name a feature again; the PM's ≤1 hour/day in delivery ceiling as a diagnostic of *poor* discovery, not of a light workload), [[product-designer]] (the **refrigerator analogy** for the PM-vs-designer research question: *"it's the job of the refrigerator to cool the drinks inside, not to drink the drinks inside — the designer's job is to get the research insights into the heads of the team, not to hold them"*; his answer to Jared Spool's PM-and-UX-merging hypothesis)
- diagnostics: [[stakeholder-relationship-health]] (**highly-regulated / risk-averse companies push back on discovery hardest and need it most** — they cover risk with process and bureaucracy instead of tackling risk with evidence)
- concepts: [[trust-over-control]] (**dysfunction → high-functioning** rests on the trust dynamic between team and leadership; trust = **competency + character**, and most orgs' problem is *perceived competency*, not character — the manager's job is coaching to competency)
- entities: [[christian-idiodi]] (his self-report: *"I build products every year… I pressure-cook discovery every day"* — the framing that motivates why the reference-customer technique is the one he tests everything against)

## Notes for the coach
- **The 30-minute-per-day engineer discovery slot** is the fireside's most useful operational number. Answering Cynthia's question about a trio with three additional engineers: don't pull all engineers into discovery; the tech lead is the trio's engineering seat, and the other engineers spend *30 minutes a day playing with a prototype of what's coming next quarter*, asking the two questions above. *"If 30 minutes for your engineers every day makes them miss your implementation magical deadline so much, you have a different cultural problem."* Cite from here when a team argues they can't spare engineering time for discovery.
- **The MVP-as-prototype clarification** matters because it's Idiodi's cleanest short version of an argument the SVPG book canon spreads across chapters. The reference-customer program *replaces* the MVP conversation: if you're going for a real reference set, you're going for [[market-fit|product-market fit]] on the smallest thing every one of them wants — no separate MVP step, no "we'll come back and tweak it" (which he notes people never actually do). Olsen's addendum — that B2B people often argue an MVP must be sellable — is preserved on the card because it names the industry misuse.
- **Pricing discovery** is one of the sharpest fireside additions. *"Whatever you charge your reference customers becomes your price in the market"* — because references talk to each other, and a launch at a higher price will be undercut by the reference-customer telling the market what they paid. So the pricing team uses the same reference customers to discover the price, alongside product. This anchors [[reference-customer-program]]'s pricing paragraph.
- **The trust-and-coaching move** on dysfunction → high-functioning: trust breaks down at *competency*, not character; leaders don't empower teams because they don't believe those teams are competent to make the call. The fix is not a trust exercise, it's coaching: *"even the best athletes have coaches"* — the best team members need one too, and the manager owns it. This backs [[trust-over-control]]'s four-dimensions section from a different angle than [[2024-05-31-idiodi-hickman-coaching-trust|Ep 4]] does.
- **The framing-questions-to-leaders sequence** — *what do you want? → why? → what does success look like?* — anchors the leader on value and prevents them from ever naming a feature again. This is Idiodi's most compact coaching move for the *"the VP said we should do this so it must be right"* wrongful-attribution-of-value problem he flagged in the prepared talk. Cite from here on [[product-manager]] and [[coaching-vs-contracting]] whenever a coach is asked how to redirect a feature request from an executive without saying no.
- **The story-map-over-Confluence line** — story maps as living documents you add to after every customer conversation. Idiodi cites Jeff Patton by name for the technique. **Patton is not Cagan-endorsed** by the wiki's non-SVPG inclusion test (see `CLAUDE.md` §2) — like Steve Blank in the talk card, he is cited in passing from *inside this card* only, not built out.
- **Platform / non-customer-facing teams** — the Apple back-end team example (25 technicians across 6 busy Apple stores) that Idiodi did not have time for in the prepared talk. Same technique, different customer identity: find 6–25 *internal* customers (other product teams, or in Apple's case, in-store technicians) who all want the same thing and build only what every one of them wants. Anchors [[reference-customer-program]]'s platform-variant paragraph.
