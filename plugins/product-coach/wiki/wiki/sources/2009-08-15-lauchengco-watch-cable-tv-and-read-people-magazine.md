# Watch Cable TV and Read People Magazine

> **Citation:** [[martina-lauchengco|Martina Lauchengco]] · *Watch Cable TV and Read People Magazine* · SVPG, Aug 15, 2009 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/watch-cable-tv-and-read-people-magazine/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the earliest wiki-attested Lauchengco statement of *relevance as a marketing discipline***, and the article-form root for the **show-don't-tell** rule that *[[loved|LOVED]]* later formalizes. Sits chronologically between [[2008-02-21-lauchengco-whats-in-a-name|What's in a Name?]] (2008) and [[2011-01-12-lauchengco-great-products-not-just-the-product-guys-job|Great Products Are Not Just the Product Guy's Job]] (2011) in her SVPG run.

Key material:
- **The framing case: Palm Pre vs. iPhone.** Watching two smartphone ads back to back, Lauchengco could not tell what the Palm Pre *was* — an ethereal spokesperson, a glimpse of the device, "three unintelligible screens," and a closing shot of the case shut, "hiding the whole interface." The iPhone ad that followed showed the phone and the apps in action: "After 30 seconds, I was both more interested and educated." Her reaction is the argument: "I felt sad… really sad for Palm and all the hard-working people whose interesting product remained utterly mysterious to me, a potential early adopter and evangelist."
- **Relevance requires context.** "Products need to be relevant for people to pay attention, and that requires setting the proper context. If you don't understand your customers' context — how messaging appears relative to everything else around them — your marketing won't work." The ads mattered less on their own merits than sandwiched between movie and lifestyle-car spots competing for the same attention.
- **Why the pop-culture homework is actual work.** "Successful marketing requires knowing what's in the national conversation" — not current events, but the cultural context and the visuals and messaging competing for attention. Her jab at her own profession: "Technology marketers tend to be intellectual snobs about mass media, but at the end of the day, everyone — even your most elite customers — downshift their brains." Hence the title's instruction, plus YouTube as a way to see "exactly how much traction and audience something has found."
- **Create a hook.** "Like good pop songs, good marketing needs a hook." Especially in a crowded category, marketing must quickly communicate why anyone should pay attention — via story, a cool feature, or innovative visuals.
- **Show, don't tell.** The rule stated plainly: "be sure to SHOW not TELL your product's 'hook.' Watch this phone do this amazing thing vs. 'Look at our amazing phone!'" Because "people believe what they see and can judge for themselves. This is why infomercials and direct marketing never die — seeing is believing."
- **The other context-setters.** Beyond pop culture: critical reviews, category definition and its attributes, analyst reports, third-party studies, blogs, commentary and stories. Her instruction is to become "a student of what grabs your own attention."

## Notes for the coach
Useful against the most common messaging failure mode: copy that is accurate, internally agreed, and completely unintelligible to someone who does not already know the product. Lauchengco's test is not "is this correct?" but "if this appeared between two unrelated ads, would a stranger learn anything?" The show-don't-tell rule is the concrete fix and travels well into demos, launch assets and landing pages. Note the period detail — the 2009 Palm Pre example dates the piece, but the underlying claim (attention is won in a competitive context you do not control) is the same one she makes about AEO/GEO in [[2026-06-18-idiodi-lauchengco-product-marketing-in-the-ai-era|the AI-era episode]] seventeen years later.
