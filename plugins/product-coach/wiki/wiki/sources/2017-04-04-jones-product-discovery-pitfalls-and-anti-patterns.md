# Product Discovery: Pitfalls and Anti-Patterns

> **Citation:** [[chris-jones|Chris Jones]] · *Product Discovery: Pitfalls and Anti-Patterns* · SVPG, Apr 4, 2017 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/product-discovery-anti-patterns/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — Jones's named catalog of discovery anti-patterns**, complementary to Cagan's positive discovery techniques and predating [[transformation-anti-patterns]]'s later cataloging work by seven years. Provides language for "how discovery gets misused by teams that think they're doing it."

Key material — the anti-patterns (paraphrased):
- **Confirmation-Biased Discovery** — teams using discovery to rubber-stamp pre-existing ideas. Sign: number and nature of ideas the team began with closely matches what shipped.
- **Product-as-Prototype Discovery** — a "prototype" is confused with an early version of the shippable product ("the MVP"). Consequences: slow, expensive, engineering-heavy, solution-locked. Extreme version: confusing discovery with Beta testing.
- **Partial-Team Discovery** — the PM (or PM + designer) does "discovery" without the tech lead — feasibility risk gets skipped or discovered only in delivery.
- Additional pitfalls continue — insufficient customer time, treating discovery as a phase rather than a habit, etc.
