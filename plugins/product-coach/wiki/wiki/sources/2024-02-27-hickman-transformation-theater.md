## Notes for the coach
- **Seven named forms, in the article's own order:** (1) **Transformation Theater** itself — activity over results; showcasing training, reorgs, ceremonies, and tools while operating business-as-usual, often worsened by change fatigue from prior failed transformation efforts. (2) **Title Theater** — titles and job descriptions change (PO → PM, graphic designer → product designer, engineer → tech lead) while the same people do the same work; fatal without people who can assess real skill and coach toward it. (3) **Innovation Theater** — a dedicated "innovation team" absolves the rest of the org from having to innovate, and rarely gets its discoveries into customers' hands because the team that discovers must also deliver. (4) **Discovery Theater** — continuing to design and build every idea while calling it discovery; the tell is whether the team is discarding roughly half its ideas, not just producing wireframes. (5) **Agile Theater** — an army of Agile coaches, roles, and ceremonies with no resulting empowerment or release-frequency change; cites the "Fake Agile" framing (Forbes/Steve Denning). (6) **Strategy Theater** — "peanut butter" resource allocation to stakeholders dressed up as strategy; requires strong, skilled leaders willing to say no. (7) **Stakeholder Collaboration Theater** — product leaders treat the empowerment shift as a transfer of power *from* stakeholders rather than a move to genuine collaboration, and end up ignoring or merely humoring the people they need as partners.
- **The consequence Cagan/Hickman name explicitly:** transformation theater's most damaging cost is losing your *best* people — those who stayed hoping the transformation was real, trained up, and then leave for a company that already works the way they were promised.
- **Explicit good-faith framing:** none of these forms of theater are usually intentional — people follow the path of least resistance (avoiding change), and naming the pattern is the intervention.
- **When to cite:** use this article as the origin of "transformation theater" and its six named sub-forms (Title/Innovation/Discovery/Agile/Strategy/Stakeholder Collaboration); it predates [[2024-03-28-cagan-hardest-challenges-product-strategy-product-direction|Cagan's own later spoken use of "transformation theater"]] by a month.
