# Cagan — Transformation Fail

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Transformation Fail" · svpg.com, 2023-10-05 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/transformation-fail/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — the article that coins the "[[product-strategy|peanut-butter product strategy]]" phrase** the wiki uses in several places (diagnostics, product-strategy, stakeholder-relationship-health). It also names, ahead of *TRANSFORMED* (2024), the leadership-abdication pattern that causes most transformations to fail — leaders delegating transformation downward, misunderstanding the scope, and mistaking a technology-department reshuffle for adopting the [[product-operating-model|product operating model]].

## What it backs
- concepts: [[product-strategy]] (source of the *"peanut butter product strategy"* nickname — "spreading the companies resources thinly across the company"), [[transformation]] (the leaders-must-champion-it-personally frame; transformation extends far beyond the tech department; **root cause #10, "Looking For Love In All The Wrong Places," re-roots the page's "hiring a management consultancy" anti-pattern**, predating the previously-cited 2025 talk by ~18 months), [[transformation-anti-patterns]] (leadership delegating transformation; consultants without product-building experience), [[missionaries-vs-mercenaries]] (root cause #5, "Misunderstanding Role of Engineering" — outsourcing engineers as a commodity, taken to its logical conclusion)
- diagnostics: [[product-strategy-quality]] (Level 1 red flag — capacity smeared evenly across stakeholders rather than concentrated on a few bets), [[stakeholder-relationship-health]] (the "fair allocation" roadmap pattern), [[leadership-readiness]] (leaders who won't personally champion the change)

## Notes for the coach
- **Quote magnet — the peanut-butter phrase, verbatim:** *"in most companies they simply allocate resources to the many stakeholders across the company in what is known as the 'peanut butter product strategy' (spreading the companies resources thinly across the company)."* Attribute *this exact phrase* to this article; the *concept* is grounded across the SVPG canon (see [[product-strategy]]).
- **The contrast (verbatim):** successful companies pursue *"an inspiring product vision and a holistic, insight-driven product strategy"* — quote alongside the peanut-butter line when a leader asks "isn't a balanced allocation fair?"
- **Leadership-abdication pattern** — leaders delegate transformation downward, misunderstand technology's strategic value ("turning technology from a cost center to a profit center"), fail to develop product-role competencies, neglect product-leadership coaching, and hire consultants who have never built a product. Same failure mode Cagan later restates as the #1 transformation-failure cause in [[2025-02-06-idiodi-cagan-coaching-transformation-politics|Product Therapy Ep 14]] and treats book-length in *[[transformed]]*.
- **When to cite:** any time you use the peanut-butter phrase, cite this article as its origin (not *TRANSFORMED*, which restates it). For the broader diagnosis of *why* transformations fail, prefer *[[transformed]]* as the root and cite this article as an early supporting statement.
