# What's in a Name? Less Than You Think.

> **Citation:** [[martina-lauchengco|Martina Lauchengco]] · *What's in a Name? Less Than You Think.* · SVPG, Feb 21, 2008 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/whats-in-a-name-less-than-you-think/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local. One of Lauchengco's earliest wiki-attested SVPG articles.

## Role
**Primary — Lauchengco's brand-vs-rebrand SVPG article-form root.** The earliest wiki-attested statement of the *brand-as-promise* argument later formalized in [[loved|LOVED]] Ch 16.

Key material:
- **The rebrand myth.** *"Somehow 'brand' became magic pixie dust to sprinkle on company problems and make them go away."* Webvan's "rebrand into oblivion" as the cautionary tale.
- **Brand as promise.** *"A true brand is much more than a company's name, visual identity, or how much it advertises. A brand is a promise to customers that transcends any individual product or service."*
- **The best-brands examples.** McDonald's (cheap food fast), Starbucks (mid-afternoon coffee boost), Nordstrom (help finding the perfect tie), Apple (coolest new tech gadgets), FedEx (need it overnight).
- **The logo-churn observation.** *"Do you know how many logos these companies have gone through in the last five years? My guess is no, and you probably don't care."* — brand power ≠ logo redesigns.
- **Brand from the 4Ps.** *"Great brands are powerful because their relationship to us is emotional, not just rational... they come from the systematic delivery against every aspect of the 4Ps, starting with products (or services)."*
- **The consistent-product-experience proof.** YouTube, Facebook, FlickR, MySpace — succeeded via consistent product experience, not advertising or logos. Same for Google (search), Amazon (buy online), eBay (buy/sell people's stuff).
