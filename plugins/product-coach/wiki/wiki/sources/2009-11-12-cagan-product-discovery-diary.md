# Product Discovery Diary

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Product Discovery Diary* · SVPG, Nov 12, 2009 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/product-discovery-diary/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the corpus's only day-by-day worked example of what discovery actually feels like.** Everything else in the Discovery Series argues about discovery; this one *shows* it. Cagan is explicit that the scenario is constructed: *"I did fabricate this scenario however because I want to illustrate as many of the important points as I can."*

Its purpose, in his words: to help people moving *"from the very linear, Waterfall-based processes"* **appreciate and adapt to the fast pace and rhythm of product discovery.**

**What the two weeks contain** — the value is the *cadence*, not any single technique:
- **Day 1** alone: review the [[opportunity-assessment|opportunity assessment]] with the stakeholder, agree a set of [[product-principles|product principles]] with the lead designer and lead engineer, brainstorm, first-pass personas.
- **Prototype from day 2**, revised continuously — by the end of week 1 it has three key scenarios; by week 2 it has been through customer tests, visual design and re-scoping.
- **Customer contact is continuous, not a phase.** Screening calls on day 1; six [[reference-customer-program|charter customers]] signed by day 4; three separate customer test sessions inside week 2.
- **The trio works as a unit.** The lead engineer twice changes the product — killing an expensive idea and substituting a cheaper near-equivalent, then eliminating a whole data-collection requirement by deriving the data from what already exists. See [[the-product-team-trio]].
- **Stakeholder and legal are inside the loop, early.** The stakeholder attends a customer visit; legal sees the prototype in week 1 *"just to see if any of our ideas raised any flags."*
- **The MVP question, tested rather than argued.** They strip a "cool feature" to see whether it is essential, get a *"lukewarm response in terms of value,"* re-add it, and get the excitement back — so it stays.
- **The week-2 ending.** Commit dates from the lead engineer, within budget, no remaining high-risk areas — the [[2009-08-23-cagan-the-two-week-rule|two-week rule]] in practice.
- **Iteration is the norm, not a setback.** The honest beats are kept in: *"none of the users got as far as we had hoped, so we have some work to do."*

## Notes for the coach
The best single artifact to hand a team that agrees with discovery in principle and cannot picture the week. It is also quietly a **pace diagnostic**: a team whose prototype changes twice a month rather than twice a week can compare itself against this and see the gap without being told. Flag its age (2009) and its fabricated nature when citing — the rhythm holds up, the tooling references do not.
