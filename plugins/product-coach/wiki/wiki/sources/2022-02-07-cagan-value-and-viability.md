# Cagan — Value and Viability

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Value and Viability" · svpg.com, 2022-02-07 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/value-and-viability/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — the clearest single statement of *what the product manager is actually accountable for*, and why that accountability is the dividing line between a product team and a feature team.** Frequently linked from other SVPG articles as the canonical value-and-viability reference.

## Notes for the coach
- **The framing adage.** "When the product succeeds, it's because everyone on the product team did what they needed to do; but when the product fails, it's the product manager." Cagan attributes it tentatively — "I am fairly certain that I first heard this from Ben Horowitz many years ago, but I've no idea if he coined the phrase." Note this is a *passing attribution*, not an endorsement of Horowitz's work; the §2 non-SVPG boundary still applies and `good-product-manager-bad-product-manager` remains a documented non-target.
- **The role split, stated plainly.** On empowered product teams: the **product manager** is responsible for *value* and *viability*, the **designer** for *usability*, the **engineers** for *feasibility*. "Together we make a product."
- **The exclusion clause — cite this one often.** "Note that if you're a product manager on a feature team, then you're *not* responsible for value or viability (the stakeholder requesting the project or feature is), so this doesn't apply to you." This is the most compact SVPG test for whether someone is a PM in name only; it pairs directly with [[product-management-theater]].
- **Why value subsumes the other risks.** Cagan's argument: value "is in no small part a consequence of viability, usability and feasibility" — so saying the PM is responsible for value means "we're counting on the product manager to ensure that *all* of these risks have been addressed." That's why the accountability doesn't decompose neatly.
- **The CEO's-eye view — the strongest passage.** If the PM won't or can't take responsibility for value and viability, leadership "ends up looking to others to take on that responsibility" — a stakeholder, a line manager, a GM or named business owner. "Unfortunately, now you're a feature team there to implement what that stakeholder or GM considers valuable." The point: **the feature-team model is often the consequence of an accountability vacuum, not of bad intentions.**
- **What viability concretely means** — Cagan's list: marketing must be able to market it, sales to sell it, finance to fund and monetize it, and the offering must be legal and compliant. And the corrective: "When a product manager says, 'not my fault' that sales can't sell, they are not only mistaken, but they are missing the point of their role."
- **Self-flagged as incomplete.** Cagan closes saying viability needs "another article, or more likely a series of articles" — which the [[the-pledges|Pledges trilogy]] (Sep 2022 – Jun 2023) and [[2023-07-10-cagan-product-risk-taxonomy|Product Risk Taxonomy]] (Jul 2023) go on to supply. Useful as a reading-order note.
