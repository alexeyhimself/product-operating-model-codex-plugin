# Cagan — Coaching: Collaboration

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Coaching – Collaboration" · svpg.com, 2019-08-10 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/coaching-collaboration/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** The coaching-series entry that defines collaboration *negatively* — it is **not consensus, not democracy, not artifacts, not compromise** — and then positively, as expertise-deference around a shared prototype. The root SVPG article for the "collaboration ≠ consensus" formulation that later pages restate.

## Notes for the coach
- **The four negations.** Not *consensus* (we defer to expertise, not agreement); not *democracy*; not *artifacts* (a "requirement" ends the conversation and reverts the team to waterfall); not *compromise* (a mediocre experience plus slow performance plus dubious value = the team loses).
- **The positive form:** sit around a prototype so the designer can explore experiences, engineers can surface enabling-technology possibilities, and the PM can weigh privacy/sales/financial consequences — until a solution emerges that is valuable, usable, feasible *and* viable.
- **Prototypes and story maps do double duty** — they facilitate the collaboration *and* leave behind an artifact of the decisions. The artifact is the side-benefit, not the point.
- **Two named failure modes:** (1) the PM hasn't done her homework, so the team lacks the business context to solve the problem; (2) **arrogance** — a PM certain of her solution stifles collaboration and converts missionaries into mercenaries.
- **Extends beyond the trio** — the same stance governs stakeholders (not requirement-gathering, not dictating), executives (who care about everything, so homework matters more), and prospects (the customer-discovery-program technique).
- Cagan cites his own *Beyond Lean and Agile* for the "three critical characteristics of strong product teams," of which solving problems collaboratively is the second.
