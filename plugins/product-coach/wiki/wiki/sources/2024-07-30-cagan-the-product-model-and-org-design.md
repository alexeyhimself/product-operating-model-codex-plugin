# Cagan — The Product Model and Org Design

## Notes for the coach
- **The TL;DR, verbatim:** *"in most cases, a reorg is not necessary."* A reorg is tempting to leadership as a fix, but Cagan: *"it's simply rearranging the deck chairs on the Titanic"* — the real driver of outcomes is product-leader quality, team skill, and product culture, not org-chart shape.
- **The two named models + the hybrid.** GM (general manager / business-unit / single-threaded-leader) model — Amazon's choice, optimized for spinning up many new products/lines of business fast. Functional model — Apple's choice, optimized for a holistic UX across fewer, deeply-integrated products. Hybrid — the most common in practice, when a company has a portfolio of loosely related products but wants a shared platform's leverage.
- **The driver heuristic:** business strategy → product vision/strategy → org design (not the reverse). Loosely related product portfolio → GM model fits. Tightly integrated holistic experience → functional model fits.
- **The orthogonality claim, load-bearing for [[where-the-model-applies]]:** *"whether or not the company embraces the product model is orthogonal to the org design they choose."* Feature teams or empowered product teams can both exist inside either org-design model; neither model prevents nor guarantees product-model success.
- **Coaching implication:** don't let "should we reorg?" substitute for the harder, real transformation questions (leadership quality, team competencies, product culture). Every org design makes some things easier and some harder — same principle as [[team-topology|team topology]], one level up the structure.
