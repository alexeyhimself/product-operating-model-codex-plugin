# SVPG — Example PRFAQ (EMPOWERED Enterprise Case Study)

> **Citation:** SVPG · "Example PRFAQ" · svpg.com, 2022-05-03 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/example-prfaq/). Slim provenance card; the PRFAQ text is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** The **worked PRFAQ example** SVPG hosts alongside its [[2022-04-28-svpg-examples|Examples hub]] — a lightly-anonymised version of the enterprise-expansion PRFAQ from the EMPOWERED "Jobs Inc." case study (Ch on the enterprise-expansion pivot). Concrete artefact of the technique Amazon calls PRFAQ (a Press-Release-First 6-page narrative) that Cagan endorses as the strongest tool for considering *major new product offerings*.

## What it backs
- frameworks: [[opportunity-assessment]] — the PRFAQ **is** the SVPG-endorsed template for large opportunity assessment. The article's own frame: *"It is an example of a technique used to propose and consider a major new product offering."* Cite this card wherever the wiki mentions PRFAQ / written narrative / 6-page narrative.
- concepts: [[product-discovery]] — the FAQ half models **assumption-testing before commitment**. The example's Q1 identifies the primary risk (SMB → enterprise expansion failure) and proposes the Customer Discovery Program (CDP) as the mitigation — a 6–8 target-enterprise engagement to test the value / viability / feasibility questions.
- frameworks: [[reference-customer-program]] / customer discovery program (CDP) — the example describes CDP concretely: 6+ referenceable enterprise customers as the exit criterion; direct engagement to test go-to-market and integration constraints.
- concepts: [[the-four-big-risks]] — Q3 (professional services), Q4 (onboarding time), Q7 (legal/contractual) are viability-risk questions; Q1 (SMB→enterprise pattern of failure) is value risk; Q8 (technology impact) is feasibility risk. The PRFAQ walks the four risks explicitly through anticipated Q&A.
- frameworks: written narrative / 6-page narrative — the technique's mechanics: (a) press-release form focused on **customer benefits**, (b) FAQ answering *anticipated* stakeholder questions, (c) the reviewer's job is to judge whether the proposal has been carefully considered. *"This allows those reviewing the PRFAQ to judge whether the proposal has been carefully considered, and the various perspectives and concerns acknowledged."*

## Notes for the coach
- **The PRFAQ tests thinking, not sells.** Cagan's operative reason to endorse it: not that it produces a great press release, but that writing *anticipated* FAQ answers forces the author to surface constraints and stakeholders early. When coaching a PM using this, focus first on *"which questions have you not yet asked?"* rather than the release language.
- **Anatomy of this example** (short pointers, no reproduction):
  1. Context paragraph (fictitious "Jobs Inc." — an SMB jobs-marketplace considering enterprise expansion) — one screen.
  2. Press release — dated 12 months forward, customer benefits + two hypothetical enterprise-customer quotes (Bayer Pharma, Siemens) + a hypothetical CEO quote + a "we did not want to announce this until several large enterprises were running successfully" credibility line.
  3. FAQ — 8 anticipated questions ranging from strategic (SMB→enterprise failure precedents; direct-sales cost implications) to operational (professional-services scope, onboarding time, pricing, legal/contract, technology impact).
- **The Customer Discovery Program is the FAQ's structural spine.** Almost every unresolved question in the FAQ points to CDP as the mitigation — *"we plan to engage directly and intensely with 6–8 prospective enterprise customers."* This makes the PRFAQ inseparable from CDP; if a company adopts PRFAQ without CDP, they lose the mechanism by which the FAQ's open questions actually get answered.
- **Not a real press release.** The article opens with this warning: *"This document is not a real press release."* Add it to any wiki page that mentions PRFAQ so a reader doesn't mistake the example for something Jobs Inc. actually announced.
