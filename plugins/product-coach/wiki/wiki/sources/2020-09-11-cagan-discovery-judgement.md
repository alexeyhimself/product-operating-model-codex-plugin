# Discovery – Judgement

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Discovery – Judgement* · SVPG, Sep 11, 2020 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/discovery-judgement/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the root article for judgement as the load-bearing discovery skill**, and the clearest article-form statement of the anti-template stance that [[principles-over-process]] generalizes. Cagan calls this *"the single most common confusion I encounter."*

**The four areas where judgement is necessary** — a usable checklist, and the article's spine:

1. **Assessing risk.** The [[the-four-big-risks|four risks]] apply to everything, but *"that doesn't mean that all four risks are significant or equal."* Two dials, not one: **severity** and **consequence**. *"If it turns out that you judge something wrong, but the time and effort to correct that mistake is just a few hours of work, then that is a very different situation than when the mistake causes substantial lost revenue."* And the cost of over-caution is explicit: treat every risk as major and *"you'll move way too slowly"* and *"won't have the time you need for the items that truly are major risks."*
2. **Assessing necessary evidence.** A spectrum from *"qualitative opinions from a small group of users"* to *"statistically significant results in a discovery A/B test."* *"You could always insist on the highest standard of evidence… but with most products, you'll pay a very high price for that in terms of time."* His own default: save large-scale live-data tests for *"the truly risky, high consequence issues"*, and make the bulk of decisions on *"much faster, albeit lower confidence, qualitative tests."*
3. **Assessing scope of work.** Bug fixes → performance work → optimizations → features → projects → initiatives. *"Projects and initiatives are, almost by definition, risky"* simply because of the time and effort sunk. But *"even certain bug fixes have the potential to cause real damage."*
4. **Assessing necessary documentation.** Provide what engineers need and *"nothing beyond that."* The named failure mode: *"companies end up creating a template of this sort of supplementary documentation, and eventually, what begins as very minimal documentation, grows to a big overhead that's rarely read anyway. So please don't do the template thing."*

**The trio point, twice.** The most common *"yet largely avoidable"* problem is *"when a product manager judges technical feasibility risk, without consulting the engineers"* — named as the root cause when work takes far longer than anticipated. Same for leaning on the designer for usability risk, *"especially with complex workflows as is often the case in B2B software."* See [[the-product-team-trio]].

**Where judgement comes from.** *"This is one of those cases where experience is a real advantage."* For a newer team member *"we depend on the person's manager to provide the necessary coaching on developing this judgement"* — the hand-off to [[product-coaching]] and [[product-sense]].

## Notes for the coach
The antidote card for a team that has industrialized discovery — a fixed template per feature, the same battery of tests regardless of stakes. Cagan's framing gives permission to *skip* discovery on low-risk, low-consequence work, which teams rarely believe they are allowed to do. The "don't do the template thing" line is quotable verbatim in a working-agreement discussion.
