# Strong Opinions, Loosely Held (Cagan, Sep 2026)

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · *Strong Opinions, Loosely Held* · SVPG, 11 Sep 2026 (the narrative version of a keynote for the [Lenny & Friends Summit](https://www.lennyssummit.com/)) — [read it at svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/strong-opinions-loosely-held/). This is a **slim provenance card**: the raw file stays local (gitignored) and is **never reproduced** here. Follow the link for the real thing.

## Role
**Primary (first-party Cagan/SVPG).** Unusually, this is a **first-person retrospective on his own published work** — Cagan answering the question *"what did you used to argue was true, that you no longer believe?"* He draws the line at the 1st edition of [[inspired|INSPIRED]] (2008) and lists **the ten biggest things he's learned since that represent a significant change from what he had publicly argued**. That makes it a rare source that is *itself* about lineage and correction — its natural home is [[your-evolving-thesis|the wiki's evolving-thesis page]], and it confirms/extends several corrections the wiki was already tracking. Feedback on early drafts credited to [[chris-jones|Chris Jones]], [[teresa-torres|Teresa Torres]] and [[shreyas-doshi|Shreyas Doshi]].

## The ten (his own numbering)
1. **Business viability** — "I completely understated the importance of business viability." In INSPIRED 1st ed the risks were only value/usability/feasibility; viability was buried under feasibility until the 2nd ed. Now, especially for AI products (cost, monetization, liability, ethics), it's essential — the real superpower is deep viability understanding *from a systems-thinking perspective*.
2. **Problem discovery** — he underestimated how strongly people would over-index on **problem** discovery (becoming gatekeepers over "is this a problem worth solving") and leave too little time for **solution** discovery. "When a product fails, it's almost always because the solution … just wasn't good enough."
3. **The wrong "why?"** — the *why we're working on this* is already spelled out by vision + strategy and is the *easy* why. The important, neglected one: **"why are people using — or not using — our product?"** — churn/retention learning almost nobody does.
4. **Humility** — "knowing what you can't know, and admitting what you don't know." His "CEO of the product" message was "not exactly a message of humility"; the lack of it (believing you know the answer when you don't) is a root cause of failed products.
5. **Predictability** — he "didn't appreciate just how powerful and deeply rooted the desire for predictability was," and how at odds it is with humility, innovation and outcomes. Roadmaps and PRDs are *enablers for thinking we know more than we really do*; predictability is at the root of output focus and of the lack of trust.
6. **Politics** — "I tried to ignore the importance of politics … That was naive." Politics hides behind "stakeholder management / alignment"; most of his content in the past three years is about politics or AI.
7. **Product leadership** — INSPIRED focused on teams, "with very little on the role of product leaders." The consequence: companies thought setting up empowered teams was enough. "Empowered product teams don't require *less* management, they require *better* management" — at minimum vision + strategy. EMPOWERED tried to close the gap.
8. **Corporate governance** (not PMO/process governance) — he wrongly thought a product leader needn't spend time here. Great products don't guarantee great companies; success makes you a target for people with different motivations. **Today he coaches product leaders to work proactively with the CEO on a governance structure and culture durable enough to succeed on the product vision.** Cites Eric Ries's *Incorruptible*.
9. **Competition** — "product is not as genteel as I made it sound." Solving the problem isn't enough; you must solve it *significantly better than the competition*. "For commercial products competing in the open market, product is a **full-contact blood sport**" — especially with AI's many fast-moving competitors.
10. **Thinking** — "good product work is about thinking, and I simply did not appreciate the lengths people would go to in order to avoid thinking." Structuring INSPIRED as "people, process, product" fed the hunger for process-as-substitute-for-thinking. He should have called out **thinking** and its foundation, [[product-sense|product sense]], much more loudly. Worry: LLMs used as an alternative to thinking rather than a thought partner.

**Conclusion — why he's still bullish.** The two standing objections to the model (companies addicted to output/predictability; the skills are too hard to develop) are both *weakened* by AI: the futility of output-focus is clearer than ever, and it's dramatically easier to **build-to-learn**. Falling delivery cost only *increases* the relevance of the model's principles, of product strategy, and of the craft of discovery.
