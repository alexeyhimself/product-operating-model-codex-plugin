# Product Therapy Ep 43 — Understanding Cultural Debt (Mike Fisher)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[mike-fisher|Mike Fisher]] (CEO, MyFitnessPal; former CTO, Etsy) · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 43 · SVPG, August 20, 2026 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8ob3QipukAM). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the wiki's canonical treatment of *cultural debt*.** Fisher's second Product Therapy appearance (after Ep 40, [[2026-05-21-idiodi-fisher-coaching-future-of-product-engineering-ai]]) — where Ep 40 was macro AI-and-work, this ep introduces the *cultural debt* frame as the analog to tech debt, along with the signals to detect it, how to pay it down, and the leadership behaviors that let it silently accrue.

Key material:

(a) **Defining culture and cultural debt.** Fisher: *"Culture is the system that governs how people work together. It covers the trust we have with each other, the relationships, how we communicate, how we make decisions. Cultural debt is the hidden cost of neglecting those systems. Every time we build products we'll have technical debt. When we make decisions we'll embrace some cultural debt. That's OK. The problem is when we ignore the interest on that debt."*

(b) **A real-world MyFitnessPal example.** Fully-remote company, HQ in Austin, office in NY. Fall of last year the leadership decided to *skip* the all-company gathering to focus on shipping features before the January signup surge. *"That's cultural debt. We didn't name it — but for a remote team, getting together renews relationships, renews friendships, builds trust. By not doing that, we incur debt."* Fisher's paying-down move: at every monthly all-hands, he now speaks for 10 minutes on a topic (the *"School of Fish"* segment).

(c) **The everyday compromises that quietly build debt.** Wells Fargo in the early 2000s: *"externally proclaimed customer-first, internally rewarded sales quotas."* The gap eventually caused people to sign customers up for fake accounts to hit quotas — millions in fines, lost trust. *"You say one thing and reward something else. That is a cultural debt. It doesn't break the first time — but if you have values and then reward something else with money, promotions, or attention, people notice."*

(d) **What you reward always beats what you say.** *"The higher you go, you don't realize how much people watch what you do. They watch what you say, who you meet with — they notice tiny little things. That gives signal. If that is different from the values you claim, people notice, and that becomes cultural debt."*

(e) **Wartime rationalizations.** *"It's OK to say 'we're in wartime, we're in a crisis, we've got to do something for a short-term gain.' If you keep doing that, it becomes the culture. Public companies under quarterly-earnings pressure often get stuck in this cycle — talking about long-term thinking and big bets while only worrying about optimization. Eventually you run out of things to optimize. You've got to stop the wartime and let people think about bigger problems."*

(f) **Ben Horowitz's book** (referenced by Idiodi): *"What You Do is Who You Are."* Fisher: *"Culture debt is inevitable — but you have to pay it back."*

(g) **The Idiodi scaling framework.** *"I think about scaling from a people, process, and technology perspective. Every company is scaling — 5 to 50 employees, 50 to 500 customers. If we scale technology too late, everything falls over at 500 customers. Too early — wasted money. Organization is the same — three layers of management for five people is bureaucracy; one layer for 5,000 is confusion. Culture debt is the same. You've got to keep paying it down because you keep incurring it."*

(h) **Signals of unhealthy cultural debt.**
- **Silence in rooms where there used to be debate.** *"When people stop pushing back and just go 'yeah,' that's a signal we're getting too much cultural debt because people aren't comfortable — they don't have the trust or relationships. They don't understand that 'oh, you could push back on Fish, you could say I disagree with that.'"*
- **Increased translation work.** *"When middle management or line managers hear you say something and go 'well, this is what he really meant when he was saying this.' When those phrases start appearing, that's an indicator that cultural debt is building."*
- **Losing great people.**
- **Impacting customers.**

(i) **The Boeing example — how long cultural debt can last.** *"For decades Boeing was synonymous with engineering excellence. Priorities shifted to cost efficiencies. Cultural debt built up. Safety concerns were minimized. Engineers were discouraged from raising issues. Internal dissent seemed like inconvenience. Tragically, two 737 Max crashes. And what's interesting: years later, Alaska Airlines' 737 Max came off the factory line missing critical bolts in one of its doors. That shows how long cultural debt can last."* Wherever product touches lives — marketplaces where sellers make a living, payments, health — cultural debt has real customer impact.

(j) **Blameless postmortem for cultural failures too.** *"If we start with the premise that people come to work to do good work — 99.99% of us come to work saying 'I'm going to do the best I can' — a postmortem is usually about 'something surprised me.' Culture debt's the same. These engineers, designers, mechanics at Boeing were trying to work within a system. Things got ignored. Ultimately it's a leadership problem — 100%. But it's not the people. It's not the mechanic who didn't put the bolts on. It's all the things that led up there for years."*

(k) **Culture is a C-suite responsibility, not an HR problem.** *"You can't go to your HR leader and say 'fix our culture.' This should be a C-suite issue that people at the highest level own — by the way, what they say, the walk they do, what they measure, what they reward, who they talk to, who they praise, the projects that get pushed forward. All of those are indications, and that is how we change our culture."*

(l) **"No More Mr. Nice Guy" — avoidance disguised as kindness.** Fisher's blog title / Idiodi's summary: *"The problem is not kindness, it's avoidance disguised as kindness."* Coaching culture vs conflict avoidance is a leadership fork — the former is candor with care, the latter is silence with a smile.

(m) **Psychological safety as candor without fear** — Fisher's version of the Ep-22 ([[2025-06-12-idiodi-leto-coaching-psychological-safety|Kate Leto psychological safety]]) frame: not the absence of conflict, but the presence of candor that people trust won't be punished.

(n) **The Fish model for paying down culture debt.** Concrete moves the ep bills as Fisher's operational framework (see timestamps): building a **challenge network**; defining **behaviors instead of slogans**; making culture a **C-suite responsibility**; the leadership behavior that builds trust fastest is *"walking the walk over talking the talk."*

(o) **Is a company like a family?** Fisher pushes back on the metaphor — sports team is a better model — but the piece of the family metaphor worth keeping is *long-term shared commitment* to how we treat each other.

(p) **The culture Fisher is building right now** — closing segment. The specific practices he leans on at MyFitnessPal today (see full episode).
