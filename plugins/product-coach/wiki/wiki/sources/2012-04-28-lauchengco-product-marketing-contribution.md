# Product Marketing Contribution

> **Citation:** [[martina-lauchengco|Martina Lauchengco]] · *Product Marketing Contribution* · SVPG, Apr 28, 2012 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/product-marketing-contribution/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — Lauchengco's Jane-and-John parable defining what product marketing is and isn't.** The archetypal story that ends with the PM realizing marketing doesn't get the product. Article-form root for the *PM/PMM boundary in embryo* that later becomes LOVED Ch 8's PM/PMM partnership framework.

Key material:
- **The parable.** Jane (marketing team, launch checklist) meets John (product manager). John draws the segmentation, explains features. Jane sends: press release with mixed-up features, no positioning, "Web 2.0 robot" quote. John rewrites. Website copy: John's corrections aren't in it; a copywriter re-broke things. Bob (head of marketing) assures John "we're in great shape" — because everything on the launch checklist is on schedule.
- **The diagnosis.** Confusion or frustration familiar? *"You are not alone and probably suffering from the need for Product Marketing."*
- **The distinction.** *"What makes Product Marketing distinct from corporate marketing is its specific focus on using products—most technology companies' greatest asset—to drive market strategy and growth."*
- **The scaling condition.** When a company is a startup with one product, marketing ≈ product marketing. The need for PMM as a distinct discipline becomes clear when the company branches into multiple product lines or gets highly technical or complex.

## Notes for the coach
When someone asks *"what's the difference between marketing and product marketing?"* the Jane-and-John parable is the sharpest single-article answer. Attribute it to Lauchengco. Note the scaling condition — for many one-product startups, this article's answer is *"you don't need PMM yet; the founder + PM cover it"* — cite this article when advising against premature PMM hiring.
