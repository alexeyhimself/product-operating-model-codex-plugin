# Discovery – Learning vs. Insights

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Discovery – Learning vs. Insights* · SVPG, Sep 1, 2020 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/discovery-learning-vs-insights/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the opening article of Cagan's "common confusions about discovery" sub-series**, and a root source for [[insights]] and for the learning-theater anti-pattern on [[product-discovery]].

Key material:
- **The distinction.** Insights are *"learnings that we can leverage."* Quoting a leader he works with: *"insights are learnings we can take to the bank."* Learning happens every day; insights are what you are actually hunting.
- **The blunt thesis.** *"The purpose of a product team is not to learn. The purpose of a product team is to solve hard problems in ways our customers love yet work for the business."* Learning is *"the means not the ends."*
- **The sharpest consequence.** *"You can be a product team that's great at learning, but if your team doesn't know how to close the deal and solve the problem for the customer, you are not a strong product team."*
- **Three tiers of insight** — a useful escalation ladder: (1) an insight that fixes the solution to the problem at hand; (2) an insight important enough to feed [[product-strategy|product strategy]]; (3) an insight *"so profound that it causes us to revisit everything, possibly even changing the course of the company"* — a **vision pivot** (see [[2013-02-03-cagan-vision-pivots-vs-discovery-pivots]]).
- **The OKR anti-pattern, named explicitly.** On whether a team should have an OKR measuring learning in discovery: *"I push them to keep their key results as business results, and not fall into the trap of confusing the means with the ends."* Direct grounding for [[okrs]] and [[outcomes-over-output]].
- **Technique obsession as the general failure mode.** Teams *"get so excited about their personas that they obsess over the technique, and don't turn the technique into successful products. Or they get so excited about design thinking. Or jobs-to-be-done. Or their use of Agile."*
- **Why he wrote it.** Some people *"have simply rebranded their tired old product thoughts as product discovery"* — the same motivation behind [[2020-06-12-cagan-product-management-start-here|Product Management – Start Here]].

## Notes for the coach
The highest-leverage card for a team that is *busy* in discovery but not *moving* — lots of interviews, lots of "learnings" slides, no shipped outcome. Cagan's line that a great-at-learning team can still be a weak product team is unusually direct and lands well without further framing. Pair with the OKR point when a team proposes "run 10 customer interviews" as a key result.
