# Product Therapy Ep 34 — Coaching Pilot Teams (with Gabrielle Bufrem)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[gabrielle-bufrem|Gabrielle Bufrem]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 34 · SVPG, December 11, 2025 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sKip0Uwm44). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the wiki's most complete podcast-form teaching on the [[pilot-teams|pilot team]] pattern as the *entry vehicle* of a transformation.** Materially deepens [[pilot-teams]] beyond the SVPG articles and complements the concrete example in Ep 28 ([[palace-resorts-transformation]]) where Bufrem coached the pilot from setup to result.

Key material:

(a) **Pilot as spiral, not project.** *"Companies say 'let's do a transformation project' — that's exactly what we're trying to go away from. It's a spiral, something you're always evolving."*

(b) **Pilot = poster child, not prototype.** *"We're calling it a pilot team, but it's the poster child. It's a proof point to demonstrate what's possible with the right structure, the right context, the right coaching, to get outcomes."* Idiodi: *"It is actually not a prototype. It's a proof point."* Framing for leaders: *"the second you commit to moving to the product model, it's a race — a race to outcomes. You don't have the trust, you don't have credibility, we don't have any proof points."*

(c) **Trust debt, not zero.** *"We're not starting from actually not having a lot of trust — we're starting from **trust debt**. By the time we're brought in they've probably tried other stuff. So we really have the pressure of delivering something great."*

(d) **What makes a good pilot team — the ingredient list (Bufrem).**
- **The best people you have.** Best PM, best designer, best engineers. Sometimes you don't have a titled PM — take the *"person who will become your first PM."* PM traits Bufrem looks for: **empathetic, business-minded, great collaborator, very proactive, coachable.**
- **Clear scope** — the team needs to know exactly what they own; leadership needs to know; Bufrem needs to know.
- **Customer-centric problem area** — customer-facing enough that a win is visible, delivering value to customers.
- **Access to customers** (*"if we can't talk to them, how can we build for them?"*).
- **Access to data** (*"if we can't measure it, it becomes a feelings game. Execs' tech love language is Excel — you give them a well-populated Excel showing progress, we have a happy conversation."*).
- **A clear problem to solve** — worked with leadership team to nail before starting.
- **Ideal team size / topology** — one PM + one tech lead + one designer per team (or one designer per two teams).

(e) **Where to start — not the worst team, not the easiest.** *"I have a lot of companies that tell me 'these are our most problematic people, let's start there.' Why? This thing is hard enough as it is."* Also not the team where *"everyone already believes they're working this way — then it's just 'that was a win already.'"* Pick the middle: **meaty enough problem that doesn't break the business.** Time horizon: *"we have about 3 to 6 months to show some sort of results."*

(f) **Sponsorship — leadership fully bought in.** *"Would you want to go to the gym with me for two hours every day? Are you committing to that?"* Bufrem asks every leader-sponsor *"what's plan B?"* — the answer she looks for is *"there's no plan B, this needs to work."*

(g) **Bufrem's team-first coaching agenda.** Weekly 90-minute coaching with the pilot team. **First meeting: what it is to be a team, what our roles are, who we are as people.** Because before, design was agency-model, PM was list-taker, engineers were barely there. Only after that: what discovery even *is*, how to run it, how to test.

(h) **The two muscles the pilot has to build.** **The team's muscle: solving a problem** (they're good at delivering; solving is new). **The leaders' muscle: deciding which problems to solve with context** (they're good at control; context-not-control is new). *"Both need coaching."*

(i) **Swim team vs soccer team — Bufrem's transformation metaphor.** *Project model = swim team* — everyone swims their own lap, points get tallied. *Empowered team = soccer team* — you can't play without the goalie, you only win if you play together, there is no playing alone. Bufrem's caveat: *"Brazil has incredible players who somehow can't play together — stars are not enough. You need a constellation for people to shine."*

(j) **The pilot lives on an island — protect it.** *"There's an island and the mainland. Is there a bridge? Nope. There's a boat that goes very infrequently, and you need to be in charge of that boat."* Idiodi's protection roles for a leader-sponsor: (i) shield the team from random stakeholder requests and old habits; (ii) staff with the necessary range of skills; (iii) make sure they know what good looks like and what outcome; (iv) coach the leaders AND the team, because both haven't done this before.

(k) **"But this isn't the real world" — Bufrem's counter.** *"When someone's just learning to run you don't sign them up for the marathon. You teach them in a controlled environment. Then they get better and start being better at communicating with stakeholders — and the boats can start running more frequently."* Idiodi's judo move (turn every objection into coaching): *"So you're saying if you put a competent PM on something, we will get good results? So you're saying if you had more leaders that protected their teams from distractions, good things could happen here?"*

(l) **The pilot exposes what good work looks like — and companies already know how.** Idiodi: *"Every company has a story of the last time they did something amazing. When I ask them to play it back — 'everyone was in the room, we didn't care about titles, we all knew what the problem was' — they end with 'it was so stressful but we won.' And right after that they go back to how they've always done things. What I'm trying to say to people is: I don't want you to be pointed to a hero we can't replicate. I want you to be pointed to the environment, the context, the principles — because THAT is what a pilot is showing you."*

(m) **Signs a pilot is working.**
1. **Outcomes** — the numbers move.
2. **People want to join the team** — CEO starts getting emails.
3. **Teams catch themselves in old habits** — *"'No, that's how we used to do things, now we do something else.'"* Idiodi's frame: *"we're actually experimenting; there's more insights in decision-making; there's more focus; we're working more together and less in handoffs."*

(n) **How to know a pilot is *ready* to graduate — Bufrem's checklist.**
- The team has been through **at least one or two full discovery loops** with the coach — they've practiced end-to-end.
- They have a real **toolkit** (not a process) — things they can pull out when the situation calls for it.
- They can tell you the journey — *"where were we when we started, where are we now, how do you feel about continuing that?"*

(o) **What comes next — avoid pilot theater.** *"Pilot theater = the pilot works but nothing broader changes; you're stuck in infinite pilot cycle."* The critical combination is **coaching the leadership team as you coach the pilot team** — because you need to see what the team can't see from their vantage point, and the leaders start influencing the ecosystem. Idiodi's scaling move: *"if a leader has seen good work and knows what it takes, the fastest way to scale is literally move that leader to another part of your company — magic happens there because they know what the environment looks like."*

(p) **Dream Team — the responsibility framing.** Bufrem calls every pilot team she works with a **dream team**. *"There's some responsibility in being part of the dream team — what you learn, you're going to teach other people. You get this awesome experience, and now you become the multiplier."* The concrete example: a person who wasn't even a PM when the pilot started, now a director of product coaching other people.

(q) **Common mistakes — Bufrem's short list.**
- **Forgetting the middle-manager layer.** The pilot team's own manager (not the CTO/CPO who signed the pilot off) is scared: *"my job is changing but I'm not in the pilot team — how do I get looped in?"* Transformation needs to happen at all layers.
- **The "pilot team factory"** — deciding "this is the way" and instantly having hundreds of pilot teams. *"That's the opposite of a pilot team. That's just a bunch of teams."* Cookie-cutter kills the very learning the pilot pattern exists to create.
- **The "sacred golden child" pattern** — leadership starts throwing every hard problem to the pilot team because it works, without changing the rest of the company. Bufrem's counter: the dream team's responsibility is to *teach others*, not to be the only ones who can do it.

(r) **How to sell the pilot to executives** (Bufrem's short pitch): *"I know this is a lot of change and it's going to feel different. So let's start with a controlled experiment. If it works, we can do more and learn from there. We know our business is important, so we don't want to rock the boat on everything. Let's pick an area that makes sense and set it up for success. If that happens, we can do more."*
