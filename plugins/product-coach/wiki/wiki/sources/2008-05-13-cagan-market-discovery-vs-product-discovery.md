# Market Discovery vs. Product Discovery

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Market Discovery vs. Product Discovery* · SVPG, May 13, 2008 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/market-discovery-vs-product-discovery/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the article that separates *finding the opportunity* from *finding the solution*.** The oldest piece in the Discovery Series (2008) and the one that gives [[market-fit]] its upstream half.

- **The definitions.** Product discovery = discovering a product that is valuable, usable and feasible. **Market discovery** = *"all about identifying opportunities worth pursuing."* Sometimes the market is obvious and established (cell phones, MP3 players, weight-loss diets); sometimes it is *"undiscovered, unrecognized, and/or untapped"* (DVR, Twitter).
- **The myth it dispels.** *"It helps to dispel the myth that you can only win big by identifying new markets."*
- **The exemplar split — a genuinely useful 2×2 in list form:**
  - Strong at **product** discovery, rarely new markets: **[[apple|Apple]]** — *"Apple rarely discovers new markets. Instead, their magic is in product discovery – coming up with great products to serve well-established but unmet needs. The iPod, iPhone, and Mac are all great examples."* **[[google|Google]] search** in the same category — *"nowhere close to being the first to identify the search market, but they did a fantastic job on product discovery."*
  - Succeeded at **market** discovery first, then delivered on product discovery: **eBay, TiVo, Nintendo Wii.**
  - Strong at market discovery, weak at product discovery: the general case that makes fast-following viable.
- **The fast-follower argument.** *"There's nothing wrong with letting the big guys pay to develop a new market and then you come in with a product that actually delivers on the need"* — effective *"at least if your company is strong at product discovery."*
- **The non-tech exemplar: OXO.** Cagan cites OXO's president at the GEL conference on how they discover new markets — tapping aging baby boomers' latent needs into whole product lines. Their technique: *"They learn a great deal by watching consumers interact with everyday products, which has long been a favorite technique of mine as well."* A rare pre-2010 SVPG example of the model applied outside software.
- **The closing priority.** *"Since companies sell products, you must learn to succeed at product discovery. It doesn't do you any good to come up with all these great opportunities and then not deliver products that meet the need."*

## Notes for the coach
Useful against two opposite delusions. First, the team convinced it must invent a new market to matter — Apple and Google search are the counterexample. Second, the team that has found a real unmet need and assumes the hard part is done — Cagan's closing line is the correction. The OXO example is worth keeping in your pocket for non-software or hardware coachees, who often assume the model is a tech-only import.
