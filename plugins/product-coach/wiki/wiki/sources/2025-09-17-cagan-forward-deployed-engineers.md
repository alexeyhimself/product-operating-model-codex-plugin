## Notes for the coach
- **The core model.** Send empowered engineers (and, increasingly, other product creators — PMs, designers) to embed directly with a target customer, to deeply understand their environment, problems, and what solving them actually requires — then discover and deliver a solution that achieves the outcome.
- **Not a Palantir-only concept, despite the association.** Cagan explicitly frames the article as *not about Palantir* — Palantir just makes the model legible because of its scale (cited at >$400B vs. Accenture's ~$150B, as of the article's writing). He's careful to say Palantir is a **product company**, not a consulting/custom-solutions company, despite the surface resemblance.
- **Custom-solutions companies vs. product companies (the Accenture contrast).** Accenture: client dictates the spec, Accenture builds it, Accenture can't sign up for an outcome because the solution was dictated to them. Palantir: bets on itself to solve the customer's problem in a way that creates value for the customer *and* for Palantir's own product offerings — needs direct access to the client's users, stakeholders, and data to do this.
- **The scaling problem, named directly.** FDE-only orgs risk ending up with "thousands of large, bespoke solutions that would need to be maintained and improved indefinitely." The fix is treating it as a **platform product** problem: each FDE builds on the platform's latest services; the platform product org continuously generalizes what's learned across FDEs (or across many clients one FDE sees) into buildable abstractions and new platform capabilities. Cagan is explicit this is "much easier to say than it is to do."
- **You don't need Palantir's scale for this to work.** The "original form" of FDE: send engineers to *multiple* customers so they personally see the similarities and differences, aiming at a *single general product* — this is, in Cagan's words, "the essence of [[reference-customer-program|customer discovery]]."
- **Lineage/advocacy.** Cagan cites himself, Steve Blank, and Bill Campbell as having advocated this kind of direct engineer-customer embedding "for as long as I've been writing articles and books" — i.e., this is a long-standing SVPG position, not a new one; what's new is the naming and the current AI-driven relevance (product discovery for AI agents named as a current example).
- **Career signal.** Cagan notes product creators who've worked this way have "disproportionately gone on to exceptional careers in product creation, product leadership, and founding startups."
- **Real named companies referenced (both real-world exemplars, not the reader's org):** [[palantir]] (the FDE/platform-product exemplar) and Accenture (the custom-solutions contrast). **Resolved 2026-09-06 (ingest-plan Batch 10):** [[palantir]] built as a full org-entity page — substantively discussed here, not just named, and the article's central worked example. `Accenture` — **closed as a documented non-target**: it appears only as a brief foil (the custom-solutions company Palantir is contrasted against), with no independent discussion beyond that one contrast — not enough standalone material to justify an org-entity page the way Palantir's sustained treatment does.
