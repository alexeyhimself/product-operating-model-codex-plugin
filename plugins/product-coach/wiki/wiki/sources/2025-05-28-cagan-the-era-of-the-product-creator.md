# Cagan — The Era of the Product Creator

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The Era of the Product Creator" · svpg.com, 2025-05-28 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-era-of-the-product-creator/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — the origin of "product creator" as the wiki's inclusive term** for anyone actually doing the product-management job (PM, designer, engineer, founder, business stakeholder), regardless of title. The wiki already used this term on [[model-as-product-coach]], sourced there to the Feb 2026 "Product Coaching and AI" article — that citation is now re-rooted here, ~9 months earlier and closer to the term's actual origin. Explicitly continues [[2023-06-09-cagan-preparing-for-the-future|Preparing For The Future]] ("I have been sounding the alarm" links directly to it) and is itself later tempered by [[2026-08-10-cagan-a-fresh-definition-of-the-product-role|A Fresh Definition of The Product Role]] (Aug 2026) — see below.

## What it backs
- concepts: [[model-as-product-coach]] (**re-rooted** — the "product creators (PMs, designers, engineers)" inclusive framing), [[product-management-theater]] (the creator/non-creator split sharpens the theater diagnosis — non-creators are "increasingly and especially vulnerable")
- competencies: [[product-manager]] (extends the "creator, not facilitator" framing from [[2024-03-10-cagan-product-management-theater-lennys|Lenny's]] into an explicit definition: *"anyone actively shaping the product and tackling the product risks [value, usability, feasibility, viability] is a product creator. Their title doesn't really matter."*)

## Notes for the coach
- **The core definition, verbatim.** *"Rather, the necessary role of a product manager is a product creator, working alongside the product designer and engineers every day to solve problems for our customers in ways our customers love, yet work for the business."* And: *"anyone actively shaping the product and tackling the product risks... is a product creator. Their title doesn't really matter."*
- **Who's newly welcomed into the role.** With GenAI tools and a growing understanding that [product sense](https://www.svpg.com/product-sense-demystified/) is developed, not innate, strong designers, engineers, founders, product leaders, and business stakeholders are increasingly playing the product-creator role themselves — previously the rare exception (a founder with the vision and the range), now a "fundamental shift."
- **Not a rename, and not usually solo.** Cagan is explicit this isn't "rename PM to product creator" — and that most serious products still need a range of skillsets even with strong tools; solopreneurs remain "the rare exceptions and not the rule."
- **The prediction: a clear split.** High-agency PMs who already understood they own value and viability are "ideally suited for the AI-powered future" (market signal: rapidly increasing salaries). Non-creator PMs — facilitators, cheerleaders, backlog administrators — are predicted to be left behind, a possibility Cagan frames with real sympathy: *"I feel for the people that will have their lives disrupted."*

## Later tempering (Aug 2026) — track alongside the Feb 2026 retraction
This article's optimism about the *breadth* of who becomes a product creator gets a partial, milder correction 15 months later in [[2026-08-10-cagan-a-fresh-definition-of-the-product-role|A Fresh Definition of The Product Role]]: *"About a year ago I published one of our most popular articles, The Era of the Product Creator, in which I celebrated the arrival of these new tools that make the product creation process... so much better... That's been maybe a little bit true, but not anywhere near what I had hoped for."* Distinct from the [[model-as-product-coach#the-zero-to-one-problem-a-cagan-retraction|Feb 2026 zero-to-one retraction]] (which is about whether newcomers *can* learn fast enough) — this one is about whether the *tools alone* actually widened who becomes a strong product creator in practice. Cagan's Aug 2026 diagnosis, borrowed from Benedict Evans: the bottleneck was never the tools, it's whether the person thinks like a product person at all.
