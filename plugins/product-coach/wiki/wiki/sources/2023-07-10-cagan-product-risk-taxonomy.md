## Notes for the coach
- **The version history, from the source.** INSPIRED 1st ed (2008) used **three** risks — *value, usability, feasibility* — which Cagan used "for well over a decade" and liked for being intuitive. INSPIRED 2nd ed (2018) split value into customer **value** and business **viability**, giving the modern four. Cagan is candid about the trade-off: "it really is better to have 3 items rather than 4," but the extra cognitive load is worth it.
- **Why the split happened — the diagnostic detail.** "Value" was meant to cover both customer *and* business value, but teams "would focus on *customer* value, but would gloss over *business* value." Cagan names the persisting form of this error: product people, "even some that are considered thought leaders," who argue "the only thing you need to focus on is coming up with a product your customers love." He suspects correlation with the [[2021-06-10-cagan-the-cspo-pathology|CSPO pathology]] — before Agile spread, teams understood solving for the business better.
- **The third taxonomy, treated fairly.** IDEO's *desirability / viability / feasibility*, popularized through design thinking, where **desirability combines customer value and usability**. Cagan says he likes it and has seen it work — but finds it **a much better fit for consumer than business products**, and the reasoning is the genuinely useful part: for consumer products the user is the buyer, so value and usability are intertwined; in business contexts user and buyer differ, users often have no *desire* to use the product but must to do their job (so **usability** is their concern), and **value** is addressed by the buyer.
- **What's hidden inside viability.** Cagan names ethical risk, go-to-market risk, and compliance risk as embedded in business viability, and says he's "sympathetic" to arguments for breaking them out but weighs memorability against completeness. **Cross-check:** he *does* later propose a fifth, ethical risk in [[2020-01-28-cagan-coaching-ethics|Coaching – Ethics]] (Jan 2020) — i.e. **earlier** than this article. Treat these as a live tension rather than a clean progression, and see the existing note on [[product-ethics]].
- **The headline position.** "At SVPG, we evangelize the four-risk taxonomy … But the higher order point to remember is that *any* of these risk taxonomies can work, and *any of them* are far better than using none at all." Cagan draws "a straight line between not using one of the risk taxonomies, and failed product efforts. Especially failed MVPs."
