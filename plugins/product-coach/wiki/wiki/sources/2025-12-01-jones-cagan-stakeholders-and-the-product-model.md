## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — the wiki's first article written *for* stakeholders rather than about them.** Everything else backing [[stakeholder-relationship-health]] takes the product manager's or product leader's point of view (how to earn stakeholder trust); this article is the practical brief Jones & Cagan hand directly to a stakeholder — sales, legal, finance, HR, or any non-product function reliant on the product organization — explaining what changes about how they should engage once a company moves to the product model.

## Notes for the coach
- **Who counts as a stakeholder.** Anyone responsible for a key aspect of the business (P&L, legal, finance, HR, business operations) who is *not* part of the product organization but relies on it for technology solutions.
- **The three foundations of effective collaboration.** (1) **Sharing business context** — constraints (go-to-market, regulatory, financial, partnerships) that only the stakeholder can supply; (2) **Framing requests as problems to solve, with a definition of success** — rather than pre-specified features and dates, so the product team retains room to discover a solution that actually works for both customers and the business; ideas are still welcome, just framed as input rather than a mandate; (3) **Direct access to customers, users, and data** — necessary for the product team to discover and validate solutions; access-control concerns should be solved with tooling and authorization, not by blocking access outright.
- **Prototypes replace documents as the collaboration artifact.** Instead of slow, document-heavy specs, stakeholders should expect to see frequent prototypes of proposed solutions *before* anything is built — a stakeholder's chance to judge business viability while changes are still cheap. The same prototypes are tested with users/customers for usability and value, and with engineers for feasibility.
- **Keep-the-lights-on, named for a stakeholder audience.** Ongoing, non-strategic work — business reporting, compliance changes, fixing critical issues — continues alongside the larger problems-to-solve work; it usually doesn't need to be expressed as a problem with a measurable outcome, and doesn't usually involve discovery.
- **High-integrity commitments, named for a stakeholder audience.** When a stakeholder genuinely needs a firm date, the product team can provide a high-integrity commitment — a trustworthy date — but it requires some discovery work first and should be used sparingly.
- **Navigating multiple product teams.** When several product teams contribute to one offering (the [[team-topology|team topology]]), a stakeholder's primary point of contact is usually product leadership, who may route to a specific PM as needed — not every team individually.
- **The closing framing.** "True collaboration" between stakeholders and product teams — as distinct from the old order-taking relationship — is what most companies report as the biggest felt difference after moving to the product model.
