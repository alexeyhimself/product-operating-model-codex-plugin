# Product Therapy Ep 37 — Coaching Transformations

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[lea-hickman|Lea Hickman]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 37 · SVPG, February 19, 2026 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=POFh9tCE51o). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — Hickman's field-battle-stories treatment of transformation, the *messy middle* she and Idiodi live in.** Complements Ep 38 [[2026-03-19-idiodi-castenfors-coaching-transformation-anti-patterns|Coaching Transformation Anti-Patterns]] (Castenfors's 10 anti-patterns) and Ep 42 [[2026-07-16-idiodi-hickman-strategy-jumpstarts|Coaching Strategy Jumpstarts]] (Hickman's deep-dive on the *Strategy Jumpstart* tool). This ep is where Hickman first names the Strategy Jumpstart as the primary product-strategy-creation tool SVPG uses when coaching a company.

Key material:

(a) **Why companies call SVPG.** *"They're frustrated. They see competition and can't compete effectively. They spend a lot of money and don't know what they're getting. They have some teams performing well and want consistency and continuity across the whole organization."*

(b) **Admitting there's a problem is step one.** *"When an organization is having an existential crisis, everyone wants to change because they're frustrated. When you work with an organization driving innovation in pockets, it's harder to make that change. Making the leaders honest about what they're actually trying to achieve is the first step."*

(c) **The mostly-fine-but-blind-spots trap.** For enterprises with revenue rising, stock price fine, margin targets met — the case for change is not obvious. Hickman's own operational anecdote: revenue seemed fine, margin targets fine, *"but we weren't driving the level of innovation we could in the past."* Reskilling and retooling for the future is scary because *"most people have reached that level in the organization because they have successes under their belts and they're used to doing things a certain way."*

(d) **How Hickman convinces a business leader.** Role-play opener: *"Have you released a capability, a new feature, a new product to the market and it didn't achieve the results you expected?"* Pretty much universally, yes. *"What if I could give you a formula that would help you mitigate that scenario? What if you could actually before you roll something out mitigate the risk in rolling that capability? Would you be interested in hearing more about that?"* — pose it as a formula the great companies use, not a slight of the individual or leader.

(e) **Idiodi's leader-pain-point ammunition.** *"Every way you've grown in the last 10 years has come from you buying a company. Every time you want something fast, you have to outsource it or create an innovation lab. All of these are indications for you that the way your engine is working today is not giving you the results you want."*

(f) **The three dimensions of the [[product-operating-model]]** — Hickman restated for a general audience:
1. **How you build software** — small frequent releases, structure the engineering organization to support that.
2. **How you discover the right solution** — [[product-discovery]] (valuable/usable/feasible/viable testing).
3. **How you decide which problems to solve** — the biggest unlock for leaders, because they've been taking capabilities from stakeholders at face value.

(g) **The Strategy Jumpstart — first named here.** *"I do a lot of work with companies on what I call strategy jumpstarts because it's just a great way of getting — especially for organizations where you have someone who owns a business separate from the product team — getting people in the same room to make those hard decisions and determine what's that prioritized list of problems to solve for this year."* → Full treatment in Ep 42.

(h) **AI accelerating the case for transformation.** *"Companies that have already adopted this way of working — doing discovery, having a culture of experimentation — are better equipped to take advantage of AI. They can experiment and test more appropriately, get to a better solution faster."* Idiodi's version: AI has brought many CTOs to the executive table for the first time — *"the fact you had to be invited is a demonstration of what needs to transform."*

(i) **ROI reframe — from output to outcome.** Hickman: *"For a leader pivoting from output to outcomes, make them understand that delivering something / releasing a capability is not success — that's a means to an end. Really focus those leaders on define AND quantify what success looks like. Suddenly they're like 'what do you mean quantify?'"* Then work with finance to map the business model to the quantified definition of success.

(j) **Idiodi's revenue-is-not-a-project frame.** *"I know a problem you're going to have every year — you'll want to grow revenue. So who wakes up in your company every day to grow revenue? Sales just captures what we sell. Marketing markets. Growing revenue is not a project — after you've grown revenue this year, guess what you want next year? Grow revenue. The best companies have a group of people that wake up every day to discover new ways to solve that problem."*

(k) **Where transformations fall apart — Hickman's big three.**
1. **Product management theater — installation, not adoption.** Renaming Product Owners to Product Managers and calling it done. *"A great product leader once told me that's a problem of installation, not adoption. What we're trying to drive is adoption — grow the skills, develop them, coach them, make sure we're not just changing titles."*
2. **Culture is hard.** Being declarative about the product culture you want, holding time for experimentation and discovery, allowing empowered decision-making — especially hard in larger orgs where people have never seen what great looks like.
3. **The gap between leadership intention and team reality.** CEOs and ICs both say they want the model — but the middle layer is where it stalls. Root cause: **product leadership played as management, not leadership** — a bottoms-up product vision that's just an inventory of what teams are doing; no declarative view; no hard priority calls; project-managing the org instead of assigning outcome-accountable problems.

(l) **From feature-team middle-manager anxiety** — the middle-managers of pilot teams are the scared layer: *"my job is changing but I'm not in the pilot team." Transformation needs to happen at all layers.*
