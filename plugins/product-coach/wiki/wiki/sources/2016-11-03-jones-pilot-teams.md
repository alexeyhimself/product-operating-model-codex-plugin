# Jones — Pilot Teams

> **Citation:** Chris Jones · "Pilot Teams" · svpg.com, 2016-11-03 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/pilot-teams/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** Jones's dedicated *Pilot Teams* article — the first in his SVPG transformation series and the article referenced from both of Cagan's later transformation-execution pieces. This is the **origin** of the pilot-teams technique as SVPG canon: name the adoption-lifecycle problem (some love change, some hate it, laggards can sabotage a big-bang rollout), then apply small-'a' agile *to the transformation itself* — experiment, iterate, validate, and only then scale.

Names the four selection criteria that appear (in refined form) in [[2024-11-21-cagan-transformation-politics|Cagan's 2024 political-selection checklist]]:

1. **People** — a dedicated PM, UX designer, and lead developer who have the skills, mindset, and **enthusiasm** for the change.
2. **Location** — physically co-located, "next to one another" (a 2016 constraint that has softened but not vanished — see the remote note below).
3. **Charter** — scope framed in **business outcomes**, not features or tasks.
4. **Autonomy** — dependencies on the rest of the org minimized, so the pilot tests the model rather than the surrounding organization.

Then a **learning loop**: run the pilot in the new model for one to two quarters, *insulated from legacy processes*, and compare business-outcome effectiveness against status quo. Comparisons are often qualitative; that's fine.

## Notes for the coach
- **This is the article Cagan references** in both [[2024-10-29-cagan-transformation-as-a-project|Transformation as a Project]] and [[2024-11-21-cagan-transformation-politics|Transformation Politics]] as "SVPG's dedicated Pilot Teams article." Before this ingest the wiki carried a backlog note on [[pilot-teams]] saying it was not yet ingested; that note is now resolved.
- **Lineage read.** Jones (2016) is the **method**; Cagan (2024) adds the **political framing** and the four *sharper* checks — ambition ceiling, manager-support, tech-debt, skills. Together they form the pilot-teams canon: Jones supplies the how, Cagan the why-it-works-politically.
- **Co-location caveat.** The 2016 "physically next to one another" line is stronger than later SVPG guidance would state in a remote-normal world. Pair with [[remote-collaboration]] when coaching a distributed org — the *close cross-functional collaboration* requirement is the durable point; the physical-desk version is dated.
- **"Insulate from legacy processes."** The single most-often-missed step in practice. Pilot teams that still have to attend the old release-train PI planning, or feed a shared JIRA backlog, get pulled back to feature-team behaviour under stress. Coach: what specifically will the pilot be exempt from?
- **The article does not include** the Trainline / Moore worked example, the Datasite near-fail, or Bufrem's "poster-child not prototype" refinement — those live on [[pilot-teams]] itself, sourced from later podcast episodes. Jones's 2016 piece is the mechanics; the case-study weight sits elsewhere.
