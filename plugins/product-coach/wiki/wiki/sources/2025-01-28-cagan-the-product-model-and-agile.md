# Cagan — The Product Model and Agile

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The Product Model and Agile" · svpg.com, 2025-01-28 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-model-and-agile/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — co-primary root, alongside [[2018-06-30-cagan-revenge-of-the-pmo|Revenge of the PMO]], for [[pom-vs-agile-and-safe]].** This is Cagan's most direct, single-article answer to "what is the relationship between Agile and the product model?" — the "Agile is one of three dimensions, and the easiest one" framing the wiki previously sourced only from the *Escape the Project Trap* talk gets a written, dedicated root here. Revenge of the PMO stays primary for the deep, mechanism-level SAFe critique specifically (per Aleksei's call, 2026-09-05) — this article is primary for the broader "subset not synonym" argument, the real-vs-fake-Agile distinction, and the principles-vs-process split.

## What it backs
- synthesis: [[pom-vs-agile-and-safe]] (**co-primary root** — "Agile is one of the product model's three major dimensions, and it's the easiest"; the real-Agile-vs-fake-Agile distinction, defined operationally by outcome — continuous deployment, whatever the ceremony — rather than by which named process is running; the separation of Agile Manifesto *principles* from the mostly-project-management *processes* built around them (Scrum, Kanban, XP); the claim that "working software is the primary measure of progress" is fine for custom/contract work but insufficient for commercial product work, which needs outcomes; the classification of Agile coaches into two kinds — those with real product-strategy/discovery/delivery experience (valuable [[product-coaching|product]] or delivery coaches) versus those focused only on ceremony (deemphasized under [[principles-over-process]]))

## Notes for the coach
- **Not "the next new thing."** Cagan is explicit the product model has been out there 20+ years and is not a rebrand of Agile or its "next certification" — most companies are just newly exposed to it.
- **Real Agile vs. fake Agile, defined operationally.** If "Agile" means SAFe, then Agile plays no part in the product model — SAFe is "pretty much the antithesis" (cross-references [[2018-06-30-cagan-revenge-of-the-pmo|Revenge of the PMO]]'s deeper critique). Whether you're running XP, Kanban, Scrum, or none of the named ceremonies — if you're consistently doing continuous deployment, that's "real Agile" by Cagan's own test.
- **Principles vs. process, separated cleanly.** The Agile Manifesto's *principles* are valuable and still relevant — they're what attracted many people to Agile in the first place. The *processes* built around them (Scrum, Kanban, XP) are mostly project-management mechanics for the *build* dimension only; they say nothing about *deciding what to build*.
- **The one Agile principle that doesn't transfer to commercial product work.** "Working software is the primary measure of progress" may be good enough for custom/contract software, but commercial product companies need to know the software also solves the underlying problem and produces business results (outcomes, not just output).
- **Agile coaches, two kinds.** Coaches with real hands-on experience in continuous deployment, instrumentation/telemetry, monitoring, and deployment infrastructure (A/B testing) can be genuinely valuable — as [[product-coaching|Delivery Coaches]]. Coaches without that experience, focused on the specific ceremony/process, are exactly what gets deemphasized moving to the product model ([[principles-over-process|canon]]).
- **Why SVPG doesn't recognize certifications.** Certifications apply to formal processes; SVPG looks instead for the relevant experience found in real product-model companies — points to [[2024-04-30-cagan-good-product-coach-bad-product-coach|Good Product Coach / Bad Product Coach]].
