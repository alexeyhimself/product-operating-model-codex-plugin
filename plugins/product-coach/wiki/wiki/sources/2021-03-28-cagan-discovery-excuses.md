# Discovery – Excuses

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Discovery – Excuses* · SVPG, Mar 28, 2021 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/discovery-excuses/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — a named catalog of seven objections teams raise to avoid discovery, with Cagan's rebuttal to each.** Distinct from the leadership-side objections in [[overcoming-objections]]: *"in more than a few companies, the issue is not so much with the company's leaders, it's with the product teams themselves."* Direct diagnostic material for [[discovery-health]].

**The seven excuses and the rebuttals:**

| Excuse | Cagan's response |
|---|---|
| *"There's no demand"* | Usually a crappy solution misread as absent demand. *"It is easier on our ego to believe the demand isn't there, at least until a competitor comes along with a dramatically better solution."* |
| *"Our customers hate change"* | *"Customers hate bad products… But customers happily change all the time – if you give them something that truly makes their lives better."* Evidence list: *Slack. Stripe. Shopify. Tesla. Zoom. Netflix. iPhone. [[workiva\|Workiva]]…* |
| *"We're in a regulated industry"* | Most industries are more regulated than teams think — Uber/Lyft employment rules, Airbnb zoning, gaming age/privacy. And *"those same constraints increase the barriers to entry for your would-be competitors."* Points to [[2018-01-29-jones-product-discovery-in-regulated-environments\|Jones's techniques article]]. |
| *"We have a new type of product"* | Confuses "customers can't tell us what to build" with "customers can't be tested on." *"We're testing to see if they will buy the product if we do build it."* |
| *"We're not allowed to talk to customers"* | Usually direct-sales account control. Respect the GTM model, but *"that doesn't mean we don't need to talk to customers. That's non-negotiable. What is negotiable is how we do this."* Fix: a head-of-product ↔ head-of-sales conversation. |
| *"It's not possible to test this idea"* | *"I still have not run into that situation myself."* Three diagnostic questions: have you analyzed the [[the-four-big-risks\|four risks]]? do you know the [[prototypes\|four flavors of prototype]]? do you understand qualitative *and* quantitative techniques? |
| *"We don't have time"* | *"The most common, yet most frustrating excuse."* If the idea is genuinely low-risk, just build it. If it is risky, *"do you have the time not to test it?"* |

**The order-of-magnitude rule, stated as a principle.** *"The underlying principle of product discovery is that if we can't test out an idea in at least an order of magnitude less time and effort than actually building it, then you're doing something very wrong."*

**Discovery as a core competency.** He closes by arguing discovery skills must be *"a core competency of any strong product organization, just as with product delivery skills."*

## Notes for the coach
Use this as a listening guide rather than a lecture: the seven phrases are close to verbatim what teams actually say, so the card lets you recognize an excuse in the moment and respond with Cagan's counter rather than improvising. The "do you have the time *not* to test it?" reversal is the one that most often lands. Note that Workiva appears here in the disruptor list — the same company the wiki carries as a sourced exemplar.
