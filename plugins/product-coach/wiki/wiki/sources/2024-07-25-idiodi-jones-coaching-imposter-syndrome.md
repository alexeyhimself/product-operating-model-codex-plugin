# Product Therapy Ep 6 — Coaching Imposter Syndrome

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[chris-jones|Chris Jones]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 6 · SVPG, July 25, 2024 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CbLgDtKZQnM). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary.** The canonical Chris Jones story of learning strategy under coaching (an early-career strategy meeting where he mumbled through a follow-up question and destroyed his leaders' confidence; a manager who then made him write a **definitive point of view** in prose on two strategic topics — no right answer, but he had to defend it and handle objections). Jones's diagnostic: imposter syndrome is *"a nagging fear that you don't deserve to be in the job… and that somebody's going to find out."* A **small dose is healthy** — it prevents you from winging things; a **large dose is paralyzing** — it stops you taking the risks you should take. Coaching move: *model vulnerability first* — leaders humanize the feeling before asking others to admit it. Jones's **"sign up for six before you do one"** hack for public speaking: the mere fact of committed practice reps takes the pressure off any single attempt.

## Notes for the coach
Idiodi's related early-career story (23-year-old with $1M to spend, spending three months planning to avoid mistakes, then losing $26M across 17 experiments) confirms the *avoidance* pattern this episode diagnoses. The *"you can't be a good manager without being a good coach"* Bill Campbell line is invoked in passing (the fuller ground is on [[coaching-mindset]] and [[bill-campbell]]). Also attribute to Idiodi the coaching move for skip-level asks: *"in 16 years as CEO, nobody has ever asked me what I needed"* — the CEO exercise that turned a founder into an emotional advocate for reciprocal coaching upward.
