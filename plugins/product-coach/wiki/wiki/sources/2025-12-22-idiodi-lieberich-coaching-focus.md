# Product Therapy Ep 35 — Coaching Focus (with Elias Lieberich)

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[elias-lieberich|Elias Lieberich]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 35 · SVPG, December 22, 2025 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Q1eT-tqNF4). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the wiki's most complete podcast-form teaching on organizational *focus* as a coaching practice.** Complements Ep 29 ([[2025-10-02-idiodi-cagan-coaching-product-strategy|Coaching Product Strategy]]) — where Cagan established that *"you don't have a prioritization problem, you have a strategy problem"*, Lieberich's ep is the *how you build the focus muscle inside a team.* Introduces [[elias-lieberich]] (product coach at [[product-matters]], external to SVPG) as a wiki-primary voice on focus.

Key material:

(a) **Focus as a human problem.** Lieberich's opening: *"Teams have good ideas, talk to customers, see opportunities. The CEO sees something amazing in the market. Sales comes in with a customer about to close. We all come from the right place but end up with too much choice — and we just can't say no."* Culture / leadership fear / CEO FOMO are all in the mix.

(b) **What focused organizations FEEL like.** Lieberich's YouTube-ads story: one floor up was Google Maps (fun), one floor down were self-driving cars (amazing) — engineers stuck around because *"they knew what we're trying to accomplish. If they know what we're trying to get, they will literally go through walls to get there."*

(c) **The litmus test for focus** — the team can navigate day-to-day trade-offs. *"Tomorrow the escalation comes in. What do I do? If you can figure out why the things you're working on are the number-one things, and what this new thing would cost — that's the difference. If you don't have focus, if you don't know why number one is number one, a new thing coming in is very hard to disambiguate, argue, decide."*

(d) **The fastest signal a company is unfocused** — *"you spend enormous time planning and then throw it all overboard the next day."* Idiodi's list of complementary signals: roadmaps that are just stakeholder requests or past promises; teams disconnected from strategy and unable to explain it; product managers whose main job is *managing expectations.*

(e) **The prioritization=strategy diagnosis** (Lieberich reinforcing Cagan). *"People call us and say 'can you fix my prioritization? Can you implement OKRs?' … I feel like the Mr. Wolf from Pulp Fiction — 'Elias, come in, just implement OKRs so everybody knows how to prioritize.' But the root cause: the teams don't know what is important. Once we have done all the prioritization, a tiny thing changes and everything is thrown overboard again."*

(f) **The 12-key-pillars / big-rocks pattern** (Idiodi, restated crisper from Ep 29). Leadership offsite → "we've cut 200 items to our 12 big rocks" → "number one: make the platform most secure, easier to use, best-in-class." *"It is still doing all the things — best-in-class means safe, secure, comprehensive, user-friendly, one definition of all the things."* This is not focus.

(g) **The written narrative technique — before strategy, before focus.** Lieberich's coaching tool. *"Just sit down and write it out — what are we building? Who are we building for? What is the value? What is the solution we currently think is right? What data backs that up? What are unknowns? What are risks?"* Even mature leaders struggle — *"like pulling teeth."* Its purpose is not to write a perfect document — it's a **fact-finding exercise**: *"which parts were easiest to write and which were hard? If the user section is vague, that is a thing we can punt on and find out."*

(h) **The scale rule** (Lieberich): *"The larger your organization is, the larger your scope, if you don't have a clear answer in your mind — you have no business turning around to a big organization and telling them, because that uncertainty is just going to multiply."*

(i) **The grandfather test / grandparent test** (Idiodi, Lieberich's variant). *"Skip levels down in your organization. Pick an engineer trying to do a good job and ask 'what is the strategy? What are you doing here? What is all this for? Why do you show up to work?'"* — the answers reveal exactly how much context has actually made it down. Companion frame: *"in many cases leaders can only give to their teams what has been given to them. If a leader says 'because I said so,' often it's because they don't have a good narrative themselves — their boss just handed it down."*

(j) **Focus, then alignment — the two-part sequence.** *"A product strategy — even if it's sharp and Christian, you have clarity — you now need to get it to your boss, the CEO, the board, and to your team. That's the real spicy stuff — coaching alignment is the next thing."* Lieberich's method:
- Get the seed direction: 1–2 business goals from the CEO/board so you know *for what.*
- Build **handshake agreement** with the organization so it holds up when tested.

(k) **The e-commerce CPO case (Lieberich, on the eve of Black Friday).** Newly-promoted CPO. Everyone working on everything with good ideas but no traction. They'd spent two years building a customer-facing dashboard (sales-suggested; customers loved it) that *didn't move the numbers.* Written narrative revealed the CPO had strong command of one customer segment but a gap on another. He flew across the Atlantic, joined customer events, looked at win-loss analysis — realized a specific segment was *conversion-driven*, not visualization-driven. Redirected teams to conversion. Pilot team, experimentation, small win: **changed a button color (A/B tested — the change performed worse) — but the immediate feedback loop demonstrated the team could tune the important lever.** Then a second insight the team found *had a massive impact* on conversion, ready before Black Friday. Multi-million win. Freshly promoted CPO + CTO now at the senior-leadership big room.

(l) **The Idiodi rule on why alignment magic works** — *"they don't trust your insight; they don't trust you. He flew over to build a relationship, asked for help, was present, spent time with sales and customers. They saw him doing the homework. So they trusted how the insight came to be — it felt like their strategy."*

(m) **Saying "no" vs "not yet."** *"Once you have alignment, most of the time the answer is not never — it's not yet. That customer request is important, we really would want that deal, but wouldn't they also want better conversion rates? Wouldn't they be happy with more conversions for Black Friday and we could do the other thing later?"* — the tradeoff conversation only becomes possible after alignment.

(n) **The strategy narrative every quarter — Idiodi's operational practice restated.** *"Probably the most sought-after internal document in the company. Every quarter it consolidated all the learnings and insights. Always ended with a clear statement: 'we are choosing to continue our focus on X' or 'change our focus to Y.' Every stakeholder could see their problem, their request, their issue in that document — 'I heard you, I see you.' And then they still see the conclusion. What people enjoy about a good strategy is seeing their insights reflected transparently — 'yes, all the marketing issues, all the operations issues — yes, I feel heard.'"*

(o) **The Product FAQ — Lieberich's connective-tissue tool.** *"You will stumble over elephants in the room — impossible-to-reconcile decisions where you can't get to a clear answer via intellectual discourse. What you can do is just take the question and move it into the Product FAQ. Say: 'as of Q1 2026, this is the best answer we have — and it could be we don't know.' It acknowledges the concern."* Two magical effects: everyone sees themselves in the doc; you're doing the hard legwork and coming back to them.

(p) **How to help a leader articulate outcomes when they're feature-focused.** Idiodi's two-question probe: *"If we fix this, what becomes possible? What are you pretending not to know that's in your way?"* — the second one especially opens up what the leader has been avoiding.

(q) **Focus for platform / internal teams — the currency framing.** Lieberich: *"The deeper you go down into the stack, the more heavy-weight the impact is on the rest. If you're on a code-review tool team, refocus and say 'we did this because the other thing is very expensive to run and takes forever to build stuff — we're getting closer.'"* Same coaching applies to long technical migrations that everyone forgot the reason for.

(r) **Focus as muscle — the concentric-circles method.** *"It starts with a single person actually trying to answer the fundamental questions. As you bring in the rest in concentric circles — maybe your CTO, then the CEO, then your team — that is really the first step."* Second step: **immediate success**, a positive increment on a small window (a week is fine).

(s) **Cadence for holding focus.** Short-term (this quarter — what will I contribute to *this measure*?) plus **regular check-ins** every ~6 weeks / end of quarter, then annual strategy update. Alignment is fragile: *"we plan crisply, then a new person joins, OpenAI launches something, and the alignment dissipates in different directions. We have to bring it back in and bake in new information."*

(t) **The IC's response when leadership hasn't given focus.** *"Conditions are never perfect — not at Google, not at YouTube, not at SVPG. Get used to it and pivot your mindset into 'what's the opportunity?'"* Then very concretely with the backlog: **for each high-priority item, try to write down who it's for and what impact it will bring. If you can't answer, it shouldn't be there.** Ask the manager or the organization for context you don't have — being humble is fine; PMs don't have to be mini-CEOs with all the answers.

(u) **Idiodi's harder rule** — *"If you cannot answer *what is in it for the business, how will we measure success, and what problem am I trying to solve and for whom* — don't do it. It is not strategic. If an executive asks what you're working on and you cannot pointedly say 'I'm driving revenue' or 'I'm improving conversion,' when we're cutting the budget, the very first thing I'm going to cut is that."*

(v) **Lieberich's magic coaching questions.**
- To a leader in the noise: **"Who is this for?"** *"The biggest successes I had were because we realized the needs we had cut out for ourselves from our customers actually weren't cutting it — there was an adjacent user segment where there was a lot of value."*
- Bonus (Idiodi): *"You are trading clarity for comfort. And that's a bad deal every time."*
- To every CEO whose org struggles with focus: **"When was the last time you had a hit that was intentional?"** *"How much of your work is something that is happening to you? There are something you are making happen — that intentional aspect of it."*

(w) **Stop-doing prescription** (Lieberich, closing): *"Back-to-back meetings all day. That's a whole other topic — if you're in meetings all day, when did you have time to think?"*
