## Notes for the coach
- **Claim vs. demonstrate.** Everyone says they care about customers; the tell is how they behave during an outage, a confusing change, or a showstopper — and how often they actually sit face-to-face with users.
- **"Customer" is almost a sacred term.** Cagan pushes back hard on PMs who count stakeholders, customer service, the CEO, or advertising partners as "customers" — he reads it as a remnant of technology-serves-the-business, and says it dilutes the real customer. Internal users of customer-enabling products and platform developers matter, but *none have the weight of the true customer*.
- **The cadence.** His own coach required 30 visits (15 US, 15 Europe) before he could make a decision. His recommendation: ≥15 to start, then a minimum of three 1-hour customer interactions **every week**, ongoing — and the weekly 1:1 is where the manager asks what was learned.
- **Purpose of the storytelling:** to establish the PM's *reputation* as the person with deep personal knowledge of customers. Cagan's favourite illustrative stories are FedEx's wedding dress, REI's hiking-boot replacement, and Zappos.
- **The executive-override warning.** In genuinely customer-centric companies, if the team doesn't prioritise a customer problem as highly as the executives do, the executives will step in — *"if you make them choose between empowered teams and taking care of customers, you probably won't like what they decide."* Useful against a naive reading of [[empowered-product-teams|empowerment]].
- **Not order-taking.** The job is *to innovate on behalf of customers*, explicitly contrasted with focus groups. Timeline: sincere customer-centricity takes **a year or more** to develop in a new PM.
