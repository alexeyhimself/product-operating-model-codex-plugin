# Cagan — The Product Model in Traditional IT

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The Product Model in Traditional IT" · svpg.com, 2024-07-09 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-model-in-traditional-it/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon)** — opens the "probing the edges" series that continues into the Government and Outsourcing articles. First of three questions Cagan received after *TRANSFORMED*'s launch: does the model apply to Traditional IT, to Government, and to Outsourcing agencies? This one answers for IT.

## What it backs
- frameworks: [[it-to-product-organization]] (light integration — a 2024 companion/update to the 2008 ten-step framework: this article assesses *whether and how much* the model applies to an org that stays IT, rather than prescribing full conversion)

## Notes for the coach
- **The fit test, verbatim-adjacent:** *"if a product team does not have the levers to truly impact value and outcomes... the product model is not likely a fit."* Three disqualifying conditions: severely limited by a specific third-party product; no ongoing daily access to engineers; given output and/or a date that takes precedence over outcome.
- **Build vs. buy is not IT-specific.** Cagan's core clarifying move: *any* org facing a buy decision (even a product org buying version-control or cloud platform tooling) administers/customizes/integrates rather than does product work — this isn't a special IT deficiency.
- **Three friction areas, named explicitly:** funding models (project-based, designed for predictability, undermines outcomes), staffing models (project-based staffing and especially outsourced engineers limit outcome delivery), governance models (traditional IT's "serve the business" governance is built for predictability of delivery, not outcomes).
- **Nomenclature translation** — useful when a coach is bridging IT vocabulary to product vocabulary: IT's own "innovation over predictability" framing doesn't map directly (most IT orgs aren't optimizing for innovation, but for *reliably and rapidly meeting business needs* — and note that product model companies value predictability too, via [[high-integrity-commitments]]); IT's "project" often means what product orgs call an "initiative"; and "project managers" exist in both worlds, just under different assumptions (PMO-school = predictability-first, empowerment-school = outcomes-first).
- **When to cite:** whenever a coach is asked "does the product model even apply to us, we're just an IT department" — this is the canonical answer, with the fit test built in.
