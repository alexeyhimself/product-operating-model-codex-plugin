# Cagan — The Product Operating Model (nomenclature)

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "The Product Operating Model" · svpg.com, 2023-03-03 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-operating-model/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Notes for the coach
- **The naming problem, in Cagan's words.** The book forced him "to come up with a term for what it is that companies are transforming *to*" — he had written about [[2022-05-31-moore-transformation-defined|what it means to transform]] but "had not named the destination." He is explicit that he's reluctant: "I am not anxious to introduce new nomenclature."
- **What the term actually denotes.** "Simply how the best tech-powered companies work" — "a set of principles, practices and competencies." Inside strong companies it's usually unnamed, or named after the company: "the Amazon way, the Apple Model, the Netflix way, the Stripe way, or the Spotify Model." SVPG's work "is all about teasing out the common principles and practices." Useful grounding for why the wiki treats exemplar companies as illustrations rather than templates — see [[where-the-model-applies]].
- **Why not "product-led."** Cagan rejects "product-led company" and "product-driven company" because "too many people think that means 'product-management-led company' which of course it is not." Worth citing directly whenever someone conflates the two.
- **Prior naming avoidance.** He notes he'd mostly *avoided* naming the way of working, visible in titles like [[2021-03-04-cagan-the-best-vs-the-rest|"the best vs. the rest"]] and [[2019-08-29-cagan-product-vs-feature-teams|"product vs feature teams"]] — this article is where that changes.
- **The taxonomy of what you're transforming *from* — the genuinely new material.** Cagan names the variants: the **IT model** ("IT is there to serve the business"); **the project model**, its close cousin, "where the CFO plays an outsized role because funding and staffing is typically project-based"; the **feature-team model** (each stakeholder drives their own roadmap); **sales-driven** and **marketing-driven** product. And the important exception — companies claiming to be "engineering-driven" or "design-driven" are "much easier to transform … as they already have so many of the key ingredients in place." For the book he settles on the umbrella term **"prior model."**
- **Status of the decision.** The article is framed as a trial balloon — "I'm tentatively hoping to go with that term, although this article is a way of testing if that's a good idea." It obviously stuck. Note also the article does *not* claim Cagan coined the term; see [[overview]]'s existing note that "product operating model" was coined at Atlassian and adopted by SVPG.
