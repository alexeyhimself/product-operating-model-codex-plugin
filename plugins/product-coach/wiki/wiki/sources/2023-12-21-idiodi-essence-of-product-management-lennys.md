## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon).** The fullest published statement of [[christian-idiodi|Idiodi]]'s own contribution to the canon: **reference customers as the single discovery technique he'd keep**, and his coaching model. Root for the four risks remains [[transformed]]/[[inspired]]; the reference-customer material here is Idiodi's own and should be attributed to him.

## What it backs
- frameworks: [[reference-customer-program]] (**root-grade material** — the numbers he works to: **6–8 references for B2B, 15–25 for B2C**, as his own definition of [[market-fit|product-market fit]]; *"on the day I launch the app in the app store, there are 25 five-star reviews"*; recruiting difficulty is itself the signal — if you can't find enough people with the problem, it isn't one; **all 25 must want the same thing**, which is how you find the minimum; marketing copy is taken verbatim from what reference customers said, so launch expectations match reality), [[coaching-the-pm]], [[product-career-development]], [[hiring-and-onboarding]]

## Notes for the coach
- **"If you know, you will care."** Not said in these words here, but this is the episode Chris Jones cites for it — Idiodi's version is the argument that competence, not exhortation, is what produces ownership. See [[2024-11-02-jones-from-features-to-outcomes-khodor]] and [[2024-04-05-jones-product-power-play-pmdojo]].
- **The trust-acceleration technique** (Idiodi calls it an "emotional-intelligence black belt" move): to get a newcomer trusted, put them next to the company's most influential person for a week and ask *that person to teach them*. You borrow the teacher's trust, force a relationship, and make the teacher partly accountable for the newcomer's growth — so they can't later dismiss them without indicting their own teaching. Backs [[hiring-and-onboarding]] and [[emotional-intelligence]].
- **Promotion:** *"I am promoting you to do the job, not to learn the job."* The best time to practise being a VP is before you are one, because mistakes are cheap then. The origin story he gives for most micromanagement is a great IC promoted with no coaching who keeps solving the problems directly. Backs [[product-career-development]].
- **Practice arenas.** Product management is "a one-time, game-time role" — there is no practice built into the job, so find low-stakes collaborative problem-solving elsewhere (non-profits, community groups). Backs [[product-coaching]].
- **The Snagajob / Starbucks / McDonald's story** is his worked example of the reference-customer technique end to end (high-volume hourly hiring; ~$32M booked in the product's first 90 days). Told as *his own* career work, not as an SVPG-published case study — cite it from this card rather than building it a `case-studies/` page; it does not meet the wiki's org-agnostic exemplar bar.
- His Africa work (Innovate Africa Foundation, Inspire Africa Conference) is context for [[christian-idiodi]], not model canon.
