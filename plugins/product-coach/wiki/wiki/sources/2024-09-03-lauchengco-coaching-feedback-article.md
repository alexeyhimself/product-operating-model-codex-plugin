# Coaching Feedback — the SHARED Model (SVPG article)

> **Citation:** [[martina-lauchengco|Martina Lauchengco]] · SVPG article · September 3, 2024 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/coaching-feedback/). Published as a companion piece to, and cross-links, [[2024-08-29-idiodi-lauchengco-coaching-feedback|the *Product Therapy* Ep 7 podcast episode of the same name]] — **these are two distinct sources; do not conflate the article with the episode.** Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — root source for the SHARED™ Model itself**, which the existing episode card and `feedback.md` did not previously name or attribute. Key material not in the podcast-form card:

- **Origin: not Lauchengco's own invention.** The model came from **Rebecca Zucker and her team at Next Step Partners**; Lauchengco calls it "life-changing" and a corrective to "radical candor" being abused as one-way "swoop and poop" criticism.
- **The full SHARED™ acronym:** **S**hare your intent (positive intention) → **H**appened — context on when/where → **A**ctions observed → **R**esults/impact → **E**ngage with curiosity and listen (coaching conversation begins) → **D**iscuss next steps.
- **Lauchengco's own "keep it simple" 4-step compression** (what she actually holds in her head in the moment): (1) lead with your intent, (2) share the specifics of what you observed, (3) ask for the other person's experience of the situation — "the important part... engage with curiosity and listen to what's said but also what it infers," (4) finish with a coaching conversation once both perspectives are on the table.
- **A fully worked example** across all six SHARED letters (a sales-team-frustration scenario) — concrete enough to hand to a client as a script template.
- **The origin story: Costanoa Ventures CEO 360s.** Lauchengco first used the model doing 360-degree reviews for portfolio CEOs; the finding was that written 360 feedback wasn't the gap — CEOs didn't know *how* to have the harder feedback conversations with their own execs. Teaching the model directly, in 1:1 coaching, is what moved the needle. "Pay it forward" close.
