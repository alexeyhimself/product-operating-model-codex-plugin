# What is a Product?

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *What is a Product?* · SVPG, Sep 3, 2019 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/what-is-a-product/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the SVPG article-form home for the *Product = Customer × Business × Technology* equation** and the "why cross-functional matters" argument. Cagan's HP Labs origin story — a tech lead being coached that engineering alone is not enough — is the earliest wiki-attested version of the trio framing.

Key material:
- **The equation:** *Product = Customer × Business × Technology* — a successful tech product has to solve for the customer, for the business, and for the technology.
- Maps directly to the [[the-four-big-risks|four big risks]]: usability risk = solving for the customer; feasibility risk = solving for technology; viability risk = solving for the business; **value risk = a function of all three**.
- **Multiplication, not addition** — if any one is zero, the product is zero. This is why the trio is not decorative.
- Cagan's own tech lead coaching moment at HP Labs — the whiteboard equation was drawn by his engineering manager.
- The three areas are deeply intertwined: technology decisions constrain design; design decisions impact business considerations; business constraints shape design and tech options.
