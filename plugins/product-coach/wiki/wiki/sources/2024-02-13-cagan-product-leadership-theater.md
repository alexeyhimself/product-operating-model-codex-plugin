## Notes for the coach
- **Not about assigning blame.** Cagan is explicit the point of the PM-theater article wasn't to blame individual PMs, their managers, or executives — it's that skill-building helps regardless of who's "at fault." Product leadership theater is the observation that follows: where PM theater is found, product leadership theater usually travels with it, because strong PMs are typically coached and developed by strong product leaders.
- **The deflection pattern, named.** Product leaders who are challenged tend to point up at senior leadership (a non-product CEO, a top-down/sales-driven/date-driven culture) rather than at their own practice. Cagan's counter: senior leaders do have real work to do to move to the product model, but **product leaders have at least as much work to do** — and many believe, wrongly, that once executives change, everything else falls into place.
- **The five complaints, verbatim pattern:** leaders complain about weak product managers, about not being empowered, about disengaged/mercenary engineers, about stakeholders who don't trust them, about CEOs who demand detailed roadmaps — without seeing that each is a consequence of their *own* actions or inactions. A leader unwilling to take responsibility for raising her people's skill level will staff teams that lack the competence empowerment requires — which then makes the rest of the list (disengagement, distrust) unsurprising, not evidence of someone else's failure.
- **Two things for a transforming leader to hold onto (quote-worthy):** *"you have as much ownership as you have credibility"*, and *"part of your job as a product leader is to change hearts and minds."*
- **The closing balance.** Executives do have real changes to make — but the larger and harder changes fall on product leaders, and success in a transformation starts with the product organization (leaders and teams) raising its own game first, not waiting on the C-suite.
