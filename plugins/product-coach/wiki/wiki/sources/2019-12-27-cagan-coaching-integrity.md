# Cagan — Coaching: Integrity

## Notes for the coach
- **Trust = competence + character; integrity is the heart of the character half.** This is the explicit bridge from [[empowered-product-teams]] (predicated on trust) to the coaching curriculum.
- **The three behaviors:**
  1. **Dependability** — anchored on the [[high-integrity-commitments|high-integrity commitment]]: never commit until discovery has reasonably addressed value/usability/feasibility/viability, then do everything possible to deliver. And *shipping on time is not enough* — what ships must actually work.
  2. **Company's best interests** — be perceived as acting for the company, not the team or the self. Concrete moves for a single-team PM: help another team hit an objective, go above and beyond for a stakeholder, publicly credit others, and support a call that's worse for your team but better for the business. (Cagan's aside: this is why equity-based compensation works.)
  3. **Accountability** — willingness to own mistakes *even when fault lies elsewhere*. He defends the old saying — "if a team succeeds everyone did their job; if it fails, it's the product manager" — as not facetious: engineering overran because the PM under-read feasibility risk; legal blocked it because the PM never explored viability.
- **Integrity ≠ perfection.** Mistakes are survivable if you are on the whole dependable, company-first, and accountable.
- **The pressure cases** Cagan lists are worth using verbatim as coaching scenarios: the CEO demanding an urgent date against the team's estimate; the angry customer who was misled; the stakeholder quietly planning to quit; the BD partner over-investing in a product unlikely to deliver.
