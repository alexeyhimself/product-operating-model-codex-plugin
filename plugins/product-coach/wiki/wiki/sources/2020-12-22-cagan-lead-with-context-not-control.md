## Notes for the coach
- **The phrase is Netflix's, not Cagan's.** "One of Netflix's mantras is 'lead with context and not control,' and this is a great summary of the leadership style that I was taught as well." Attribute accordingly — the wiki's principle page should credit Netflix/Hastings for the phrasing and SVPG for the product-org application.
- **The anecdote that carries the whole idea.** Hastings describes a manager making what he considered an awful decision. On asking why, Hastings realised the manager "had made a very reasonable decision based on the information he had," and that "the root cause of the bad decision was that Reed and his senior leadership team had not shared enough of the context." This is the cleanest available illustration of why [[strategic-context]] is a leadership deliverable rather than a document — cite it there.
- **Why EMPOWERED exists, per Cagan.** He frames the empowerment-literature genre as inspiring but general, and says he and [[chris-jones|Chris Jones]] wrote *EMPOWERED* "to help these leaders understand how strong product companies put these principles into practice in their product and technology organizations." Useful positioning note for [[empowered]].
- **The untangling problem — a real caution for anyone copying Netflix.** Cagan: "we need to untangle the practices as they relate to product, from the cultural characteristics that are more of a reflection of the personalities of the founders." And later: "some of the practices companies do are there more to define the culture than to drive behavior." Pair with [[where-the-model-applies]] and with the naming article's Amazon-way/Netflix-way point.
- **§2 endorsement-test relevance — flag for the curator.** Cagan calls *No Rules Rules* (Hastings & Meyer) "the newest addition to my list of favorite books on the empowerment topic," and lists as "current favorites": *Leaders Eat Last*, *Startup Nation*, *[[the-art-of-action|The Art of Action]]*, *Turn the Ship Around*, *Extreme Ownership*, *Team of Teams*, *Leadership is Language* — "each of these inspiring and worth reading." Two of these already have stub entity pages ([[no-rules-rules]], [[the-art-of-action]]) with no cited endorsement; **this article is that citation.** The remaining five are named-and-praised here but are *not* on the official [recommended-reading list](https://www.svpg.com/recommended-reading/) — a judgment call for the curator on whether "current favorites, worth reading" clears the §2 bar the way a formal list entry does.
- **Netflix as exemplar.** Cagan discloses long-standing admiration for Hastings and Netflix, notes he profiled Netflix's original product manager in *INSPIRED* (via [[2016-10-01-cagan-behind-every-great-product|Behind Every Great Product]]), and tempers the book's reputation: "my experiences with the people that work at Netflix over the years is that it's not as brutal as may come across in the book."
