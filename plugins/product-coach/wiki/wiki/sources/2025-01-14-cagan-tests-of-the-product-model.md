# Cagan — Tests of the Product Model

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Tests of the Product Model" · svpg.com, 2025-01-14 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/tests-of-the-product-model/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — root article for [[disruption-response|the new disruption-response diagnostic]].** Cagan's framing: the pandemic and, now, generative AI are two real-world, involuntary "tests" of which companies actually have the product-model skills — the ability to quickly evaluate new technology and explore new solutions to existing customer problems — versus which companies fall back on superficial features, marketing, and an outsourced agency ("spoiler alert: almost never works"). Checked against [[model-maturity]] before writing: **not a competing or duplicate rubric** — model-maturity assesses steady-state maturity; this article and its diagnostic assess observed *behavior under an actual disruptive event*, retrospectively or as it's happening.

## What it backs
- diagnostics: [[disruption-response]] (**canonical root** — the whole diagnostic; the pandemic + generative-AI as the two named tests; the historical-pattern-reversing observation that today's incumbents (Google, Meta, Apple, Amazon) are innovating alongside new AI-native startups, not simply being replaced by them, as was the more usual historical pattern; the "if you think X, think again" rebuttal list)
- case studies: [[google-product-model]] (supporting — Cagan's specific congratulatory callout to Google for its GenAI-era innovation, despite his earlier skepticism that it needed to do much more beyond AdWords to keep growing)

## Notes for the coach
- **The two tests, back to back.** The pandemic (2020) was a sudden, no-warning shock; generative AI is "more like a train heading down the tracks" — visible for two years already (as of writing) and still running for years more. Both are named as real-world tests of product-model skill, but the *no-excuse-for-surprise* character of the second sharpens the diagnostic: a slow-motion, years-visible disruption handled poorly is a worse signal than a sudden shock handled poorly.
- **The pass/fail behavioral tell.** Passing: quickly evaluating the new technology and exploring new solutions to existing customer problems. Failing: superficial features, marketing as the only tool, and rushing to hire an agency to "hopefully build something" — which Cagan flags almost never works.
- **The historical pattern, and its live exception.** Historically, when a major disruptive technology arrived, existing leaders usually could not adjust and a new generation of companies took over. Generative AI shows a partial break from that pattern: alongside new startups (OpenAI, Perplexity, Gong), several long-time leaders (Google, Meta, Apple, Amazon) are also shipping major innovative AI products — evidence that at least some incumbents have genuinely absorbed product-model skills rather than just scale and distribution.
- **The Google callout (verbatim gist).** Cagan gives Google a specific congratulatory footnote: he had long believed Google had more potential than it was showing, because AdWords alone let it keep growing without needing to innovate elsewhere — but both the opportunities and threats of generative AI "seem to be once again bringing out the best in Google's people."
- **The "if you think X, think again" rebuttal list** — five common excuses for delaying a move to the product model, each rebutted with a link to further SVPG material: waiting for CEO buy-in; "it's only for tech companies"; "it's only for startups"; "there's nothing an individual contributor can do"; and the general claim that disruptive innovations will eventually stop coming (they won't).
