# Cagan & Langer — The Product Model in Government

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan & Stacey Langer · "The Product Model in Government" · svpg.com, 2024-08-07 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-model-in-government/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon), co-authored** — second in the "probing the edges" series (after Traditional IT). Stacey Langer, profiled in *TRANSFORMED* as a strong product coach with government experience, co-authors. Addresses whether the model — built for profit-seeking companies — applies to mission-driven, non-profit-seeking public-sector organizations.

## Notes for the coach
- **The reframe that unlocks the whole article:** profit is *one* way of measuring value; government measures value by mission impact, and can still run a rigorous focus-on-outcomes model.
- **Government's three advantages:** mission-driven staff (genuine passion for the mission, when tapped with a compelling vision); public/press/watchdog focus on outcomes and waste avoidance; a captive audience (little competition — though marketing is still needed to reach and inform users, and vibe-coded workarounds are an emerging exception).
- **Government's four challenges:** viability risk (legal/policy/privacy/security/ethics, each often more time-consuming than defining the user experience); fraud risk (government's trusted-entity status attracts bad actors, both against services and against the education effort); project-driven procurement (a legacy of treating tech procurement like buying a bridge — root of the waterfall/stage-gate legacy); stakeholder complexity ("lots of people who can say no, few who can say yes").
- **Five named techniques for success:** centering on shared mission with stakeholders (not just users); finding a leadership sponsor deeply invested in the mission; high-fidelity prototypes as the way to win bureaucratic hearts and minds; small skilled cross-functional teams (most impactful apps are built by fewer than ten people); reframing project-based funding into weekly discovery + biweekly delivery iteration cadences; continuously evangelizing outcomes so funding/attention doesn't evaporate.
- **The three non-negotiable roles even under heavy outsourcing:** head of product, head of design, head of technology — "without these three key people, the chance of any kind of success is remote."
- **When to cite:** any coaching conversation with a government, non-profit, or other non-profit-seeking mission-driven organization asking whether the model applies to them at all.
