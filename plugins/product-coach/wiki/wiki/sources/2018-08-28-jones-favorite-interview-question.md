# Chris' Favorite Interview Question

> **Citation:** [[chris-jones|Chris Jones]] · *Chris' Favorite Interview Question* · SVPG, Aug 28, 2018 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/chris-favorite-interview-question/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — Jones's named four-attribute hiring instrument, and the wiki's root for *hiring a PM team as a portfolio* rather than a set of identical all-stars.** Written as a deliberate counterpart to Cagan's [My Favorite PM Interview Question](https://www.svpg.com/my-favorite-pm-interview-question/) *(uncarded)*, which probes a single candidate's mindset; Jones's question is aimed at the manager building a *team*.

Key material:
- **The setup, which is half the technique.** Late in the interview: the candidate is given four attributes, told they are expected to be strong in all of them, and asked to **stack-rank themselves strongest to weakest**. Jones notes the framing "should be disarming" — there is no correct answer, which is what makes an honest conversation possible.
- **The four attributes.**
  1. **Execution** — getting things done, doing the right thing unasked, tracking many simultaneous targets.
  2. **Creativity** — being the person in the room with the most or best ideas.
  3. **Strategy** — rising above the immediate work, placing it in market/vision context, and making that clear to others.
  4. **Growth** — multiplying effort through process, team management, and similar leverage.
- **What it reveals about the candidate.** How they handle a conversation about their own self-assessed weaknesses — Jones calls it "a less contrived and more effective version of the classically stupid 'tell me about your weaknesses.'" Red flags: unwillingness to engage, or a self-assessment "wildly at odds" with what the interview already showed.
- **What it reveals about *you*, which Jones says matters more.** It is "a check on your own biases and helps ensure that you don't end up hiring a bunch of clones (usually of yourself)." Managers should periodically stack-rank themselves — the ranking changes over time — and then hire into their own deficiencies. His note on why that is easier said than done: "It's a lot easier on the ego to hire into weaknesses when you also know what makes you uniquely strong."
- **Deployment, not just selection.** Jones maps attributes to assignments: creative-forward PMs to early, pre-product/market-fit areas; execution-heavy PMs to mature products; strategy-oriented PMs to vision and portfolio work; growth people to process and team management.
- **The table-stakes exclusion.** Intelligence, communication, leadership soft skills and passion for product are *mandatory for every candidate* and deliberately kept out of the portfolio. "The important thing is to understand which attributes are mandatory, and which ones to spread across the team as a whole."

## Notes for the coach
Two distinct uses. With an individual PM, the stack-rank is a low-threat way into a development conversation — it surfaces self-awareness without the defensiveness of a weaknesses question. With a *leader*, run it on the team: rank each PM, then look at the shape of the whole. Jones's claim is that a team can be strong across all four even though no individual is, and that most hiring failures come from a manager unconsciously cloning themselves. Note also his caution that the four attributes are his, not canon — "your list of attributes may be different than mine."
