## Notes for the coach
- **The correction most product people need, stated up front.** Cagan explicitly rejects the naive ask: many product leaders "would like to think this is as easy as saying 'please just back off and give the product teams space to do their work.'" It isn't — executives have "very real needs … in order to responsibly and effectively run the company," and teams need strategic context that comes *from* executives. So: **not less interaction, more** — "product teams need frequent, high-quality engagement." What changes is the *nature*, not the volume. This is the line to quote when a team frames empowerment as being left alone.
- **Seven named sections**, each a reciprocal bargain — worth reproducing as structure (not text) on [[the-pledges]]:
  - **Decisions** — push decisions to whoever is closest to the technology and the customers; executives share business strategy, financial parameters, regulatory developments, industry trends, strategic partnerships; teams share openly the data and reasoning behind their decisions.
  - **Outcomes** — teams expect accountability to outcomes rather than output, but "this only works if the product teams are given a problem to solve (rather than a particular potential solution to build)" and as many **degrees of freedom** as possible.
  - **Disagreements** — "In the old models, contrarian or counter-intuitive ideas are not explored and innovation rarely happens." Teams pledge to run responsible discovery tests to collect evidence; executives pledge to allow and ideally encourage exploring alternatives.
  - **Promises** — high-integrity commitments only; only the delivering team may promise; and critically, HICs must be **the exception and not the rule**, because making and keeping one is expensive.
  - **Focus** — quotes Jobs ("Focus is saying no to the hundreds of other good ideas"); asks executives to hold focus "at least on a quarterly basis."
  - **Surprises** — the anti-waste clause; see below.
  - **Trust** — mutual and explicitly two-directional: teams trust that executives are leading the company somewhere good.
- **The surprises clause is the most operationally useful part.** Two symmetric wastes are named: (a) a team builds a production-quality capability and only then learns it's unacceptable; (b) an executive reviews a prototype during discovery, raises nothing, then declares a serious problem after it ships. Both trigger a **post-mortem** on how that waste can be avoided. Coaches can use this to make executive prototype reviews binding-ish without pretending they're sign-offs.
- **Read the three together.** Customers (2022) → Stakeholders (Jun 2023) → Executives (Jun 2023) is a deliberate progression outward through the constituencies an empowered team must renegotiate with. Individually each is modest; as a set they're the closest thing SVPG has published to a **social contract for empowerment**.
