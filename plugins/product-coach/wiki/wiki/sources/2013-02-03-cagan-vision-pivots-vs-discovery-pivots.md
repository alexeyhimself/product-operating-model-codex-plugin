# Vision Pivots vs. Discovery Pivots

> **Citation:** [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Vision Pivots vs. Discovery Pivots* · SVPG, Feb 3, 2013 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/vision-pivots-vs-discovery-pivots/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — resolves the long-open `[[vision-pivot]]` reference tracked by the ingest-plan Batch 8 non-target list.** Cagan's disambiguation of the two very different kinds of "pivot" — one about the vision, one about the solution being discovered — is the article that grounds the distinction across [[product-vision]] and [[product-discovery]].

Key material:
- The "pivot" term is overused; often just a synonym for "change" (tech stack, dev process, team reassignment).
- The serious problem: teams giving up on their **product vision** too early.
- **Vision pivots** — the product vision is what you're building over 2–5 years. Don't give up easily. *"Be stubborn on vision, but flexible on details"* (Bezos). A vision pivot is warranted only when the market itself is missing — i.e. the customers don't care enough about the problem you're solving; techniques for validating the market exist and are quick.
- **Discovery pivots** — during product discovery, teams try many solution ideas. Most won't work. Don't be wedded to particular solutions/features/approaches — a discovery pivot (changing the solution while keeping the vision) is normal, expected, and cheap.
- The most common mistake: teams call a *discovery pivot* a *vision pivot* and give up on the vision when they should have iterated on the solution.

## Notes for the coach
When a PM or founder says "we're pivoting," ask *which kind* using this article's frame. Most claimed "pivots" are (i) a name change with no real meaning, or (ii) a discovery pivot mis-labelled as a vision pivot (i.e., giving up on the vision when they should have iterated on the solution). Cite the Bezos "stubborn on vision, flexible on details" mantra as the operational shorthand.
