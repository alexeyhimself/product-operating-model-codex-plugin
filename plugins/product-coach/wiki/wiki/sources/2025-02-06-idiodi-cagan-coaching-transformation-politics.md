# Product Therapy Ep 14 — Coaching Transformation Politics

> **Citation:** [[christian-idiodi|Christian Idiodi]] and [[marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] · *Product Therapy* podcast, Ep 14 · SVPG, February 6, 2025 — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M2i0iBc1LHA). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the podcast-form companion to [[2024-11-21-cagan-transformation-politics]] (Cagan's Nov 2024 article of the same name).** New material the podcast adds beyond the article: (a) **the "results window"** — the moment leadership says "we're going to start a transformation" the clock starts ticking; if real results don't land inside that window, *every* org Cagan has seen loses interest, motivation, or gets distracted. Corollary: **bigger changes for a much smaller piece of the org beats small changes across the whole org.** (b) **The Jonathan Moore / Trainline pilot-team story** in Cagan's own voice — Moore picked his best PM, designer and engineers, put them on a new native mobile app (the "prize at the end of the road"), and told the team explicitly: *"we need to show the rest of our own organization what good looks like."* (c) **The multi-BU rule**: transform one business unit at a time; substitute the BU-GM for the CEO in every political dynamic; an acquired BU is "all bets off" and non-transitive from a nailed transformation elsewhere. (d) **The CEO's sweeping-change-now scenario:** if the CEO refuses a pilot, invoke the [[technology-adoption-life-cycle|technology-adoption life cycle]] — laggards are always a small %, but forcing the *middle* to move faster than ready loses would-be contributors ("you hurt all at once, discover all at once"). (e) **The #1 transformation-failure cause** (Cagan restates it crisply): *"the product leaders think all they could do is retitle their product owners to product managers"* — the stakeholders keep going to the two people who actually know the industry and deliver, not to the retitled PMs. (f) **The politics of picking problems**: trust the stakeholder that the problem is real — they've been dealing with it for 10 years — and discover the *solution*, not the problem; making the team re-verify problems is *"a way to really undermine trust right at the beginning."* Earn a seat at the problem-picking table by first delivering outcomes on the stakeholder's problems. (g) **The champion / evangelist duty** — someone (CEO or CPO) puts their reputation on the line, keeps beating the drum, and makes sure every success is broadcast; that person is not usually the pilot team's manager but is deeply involved in picking it, skip-leveling into it, and coaching the managers of the pilot team members. (h) **"Win hearts and minds before you seek to lead"** (Idiodi's closing frame) + Idiodi's *pack-leader* metaphor (*"nobody joins a pack and automatically becomes the pack leader"*).

## Notes for the coach
Attribute the *results-window* frame and the *"bigger changes for a smaller piece"* rule to Cagan (Ep 14 is the podcast-form debut). Attribute the *pack-leader* metaphor and the *"win hearts and minds before you seek to lead"* closing to Idiodi. When a leader argues for a sweeping org-wide change now, use Cagan's technology-adoption-curve response verbatim: laggards are inevitable and small, but the middle needs time — force them and they check out or leave; give them time and they become the mass of the transformation. When a product team wants to "rediscover problems," use Cagan's Ep 14 answer verbatim: *"trust your stakeholder on the problem — they've been dealing with it for 10 years — and by the way, in that remote chance they're wrong, you'll find that out the first day you test your solution prototype."* When a product leader is stuck on "how do we move from subservient to collaborative," name the trade explicitly (stakeholders trade solutions for outcomes; the team trades feature-execution for outcome-commitment) and remind them that trust is *earned* — pack-leader position is not conferred, it's assumed.
