# Cagan & Kerievsky — The Product Model in Outsourcing

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan & Josh Kerievsky · "The Product Model in Outsourcing" · svpg.com, 2024-08-13 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-model-in-outsourcing/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon), co-authored** — third and final article in the "probing the edges" series. Kerievsky (author of *Joy of Agility*, founder of Industrial Logic) co-authors. Notable for Cagan explicitly addressing the tension with his own long-standing position ("stop outsourcing your core competencies," "engineers are the single most important thing") — this article is about outsourcing *agencies themselves* adopting the product model to better serve *their own* clients, not an endorsement of outsourcing a company's core engineering.

## Notes for the coach
- **The core reframe:** move the client relationship from *"here's a spec, build it for a fixed price"* to *"discover and deliver an effective solution, time-and-materials, assessed and renewed period over period."* The client is, in effect, renting a product team.
- **Four core challenges named:** trust and client collaboration (client believes they already know the solution); the product manager role (the client thinks *they* are the PM, and the agency's "engagement manager" is really a project-manager role, not an empowered PM); the product design role (four common client scenarios, from "has their own designers" to "thinks design is a logo and color scheme"); durable product teams (client engagements often can't guarantee the same team returns, losing accumulated context).
- **Techniques for success, overlapping heavily with the Government article** (Cagan's own framing: "these are just two sides of the same coin"): discovery sprints and short initial engagements to build trust; the persuasive power of high-fidelity prototypes (50–100 versions over a typical 6–12 month engagement); reframing project-based client funding into weekly/biweekly discovery-and-delivery iteration; continuously evangelizing outcomes to the client.
- **A named real dispute, cited as a cautionary tale:** the Hertz/Accenture lawsuit, as an example of what goes wrong when the "detailed spec, fixed price" model fails a client publicly.
- **When to cite:** any coaching conversation with (or about) an agency or systems integrator that wants to apply the product model to its client-delivery work, as distinct from a company deciding whether to outsource its own core engineering (that remains a "don't" — see [[missionaries-vs-mercenaries]] and [[it-to-product-organization]]).
