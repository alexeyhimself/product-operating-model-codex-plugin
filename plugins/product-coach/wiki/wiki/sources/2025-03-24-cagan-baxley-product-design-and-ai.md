# Cagan & Baxley — Product, Design and AI

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan and Bob Baxley · "Product, Design and AI" · svpg.com, 2025-03-24 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/product-design-and-ai/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary — Cagan's most substantial co-authored article, with Bob Baxley (design leader, ex-Apple).** Written as a direct response to confusion following [[2025-02-25-cagan-a-vision-for-product-teams|A Vision for Product Teams]] — "too many people in the product and design communities simply don't understand the necessary role of one or both of product managers or product designers." Its purpose is to clarify, in the GenAI era specifically, why both roles become *more* essential, not less, and what each contributes. Only relevant to **empowered** product teams; on feature teams the PM role described here "is largely covered by your stakeholders."

## Notes for the coach

### The team, restated
An empowered product team = PM + designer + one or more engineers. PM and designer (plus the tech lead) do discovery; the full engineering team does delivery. Platform products (API-only interfaces) typically have no dedicated designer.

### Product sense (PM side) — five knowledge areas, verbatim structure
Customer knowledge (users *and* buyers, discovery/evaluation/purchase/value-over-time), data knowledge (usage data + business-impact data), market and industry knowledge (competitive landscape + emerging trends), business knowledge (sales/marketing/finance/legal/compliance/manufacturing constraints — "not expected to be an expert... expected to invest the time... to be a trustworthy partner"), and product knowledge (operational fluency in your own product). This is the same shape as [[product-manager]]'s existing table; this article is useful confirmation the framework persists unchanged into the AI-era framing, now under the explicit name "product sense."

### Design sense (designer side) — the five design disciplines
1. **Service design** — orchestrating the touchpoints and behind-the-scenes processes connecting an org and its users; pushes back on Conway's Law so the user feels they're dealing with one entity, not "shipping the org chart."
2. **Information architecture** — structuring and labeling features/content around the user's existing mental models; drives comprehension and findability.
3. **Interaction design** — choreographing the conversation between user and system; Fitt's Law, affordances, feedback loops, error handling; most visible in multi-step flows like sign-up or checkout.
4. **Visual design** — how the product looks; Gestalt principles, grids, typography; drives trust and perceived care.
5. **Industrial design** — physical products only; materials, manufacturing, repairability, transportability, environmental impact.

Framed explicitly as the craft foundation beneath the designer's contribution — parallel in structure to product sense, and named as a pair for the first time.

### Discovery in the AI era
- **Problem discovery** is mostly done by product leaders (via strategy, research, data), freeing the team to focus on **solution discovery** — "there's little chance of coming up with a good solution if the product team does not understand the problem," but most discovery time is properly solution work.
- **Solution discovery** runs through prototypes as the primary tool — GenAI tools now let the trio create several prototype forms, especially [[2011-02-20-cagan-product-discovery-with-live-data-prototypes|live-data prototypes]], in minutes or hours. Cagan calls this "a golden era of product discovery." Most ideas still don't survive contact with a fleshed-out prototype — the Steve Jobs "disease of thinking a great idea is 90% of the work" reference.
