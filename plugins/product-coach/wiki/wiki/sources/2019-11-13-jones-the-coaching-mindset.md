# The Coaching Mindset

> **Citation:** [[chris-jones|Chris Jones]] · *The Coaching Mindset* · SVPG, Nov 13, 2019 — [svpg.com](https://www.svpg.com/the-coaching-mindset/). Slim provenance card; raw file stays local.

## Role
**Primary — the article-form root for the [[coaching-mindset]] concept page's seven-principle mindset.** Currently [[coaching-mindset]] cites EMPOWERED Ch 7 as the primary; this Jones article predates the book by roughly one year (Nov 2019) and is the earliest wiki-attested SVPG source for the seven-principle framing.

Key material — the seven principles of the coaching mindset (paraphrased headers):
1. **Developing people is the single most important part of your job** — most time and energy on coaching your team; measure your own performance on team members' success.
2. **You get the best results from empowering people, not micro-managing them** — better management, not less; step back to create space, step in to remove impediments.
3. **Your own insecurities can stifle your team's development** — insecure managers control visibility, can even undermine their own team.
4. Additional principles (4–7) continue the mindset framing — coaching intent as foundation for the mechanics.

## Notes for the coach
When coaching a new product manager on how to coach their own team, cite this as *the* article to read before the coaching mechanics ([[2019-04-08-cagan-coaching-tools-the-assessment|assessment]], [[2019-04-22-cagan-coaching-tools-the-plan|plan]], [[2019-02-08-cagan-coaching-tools-the-one-on-one|1:1s]], [[2019-03-11-cagan-coaching-tools-the-narrative|narrative]]) — mindset first, mechanics second. Jones names the failure mode explicitly: coaching mechanics applied without the mindset actively undermine their intent (a 1:1 that's just task allocation is worse than no 1:1).
