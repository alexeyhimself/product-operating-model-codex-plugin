# Cagan — Is Product Art or Science?

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "Is Product Art or Science?" · svpg.com, 2024-09-26 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/is-product-art-or-science/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Primary (SVPG canon) — a personal-essay reflection, prompted by Rick Rubin's *The Creative Act: A Way of Being*, on product work as a blend of art and science.** Lower canonical weight than the wiki's core diagnostic/rubric material — this is Cagan thinking out loud about craft and creativity — but it supplies a genuinely new framing for [[product-sense|product sense]]: judgment as a creative, not purely analytical, skill.

## What it backs
- concepts: [[product-sense]] (supporting — product creators (PMs, designers, engineers) share more with artists than the science-only framing suggests: both are fundamentally *creators*, both use a "discovery" phase of experimentation before "craft" hardens the work into final form; the difference is that artists create primarily for self-expression, while product creators create to deliver value to a business)

## Notes for the coach
- **Cagan's own long-held (and self-critiqued) view.** He always assumed product work was mostly science — problems, hypotheses, prototypes, testing for value/usability/viability/feasibility, iteration — with art playing a bigger role only in consumer products/devices. Reading Rubin's book changed how much weight he gives the art side: he estimates ~70–80% of the book is directly relevant to product creators.
- **The "creators" framing.** Rubin treats artists fundamentally as *creators*; product managers, designers, and engineers on empowered teams are creators too, just creating to deliver value rather than for self-expression. Both use the words "problems to solve," "discovery" (a series of experiments exploring ideas), and "craft" (fleshing the work into final form) — a striking terminological overlap Cagan did not expect.
- **An explicit guardrail.** Cagan is careful to say this is *not* license for product people to see themselves as misunderstood fine artists expressing a unique self through their products — the target audience is product creators solving problems for customers in ways the business needs, not solo visionaries.
- **Apple as the edge case.** The clearest example of a company that works hard to express a founder's values through its products — closer to the artist's self-expression end of the spectrum than most product companies.
