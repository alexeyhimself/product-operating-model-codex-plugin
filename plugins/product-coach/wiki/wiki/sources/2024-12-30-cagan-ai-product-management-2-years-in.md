# Cagan — AI Product Management 2 Years In

> **Citation:** Marty Cagan · "AI Product Management 2 Years In" · svpg.com, 2024-12-30 — [read the original](https://www.svpg.com/ai-product-management-2-years-in/). Slim provenance card; the article is **not reproduced** here.

## Role
**Supporting — a Dec-2024 checkpoint on [[2024-04-16-cagan-ai-product-management|AI Product Management]] (the root article), not new canon.** Cagan explicitly separates two different conversations: incorporating AI *into* the products a team builds, versus how AI changes *how* a team builds products — this article is about the latter, which is exactly the wiki's [[ai-and-product-teams]] scope. Useful chiefly as a **dated marker**: as of end-2024, Cagan considered the questions below still open, and flags himself as "too optimistic" a year earlier about how fast things would move (referencing his skipped *Product Predictions 2024* piece).

## Notes for the coach
- **The self-correction.** A year after his 2023 predictions piece, Cagan admits: "overall I was too optimistic about how soon many of these things would happen... not all that much has really changed. At least as compared to what I was hoping for."
- **The root article holds up.** "The past year has shown this article [[2024-04-16-cagan-ai-product-management|AI Product Management]] to be even more important than I had realized at the time" — the PM role becomes more essential *and* more difficult with generative AI, not less.
- **The three-types-of-PM reminder** (delivery-team product ownership / feature-team product management / empowered-team product management) — restates, doesn't refine, what the wiki already holds on [[feature-teams-vs-product-teams]]. Cagan notes most public AI-and-PM discourse is actually about the first two types, which isn't his focus.
- **Why AI's impact on PM is harder to discuss than on engineers/designers.** Code and designs are immediately tangible outputs; "creating valuable and viable solutions" is not. Even where tools speed up code/design creation, that speed doesn't necessarily translate into better outcomes (cites Mike Fisher's Substack, *The AI Trap* — external, non-SVPG; hyperlink only, no wiki page).
- **The six open questions (verbatim, still unresolved as of Dec 2024):**
  1. How does generative AI change the *nature* of product discovery, and the PM's role in it?
  2. What specific *skills* become more or less important when PM work is assisted by generative AI?
  3. What will generative AI *complement*, and what will it *replace*?
  4. How does generative AI impact *creativity*?
  5. Will generative AI produce more *optimization* or more *innovation*?
  6. Will these changes lead to better *outcomes* for product teams?
- **The excitement/worry duality.** Cagan is "especially excited" by strong [[product-sense|product sense]] combined with generative AI tools, and "also worried about the prospect of providing those same tools to people that do not have the necessary product foundation" — the same asymmetry the wiki already carries via the model-as-coach "good enough to reach competence" framing, applied here to raw tool access rather than coaching.
- Closes without resolving the six questions — "two years in, we're all still figuring things out," but with "a better sense of the right questions to ask."
