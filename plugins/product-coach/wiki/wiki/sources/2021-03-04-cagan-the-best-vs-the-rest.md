## Notes for the coach
- **The observation the article is built on — genuinely useful in coaching.** Over two weeks Cagan spoke with people at pure feature-team companies who told him empowered product teams "sound like some mythical and utopian world, which can't possibly exist in reality" — *and* with people at strong product companies who couldn't understand why he'd bother writing about feature-team companies they'd "never seen" and thought he was exaggerating when told those are the majority. **Neither side believes the other exists.** Cagan's conclusion: "With so little visibility and understanding of what it's like on the other side, it's really no wonder that there's so much noise and confusion about the role of product, and also why most 'digital transformations' go nowhere fast."
- **Origin story worth keeping on [[marty-cagan]].** He traces the gap to his own first customer visits as an engineer at HP Labs building tools for other software engineers — "shocked at the differences between how our customers … were working," without yet having the vocabulary or a taxonomy for it. He credits [[2019-08-29-cagan-product-vs-feature-teams|Product Teams vs. Feature Teams]] and *EMPOWERED* with making the difference finally land.
- **The hiring passage — the most practically useful section.** A recurring question: "I've got an offer from [a well-known strong product company] but they want me to come on as an individual contributor, but I have been a product leader for many years." Cagan's explanation: sometimes the person conflated PM with product leader, but often they really were a director or VP — and the company still can't tell **the nature** of that experience. "One of the very worst mistakes they can make is to hire a product leader that does not know what good looks like, and will end up spreading bad practices to the people they are responsible for coaching and developing." He notes every company he knows has made this mistake, himself included, and it is "very costly … especially when it's not quickly identified and corrected."
- **The advice he gives.** Take the IC role: if you're good you'll be promoted quickly; if not, you'll learn what good looks like. He adds an organizational reason — "it's also important that the broader organization see that the people that are promoted are widely accepted as very strong at their role." He acknowledges the pay cut and that not everyone will take the bet.
