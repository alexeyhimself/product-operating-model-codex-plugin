## Notes for the coach
- **The trilogy's shared premise, stated here first.** Moving from sales-driven feature teams to empowered product teams changes the *customer's* experience too, not only the team's internal work — so the change has to be explained outward, not just adopted inward. Cagan's framing: customers are used to dictating features to a salesperson and expecting them on a roadmap with a date, and most are open to a change in dynamics **only if they believe they're more likely to get their needs met, not less**. That conditional is the whole negotiating position.
- **Four sections.** *Promises* — no date or deliverable committed until the team truly understands what's required. *Product discovery* — solutions customers love that work for the business. *Product delivery* — testing, regressions, and the cost of adoption. *Happy referenceable customers* — the stated highest-order goal.
- **The four promise conditions (sharpest material on the page).** (1) The product team must engage **directly** with users and customers — proxies on either side, however well-meaning, are "not sufficient," and direct access is "a prerequisite to any commitments." (2) **Only the team that will deliver may make the promise** — "not the company's executives, not sales, not marketing, not customer success, not delivery managers." (3) No promise before enough discovery to know what's required, normally "one or more prototypes to address the specific risks" — these are [[high-integrity-commitments]]. (4) The team may have to remind the customer that a **commercial product company** must find a solution general enough to work for other customers too.
- **The commercial-vs-custom boundary.** Cagan's explicit escape hatch: if the needed solution would only work in a single customer's environment, and the customer understands they'd forgo ongoing innovation and service, the team should direct them to a **custom-solution provider**. Useful alongside [[commercial-vs-internal-products]] when a coach is asked "do we just build it for them?"
- **Delivery obligations are unusually concrete for an SVPG article** — worth citing on [[product-delivery]]: test the new capability works; test it introduces no **regressions**; be "sensitive to the cost of incorporating our new capabilities into your daily use" (re-certification, re-learning, re-training are real customer costs); and when mistakes happen, correct quickly and analyze how to avoid repeating.
- **Voice.** The article is written *as* the pledge — first-person plural, addressed to the customer. That makes it directly usable as a template an org can adapt, which is why it belongs in `frameworks/` rather than `concepts/`.
